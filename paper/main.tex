\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Programmable packet scheduling.
Emphasize that policies are often hierarchical, and that clients demand line rate.

\paragraph{The reconfiguration problem.}
Consider a small-office gateway running \texttt{Strict(hi: zoom, lo: gmail)}, strictly prioritizing real-time \texttt{zoom} traffic over \texttt{gmail} traffic.
The operator wants to add a new \texttt{spotify} flow, and is considering two candidate policies:
\texttt{Strict(hi: zoom, mid: spotify, lo: gmail)}, extending the strict-priority list with \texttt{spotify}; or
\texttt{Strict(hi: zoom, lo: RoundRobin(gmail, spotify))}, keeping \texttt{zoom} at the higher tier but having \texttt{gmail} and \texttt{spotify} share the lower tier via round-robin.
In either case, the state of the art~\cite{vPIFO} would stop the world, drop or recirculate buffered packets, recompile, and reinstall.
The costs are dropped or recirculated (and therefore delayed) packets, downtime, and the rebuilding of nodes that did not need to be rebuilt.

The alternative is to reprogram a scheduler without stopping the world.
A transition from a running policy \texttt{p1} to a requested one takes one of two shapes (\cref{fig:two-timelines}): sometimes the requested policy can be installed immediately, as a single atomic switch (case~(1)); sometimes it cannot, and the scheduler must first pass through a transitionary policy (case~(2)).
Our two candidates illustrate the two cases.

Moving to \texttt{Strict(hi: zoom, mid: spotify, lo: gmail)} is doable in the case 1 style.
Before time \(t_1\), \texttt{Strict(hi: zoom, lo: gmail)} is running; at time \(t_1\) the request to transition is received, and we immediately satisfy it.
The transition at \(t_1\) is \emph{atomic}, meaning that any interaction that the network had with the scheduler before \(t_1\) happened entirely in the old regime, and whatever interaction the network had with the scheduler after \(t_1\) happened entirely in the new regime.
It is also \emph{immediate}: no period of preparation precedes the atomic jump into \texttt{Strict(hi: zoom, mid: spotify, lo: gmail)}. The two concepts are distinct.

Moving to \texttt{Strict(hi: zoom, lo: RoundRobin(gmail, spotify))} is impossible using the case 1 style, and we need case 2.
As before, \texttt{Strict(hi: zoom, lo: gmail)} runs before \(t_1\) and the operator's request arrives at \(t_1\), but we cannot move to \texttt{Strict(hi: zoom, lo: RoundRobin(gmail, spotify))} immediately since there are still \texttt{gmail} packets buffered in the scheduler.
Switching immediately would mean re-sorting those already-queued \texttt{gmail} packets into the new round-robin leaf, but their scheduling order was fixed the moment they were enqueued; we cannot rewrite that past without dropping or reordering them.
We atomically step instead into a \emph{transitionary period} that is \emph{not} the user's desired policy.
After \(t_1\) but before \(t_2\), the scheduler still accepts and emits packets under this transitionary regime.
At time \(t_2\), defined as the instant that a well-defined guard becomes true, we atomically step into \texttt{Strict(hi: zoom, lo: RoundRobin(gmail, spotify))}.

Although the user never specified the semantics of this transitionary period (\(t_1\) to \(t_2\)), the period is itself a \emph{de facto} packet scheduling regime: the transition is unavoidable, it persists for nonzero time, and during it the scheduler must continue to serve the network's pushes and pops.
So \emph{scheduling decisions} are being made during that window, whether we acknowledge it or not.
This is a central thesis of this paper: once we refuse to stop the world, the interval between two policies is itself a scheduling policy, one worth studying and getting right.
We call it the \emph{transitionary policy}, and treat it as a first-class policy of its own.
Some transitions are better than others from a network operator's point of view, and the state of the art has settled on a trivial stop-the-world transitionary policy.
Our contributions are to be clear about what the transitionary policy is and to improve on the state of the art.

Concretely, we take on two obligations.
\emph{Obligation 1: give a semantics to the transitionary policy.}
The transitionary period is shaped by hardware-level manipulations on the way to realizing \texttt{p2}, and not by a clean human-readable semantics, and so one might naturally worry that it will be hard to pin down the semantics of this transitionary policy.
We develop a grammar of atomic edits (\cref{sec:grammar}) and a sequencing layer (\cref{sec:sequences}) that carries us from the user-supplied policy \texttt{p1}, through one or more transitionary policies, and finally to the user-supplied policy \texttt{p2}.
The grammar is carefully designed so that \emph{any transitionary policy it produces is just ordinary scheduling}: it has a human-readable semantics, exactly as the user-written policies \texttt{p1} and \texttt{p2} do.
\emph{Obligation 2: improve upon the state of the art.}
We will give the semantics of the stop-the-world transitionary policy and use it as a baseline.
Several practical goals guide us as we look for better ways to transition from a given \texttt{p1} to a given \texttt{p2}: can we minimize the length of the transition period, and can we avoid dropped/delayed packets?

We will show that it is always possible to make a transition from any \texttt{p1} to any \texttt{p2}, but it is occasionally possible to make a very efficient transition.

\section{Background \& Motivation}\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Hierarchical scheduling on hardware}\label{sec:hierarchical}

A \emph{PIFO} (push-in, first-out queue) is a priority queue that is additionally defined to break rank-ties in FIFO order.
Where a single PIFO expresses just one scheduling decision, a PIFO tree composes a decision at every level (strict priority, weighted fair queueing, round-robin, and so on), capturing the hierarchical policies that operators actually deploy.

A PIFO tree lowers to hardware by mapping each tree depth onto a dedicated processing element (PE), with siblings and cousins sharing the PE at their depth.
Pinning a depth level to a single PE has well-studied benefits for computation and storage; Sivaraman (SIGCOMM '16), Mohan et al.\ (OOPSLA '23), Shrivastav (SIGCOMM '19), and vPIFO (Zhang et al., SIGCOMM '24) all build on this layout.
The PE budget is fixed by the silicon, so the running tree's shape is constrained by what the deployed PEs can host.

\subsection{The runtime model: PIFO trees and controls}\label{sec:runtime-model}

The runtime object we are ultimately editing is not a bare PIFO tree but a PIFO tree wrapped with per-node scheduling logic; we call the wrapped object a \emph{control}, after Mohan et al.\ (OOPSLA '23).
This subsection lays out both layers, first the tree (\cref{sec:pifo-trees}) then the wrapping (\cref{sec:controls}).

\subsubsection{PIFO trees}\label{sec:pifo-trees}

A \emph{PIFO tree} is a hierarchy of PIFOs: leaves hold packets, while internal nodes hold child indices that route each \texttt{pop} down to a chosen leaf.

\paragraph{Shape vs.\ contents}

A PIFO tree separates its structural shape (the underlying finite tree of nodes) from the data carried at each node.
A leaf \texttt{Leaf(p)} holds a packet-carrying PIFO \texttt{p}.
An internal node \texttt{Internal(ts, p)} carries a list \texttt{ts} of child PIFO trees and a PIFO \texttt{p} whose entries are indices into \texttt{ts}.

\paragraph{The two observable operations}

A tree exposes two observable operations, \texttt{push} and \texttt{pop}.

To \texttt{push} a packet into a leaf, we offer it in parallel to every node on its root-to-leaf path.
Each such node mints a rank for the packet and enqueues one entry at that rank.
At an internal node, the entry enqueued is an \emph{index} naming the child that leads to the target leaf, and at the leaf the entry enqueued is the \emph{packet itself}.
The rank that a node assigns to the entry comes from its own scheduling logic, which we will soon make precise as a \emph{scheduling transaction}, denoted \ensuremath{\mathit{z}}. 
For now it is enough that every on-path node contributes one ranked entry, and that these ranks are fixed at \texttt{push} time and never revised.

A \texttt{pop} extracts the best-ranked packet in the whole tree, where ``best'' is resolved top-down: pop releases the best-ranked index at the root, follows that index to identify one of the root's children, and recurses on that child.
Eventually it reaches a leaf, at which point it releases the best-ranked packet at the leaf.
Because an internal entry is only an index, the index enqueued on account of one packet may end up releasing a different, better-ranked packet that later joined the same leaf; this deferral is what lets a PIFO tree reorder buffered traffic in ways a single PIFO cannot.

These are the \emph{only} observable interactions with a scheduler, which is why we frame an \emph{atomic} transition in terms of \texttt{push}/\texttt{pop} observability.

\paragraph{Well-formedness}

A PIFO tree \texttt{t} is well-formed (written \(\vdash \mathtt{t}\)) when, at every internal node with index-PIFO \texttt{p} and children \texttt{ts}, the number of occurrences of \texttt{i} in \texttt{p} equals the number of packets held under \texttt{ts[i]}, for every legal \texttt{i}.
This is the invariant that keeps \texttt{pop} from ever getting stuck.

\subsubsection{Controls}\label{sec:controls}

A PIFO tree \emph{control} \texttt{C} wraps a PIFO tree with per-node scheduling logic: it is the concrete state a programmable switch executes at line rate, realizable on an FPGA or SmartNIC (\cref{sec:hardware} lowers a control to exactly such a target).
It is a tree of triples \((\mathit{state}, \mathit{pifo}, \mathit{z})\), one per node of the topology.

\begin{itemize}
\item \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} is a pair \((\mathit{node\_state}, \mathit{slot\_states})\): the scheduling discipline's per-node bookkeeping and a list of per-arm entries, one per child.
When the node runs a discipline without per-arm bookkeeping (e.g., \texttt{RR}), \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_states}} is empty.
\item \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} is a PIFO: at an internal node, the PIFO carries indices into the child list; at a leaf, the PIFO carries the packets themselves.
\item \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} is the node's \emph{scheduling transaction}.
Given the node's \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} and an incoming packet, \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} produces the node's \emph{path segment} for that packet, together with the node's updated \ensuremath{\mathit{state}}.
The shape of the path segment differs between internal nodes and leaves.
At an internal node, \(\mathit{z} : \mathit{state} \times \mathit{Pkt} \rightharpoonup (\mathit{idx} \times \mathit{rank}) \times \mathit{state}\), giving the child index that leads toward the packet's leaf and the rank with which to enqueue that index here.
At a leaf, \(\mathit{z} : \mathit{state} \times \mathit{Pkt} \rightharpoonup \mathit{rank} \times \mathit{state}\), giving the rank for the packet's own entry into the leaf's packet-PIFO.
\ensuremath{\mathit{z}} is partial.
When it is undefined for a packet, nothing is enqueued at this node and \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} is unchanged.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Inside \ensuremath{\mathit{z}}: classifier and ranker}

We write \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} as a single partial function, but it actually has two parts.
This is where we part with earlier presentations.
Mohan et al.\ keep one total transaction for the whole tree, returning an entire root-to-leaf path of indices and ranks.
Ours is per-node and partial, and it does not pick the index.
A valid \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}'s leaves partition a set of flows (\cref{sec:policy-dsl}), so a packet's leaf is settled before \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} is consulted.
The payload is fixed by the tree, and \ensuremath{\mathit{z}}'s job is only to rank it.

\begin{itemize}
\item The \emph{classifier} answers whether this node accepts a given packet and where it sends it.
It has a \emph{service map}, written \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves}\), which is a partial map from flows to payloads. 
At an internal node, \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves}(f)\) is a unique child index, and following it brings a packet of flow \(f\) one step closer to its leaf.
At a leaf the payload is the packet itself, so the map is the trivial one on the single flow that leaf carries.
Its \emph{admitted set}, written \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{admit} \subseteq \mathrm{dom}(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves})\), is the subset of the flows that the node currently accepts.
\item The \emph{ranker}, written \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{rank}\), is the scheduling logic that the node's discipline contributes: from the node's \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} and the chosen payload it mints the rank and the updated \ensuremath{\mathit{state}}.
A node's ranker names the discipline that the node runs.
\end{itemize}

So \(\mathit{z}(\mathit{state}, p)\) is defined exactly when \(\mathit{flow}(p) \in \mathit{z}.\mathit{admit}\). 
It then finds \(p\)'s payload in \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves}\), and pairs that payload with the rank that \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{rank}\) computes.

Keeping \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{admit}\) separate from \(\mathrm{dom}(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves})\) proves useful: silencing a flow (\cref{sec:quiesce}) drops it from \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{admit}\) while leaving \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves}\) in place, so packets already buffered under the node still have a way out. 
Only the structural deletion of a node gets rid of the mapping itself.

\paragraph{Path-consistency of admission}

One condition ties the node-local admitted sets together.
A packet's flow must be admitted at either \emph{every} node on that flow's root-to-leaf path, or at none of them.
Otherwise a \texttt{push} would enqueue an index at some ancestor with no matching packet below it, or buffer a packet under a leaf with no index above it, and the well-formedness condition below would fail.
This is a global property, not a concern of any node-local \ensuremath{\mathit{z}}.

We address node-local components of a control by a \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}, a possibly empty sequence of child indices read from the root.
The local triple at the node reached by following \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} from control \texttt{C}'s root is written \texttt{C@}\(\mathit{path}\), with fields \texttt{C@}\(\mathit{path}.\mathit{state}\), \texttt{C@}\(\mathit{path}.\mathit{pifo}\), and \texttt{C@}\(\mathit{path}.\mathit{z}\).
We also write \texttt{C@}\(\mathit{path}.\mathit{node\_state}\) and \texttt{C@}\(\mathit{path}.\mathit{slot\_states}\) for the two components of \texttt{C@}\(\mathit{path}.\mathit{state}\).

\paragraph{Well-formedness of controls}

A control \texttt{C} is \emph{well-formed} (written \(\vdash \mathtt{C}\)) when, at every internal node of \texttt{C}, the \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} has, for each legal child index \texttt{i}, exactly as many occurrences of \texttt{i} as there are packets stored in the leaf pifos of the subtree under the \texttt{i}-th child.
This is the tree-level condition of \cref{sec:pifo-trees} lifted to controls.
Stating it directly on controls allows \texttt{push} and \texttt{pop} to preserve it node-locally, without assembling a global PIFO tree to check.

\subsection{vPIFO, and the problem it leaves open}\label{sec:vpifo}

vPIFO (Zhang et al., SIGCOMM 2024) is the closest related work, and it explicitly leaves our problem open.
It virtualizes a single physical PIFO into many logical ``PIFO instances,'' with a Scheduling Description Language (SDL) and compiler, so their PIFO Visor can \emph{flexibly establish} hierarchical PIFO trees of arbitrary shape on fixed hardware; its contribution is the reconfigurable substrate.
As published, however, vPIFO has no notion of a difference between old and new policies, no formal semantics for what a policy change means, and no account of in-flight packets during a change.
Their SDL IR is for \emph{rank computation} compiled to P4 or CPU, quite different from our structural/topological grammars (our \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} and \(\delta\), \cref{sec:policy-dsl} and \cref{sec:delta-grammar}).

Our two running examples make the gap concrete.
To briefly break into terms introduced by vPIFO: the Operation Generation Table and the PIFO Instance Address Table are the structures that encode the running tree's shape and per-instance storage.
The easy change, to \texttt{Strict(hi: zoom, mid: spotify, lo: gmail)}, is the kind of edit vPIFO's substrate is architecturally close to absorbing: it over-provisions logical instances and its Visor can wire them into arbitrary tree shapes, so a targeted append to those tables would register the new traffic and its operations while leaving the running instances untouched.
vPIFO exposes no such edit at runtime, however: those tables are generated offline by its SDL compiler, so installing even this easy change on a running scheduler would require extending the substrate with an online-patch path it does not have today.
The harder restructuring, to \texttt{Strict(hi: zoom, lo: RoundRobin(gmail, spotify))}, lies beyond what the vPIFO substrate can absorb in place: it changes the running tree's shape (inserting a new internal node \texttt{RoundRobin} and re-parenting \texttt{gmail} under it), which is not exposed as an in-place edit on either table.
The closest vPIFO comes is a full reinitialization onto the new shape, which is precisely the stop-the-world baseline we want to improve on.
vPIFO names runtime updating as future work, observing that the scheduling of packets during a modification's transitional phase is unresolved.

Stated plainly: we are not competing with vPIFO and do not claim a better PIFO substrate.
We supply the layer \emph{above} a PIFO substrate (and that substrate could well be vPIFO): the formal transition between two policies, the small patch that realizes it, and the transitionary semantics.
The two layers compose.

\section{A Grammar of Atomic Policy Differences}\label{sec:grammar}

Building on the runtime model of \cref{sec:runtime-model} (a PIFO tree wrapped with per-node scheduling logic, which we call a \emph{control}), \cref{sec:policy-dsl} adds a small policy DSL and compiles it to a control, giving a syntactic handle on the scheduler actually running in hardware.
\cref{sec:delta-grammar} fixes a grammar \(\delta\) of structural edits over that DSL, admitting a production only when the hardware can install it atomically.
\cref{sec:per-production} proves each production \emph{sound}: running it has the policy-level effect we expect and leaves the rest of the tree, its state and its live packets, alone.
\cref{sec:interplay} draws these per-production results together into a single commuting-square theorem.
\cref{sec:hw-preservation} carries the soundness down to hardware.

\subsection{A Policy DSL}\label{sec:policy-dsl}

While a control is the hardware-level configuration the scheduler actually runs, a network operator cannot author one directly, and would not want to: there is no source-level surface for stating a desired policy as a control, and even if the raw hardware knobs were exposed, assembling a control by hand would be tedious and error-prone.
To talk about reconfigurations, we instead expose a programming interface for the operator.
We design a small policy DSL \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} using which the operator can specify their desired policy, and a compiler from \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} terms to controls.

This is essentially what the vPIFO paper's \emph{Scheduling Description Language} does informally~\cite{vPIFO}.
They do not pin down a grammar for SDL or formalize the compilation, so our DSL can be read as a formal core of their concrete language.
The compilation targets differ: vPIFO compiles straight to a virtualized PIFO substrate, whereas we compile first to a control (the abstraction that this section reasons over) and only then lower to a substrate (\cref{sec:hardware}).
The strategy, though, is the same: give the operator a syntactic surface, then compile.

\paragraph{Policy syntax}

\begin{figure}[t]
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
\mathit{flow} \in F\\[2pt]
\mathit{rank} \in \mathbb{N} \qquad w \in \mathbb{R}_{>0}\\[2pt]
\mathit{sPol} \mathrel{::=} \mathit{flow}\\
\qquad \mid\; \Strict(\mathit{rank}_1{:}\,\mathit{sPol}_1, \ldots, \mathit{rank}_n{:}\,\mathit{sPol}_n)\\
\qquad \mid\; \RR(\mathit{sPol}_1, \ldots, \mathit{sPol}_n)\\
\qquad \mid\; \WFQ(w_1{:}\,\mathit{sPol}_1, \ldots, w_n{:}\,\mathit{sPol}_n)\\[8pt]
\mathit{pol} \mathrel{::=} \mathit{flow} \;\mid\; D(\mathit{pol}_1, \ldots, \mathit{pol}_n)\\[8pt]
\mathit{meta?} \mathrel{::=} \epsilon \;\mid\; \mathit{rank} \;\mid\; w\\[2pt]
\mathit{init\_node}_{D} : ()\ \to\ \mathit{node\_state}\\[2pt]
\mathit{init\_slot}_{D} : \mathit{node\_state} \times \mathit{meta?}\ \to\ \mathit{slot\_state}
\end{array}
\]
\caption{The surface syntax \(\mathit{sPol}\) the operator writes, the abstract \(\mathit{pol}\) it compiles to, and the per-discipline compilation interface (\(\mathit{meta?}\), \(\mathit{init\_node}_{D}\), \(\mathit{init\_slot}_{D}\)).}
\label{fig:pol-grammar}
\end{figure}
The operator writes a \emph{surface policy}, an \ensuremath{\mathit{sPol}} (\cref{fig:pol-grammar}, top): either a leaf labeled by a flow, or an internal node running a scheduling discipline \ensuremath{D} over child policies, each arm annotated with the per-arm metadata that \ensuremath{D} needs to schedule it (e.g., a \texttt{Strict} priority, a \texttt{WFQ} weight, nothing for \texttt{RoundRobin}).
The family of disciplines is open: \ensuremath{D} is any discipline equipped with a compilation recipe (given below), and nothing in this section depends on which scheduling disciplines are on hand.
Our surface syntax and implementation instantiate \texttt{Strict}, \texttt{RoundRobin}, and \texttt{WFQ}, chosen because they maintain different kinds of state: \texttt{RoundRobin} keeps no state, \texttt{WFQ} keeps a weight together with a virtual-time tag, \texttt{Strict} keeps a priority, and a further discipline might keep state of a different kind.

Compilation erases these surface annotations, carrying each arm's metadata into its \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_state}} (via \(\mathit{init\_slot}_{D}\) below), where \texttt{push}/\texttt{pop} may read it but not modify it; it yields the \emph{abstract policy} \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} (\cref{fig:pol-grammar}, bottom).
A \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} is either a leaf flow or node \(D(\mathit{pol}_1, \ldots, \mathit{pol}_n)\) that carries no structural mark of the metadata.
The abstract \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} is what \(\delta\) (\cref{sec:delta-grammar}) and the theorems of this section reason over.
We read the arity off by counting children, so, e.g., \texttt{Strict(zoom, gmail)} is the 2-ary instance of the discipline \texttt{Strict}.
Each leaf label denotes a \emph{flow}: a predicate over packets.

A \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} is written against a fixed \emph{flow universe} \(F\): a finite set of flow predicates declared by the operator before constructing the \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}.
A \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} is \emph{valid} when (a) every discipline is applied at the proper arity, (b) every discipline is provided with the per-arm metadata that the discipline requires, and (c) the leaves form a partition of a subset of \(F\): each leaf carries a flow drawn from \(F\), and no two leaves carry the same flow.
If a packet does not match any of the flows in the scheduling tree's leaves, the packet is dropped.
Validity is a condition on the source \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}, not to be confused with the runtime invariant of well-formedness.

\paragraph{Discipline compilation: \(\mathit{init\_node}_{D}\) and \(\mathit{init\_slot}_{D}\)}

Each discipline \ensuremath{D} comes with a mechanical recipe for compiling a node that runs it: (a) a ranker, (b) an initial \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}} for the node, and (c) a \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_state}} for each child of the node.
The two state-seeding projections \(\mathit{init\_node}_{D}\) and \(\mathit{init\_slot}_{D}\) are given with the grammar in \cref{fig:pol-grammar}.
This is exactly the interface for accommodating a new discipline: an operator can bring their own \ensuremath{D} by supplying its per-arm metadata \ensuremath{\mathit{meta?}}, its node initializer \(\mathit{init\_node}_{D}\), its slot initializer \(\mathit{init\_slot}_{D}\), and its ranker \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{rank}\), and everything in this section and its theorems then applies to \ensuremath{D} unchanged.
Two obligations attend that transaction.
The first is path-consistency of admission (\cref{sec:controls}): a flow is admitted either all along its root-to-leaf path or nowhere, so that compilation and every \(\delta\) preserve control well-formedness.
The second is that \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{rank}\) records which discipline the node runs.
This is a labelling requirement rather than a behavioural one, so two disciplines that happen to schedule alike stay distinct, and the operator gets back the one they wrote.
\cref{sec:equivalences} needs exactly this to recover a running policy from a running control.

The \ensuremath{\mathit{meta?}} argument is the per-arm metadata that discipline \ensuremath{D} requires.
For our three representative disciplines, they are: a weight for \texttt{WFQ}, a priority rank for \texttt{Strict}, and nothing for \texttt{RoundRobin}.
\(\mathit{init\_node}_{D}\) runs once per node when the source \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} is compiled into a control, seeding that node's \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}}.
\(\mathit{init\_slot}_{D}\) is called in two places.
At \emph{compile time}, when a source \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} is compiled into a fresh control, it is called once per child arm to seed that child's \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_state}}, taking the parent's just-seeded \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}} and the arm's \ensuremath{\mathit{meta?}} as input.
At \emph{edit time}, when a new arm is spliced under an already-running \ensuremath{D}-parent (\cref{sec:add}), it is called once with the parent's \emph{current} \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}} and the new arm's \ensuremath{\mathit{meta?}} to produce the new arm's \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_state}}.

Choosing \(\mathit{init\_slot}_{D}\) is a scheduling decision, not just a structural one, since the choice changes how a freshly spliced arm competes with the established arms.
We broadly make the choice to ``join the current round''.
For example, if going from \texttt{WFQ(A,B)} to \texttt{WFQ(A,B,C)}, we do not want the newly added \texttt{C} to reap a huge benefit for ``having been silent all this while''; we just want it to join the others with neither a penalty nor an advantage.
The per-discipline recipes that realize this choice, for \texttt{Strict}, \texttt{RoundRobin}, and \texttt{WFQ}, are given in \cref{app:init-slot}.

\paragraph{Policy Compilation: \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} to control}

We write \(\lceil \mathtt{p} \rceil\) for the control compiled from \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} \texttt{p}.
The tree shape of \(\lceil \mathtt{p} \rceil\) exactly matches that of \texttt{p}, and at each node the local triple \((\mathit{state}, \mathit{pifo}, \mathit{z})\) of \cref{sec:controls} is filled in as follows: \ensuremath{\mathit{state}}'s \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}} is seeded by \(\mathit{init\_node}_{D}()\) and each entry of the \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_state}} list is seeded by the corresponding arm's \(\mathit{init\_slot}_{D}\) call; \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} starts empty; and \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} is assembled from its two halves, with \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{rank}\) taken from \ensuremath{D} and the classifier read off the tree, so that \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves}\) sends each flow served beneath the node toward that flow's leaf and \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{admit} = \mathrm{dom}(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves})\).
A freshly compiled control therefore admits every flow it serves.

\subsection{A Grammar for Policy Tree Differences}\label{sec:delta-grammar}

We fix a small grammar \(\delta\) of \emph{policy tree edits}: structural rewrites that transform one running policy tree into another.
Each production has two readings, both pinned down per-production in \cref{sec:per-production}.
We write \(\csem{\delta}\) for the \emph{control-level} denotation, a rewrite on the running control, of type \(\mathit{control} \rightharpoonup \mathit{control}\).
We write \(\psem{\delta}\) for the \emph{pol-level} denotation, a closed-form effect on the operator's policy, of type \(\mathit{pol} \rightharpoonup \mathit{pol}\).
The grammar is shaped by two demands.
It must be \emph{hardware-realizable}: a production is admitted into the grammar only if \(\csem{\delta}\) can be committed atomically by the hardware substrate (\cref{sec:hardware}); this is exactly what makes each edit \emph{atomic}.
It must also be \emph{pol-explainable}: every production's effect can be stated back to the operator in the same DSL they wrote, as a closed-form map \(\psem{\delta} : \mathit{pol} \rightharpoonup \mathit{pol}\) on policies.
This is what keeps the operator in the loop: the transition planner (\cref{sec:sequences}) can infer a \(\delta\), report precisely which policy change it makes, and echo that change back for the operator to approve.

An edit describes \emph{where} in the tree the change lands and \emph{what} the change is.
Throughout this section, \texttt{p1} is the presently running policy; we write \texttt{p1@}\(\mathit{path}\) for the subtree of \texttt{p1} reached by following \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} down from its root.
We call the node that a production's \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} names its \emph{edit site}.
One production is irregular in this respect: \ChangeRoot{} gives the path to the surviving subtree.

\begin{figure}[t]
\small
\[
\begin{array}{@{}r@{\;\;}l@{}}
\delta \mathrel{::=} & \Add(\mathit{path}, D, \mathit{arm}, \mathit{meta?})\\
\mid & \ChangeMeta(\mathit{path}, \mathit{meta})\\
\mid & \Quiesce(\mathit{path})\\
\mid & \Remove(\mathit{path})\\
\mid & \Designate(\mathit{path}, \mathit{survivor})\\

\mathit{path} \mathrel{::=} & [\,] \;\mid\; i \mathbin{::} \mathit{path}
\end{array}
\]
\caption{A grammar of atomic policy tree differences.}
\label{fig:delta-grammar}
\end{figure}

The grammar is given in \cref{fig:delta-grammar}.
A \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} is a (possibly empty) sequence of child indices \texttt{i} read from the root.
\ensuremath{\mathit{arm}} and \ensuremath{\mathit{survivor}} are \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}-typed payloads (\cref{sec:policy-dsl}).

Edits that would have to discard packets are expressly not in the grammar.
For example, \Remove{} has a precondition saying that it may fire only when its target subtree is already empty, so structural deletion never has to discard a buffered packet.
The richer reconfigurations an operator may want (retiring a subtree that presently has packets buffered in it, replacing a subtree in-place, pruning a tree down to a subtree) are realized as sequences of these productions, called \emph{idioms} (\cref{sec:sequences}).

\cref{sec:per-production} takes up the seven productions one at a time.
When \(\mathtt{p1} \eqR \mathtt{p2}\), the planner emits the empty sequence (\cref{sec:sequences}), and the live control is left untouched.

Before we can prove those productions correct or read a running policy back to the operator, though, we need to say when two policies, or two controls, count as the same, and how to recover a \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} from a control.
We set up that apparatus first (\cref{sec:equivalences}), and then return to the productions themselves (\cref{sec:per-production}).

\subsection{Equivalences, and Reading Off the Running Policy}\label{sec:equivalences}

We write \(\mathtt{C} \sim \mathtt{C'}\) for the equivalence on well-formed controls that holds when the two are identical except in their live \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} contents and \ensuremath{\mathit{state}}s.
Two \(\sim\)-related controls thus have the same tree shape and the same \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} at every node, and differ at most in the transient live state that traffic leaves behind.
Serving \texttt{push}/\texttt{pop} operations is one way controls come to be \(\sim\)-related: \texttt{push} and \texttt{pop} move only the live \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}}s and \ensuremath{\mathit{state}}s and leave the skeleton and \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} untouched, so any control is \(\sim\)-equivalent to a version of itself that has served some push/pop traffic.

We write \(p \eqR p'\) for the smallest congruence on \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} such that, at any internal \ensuremath{D}-node, permuting the child arms gives a congruent \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}.
The per-arm metadata that discipline \ensuremath{D} requires (a \texttt{WFQ} weight, a \texttt{Strict} priority rank) travels with its arm under the permutation; the metadata is what carries the scheduling-meaningful content, so such a permutation does not change the scheduler.

We write \(\mathtt{C} \simR \mathtt{C'}\) for the equivalence obtained by closing \(\sim\) under sibling permutations.
Every \(\sim\)-equivalent pair is \(\simR\)-equivalent, but not conversely.

\paragraph{The bridge: \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\)}

We write \(\lfloor \mathtt{C} \rfloor\) to mean ``the \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} that \texttt{C} realizes''.
Intuitively, \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) reads the policy straight off the control: it walks the control's tree and recovers, node by node, the \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} term the control implements, discarding the live \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} and \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} contents that traffic happens to have left behind.
We define it by structural recursion on \texttt{C}, one clause per node form.
Both clauses read out of \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} (\cref{sec:controls}): a leaf is named by the flow its classifier serves, and an internal node is named by the discipline its ranker names.

\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
\lfloor \mathtt{C} \rfloor = f,\ \text{when \texttt{C} is a leaf and } \mathrm{dom}(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves}) = \{f\};\\[4pt]
\lfloor \mathtt{C} \rfloor = D(\lfloor \mathtt{c}_1 \rfloor, \ldots, \lfloor \mathtt{c}_n \rfloor),\ \text{when \texttt{C} is internal}\\
\qquad \text{with children } \mathtt{c}_1 \ldots \mathtt{c}_n \text{ and } \mathit{z}.\mathit{rank} \text{ names } D.
\end{array}
\]

The recursion runs on a finite tree and consults neither \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} nor \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} nor \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{admit}\).
A structural recursion over a finite tree returns a single value.
So every well-formed control determines one \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}, independently of how that control was reached, and we may write \(\lfloor \mathtt{C} \rfloor\) and use it as a function.
The \(\eqR\)-slack that appears below lives in the \emph{comparisons} we draw against an operator's \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}, never in the value \(\lfloor \mathtt{C} \rfloor\) itself.

Three facts follow.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Compiler correctness.} Compiling a \texttt{p} to a control and reading it straight back recovers \texttt{p} itself, up to the sibling-order freedom the compiler enjoyed in laying it out: \(\lfloor \lceil \mathtt{p} \rceil \rfloor \eqR \mathtt{p}\).
This is immediate from the compilation of \cref{sec:policy-dsl}, which installs \ensuremath{D}'s ranker at each internal node and has each leaf serve its own flow.
The relation is \(\eqR\) rather than \(=\) precisely because the compiler may have chosen a different arm order when creating \(\lceil \mathtt{p} \rceil\).
\item \emph{Insensitivity to live contents.} \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) reads only the tree shape, each leaf's served flow, and each node's ranker-discipline.
It never reads \ensuremath{\mathit{state}}, \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}}, or \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{admit}\).
So \(\sim\)-related controls, which share shape and \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} outright are equal when read off using \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\). 
Thus, if \(\mathtt{C} \sim \mathtt{C'}\), then \(\lfloor \mathtt{C} \rfloor = \lfloor \mathtt{C'} \rfloor\).
This covers \texttt{push} and \texttt{pop}, which move only live contents.
\item \emph{Closure under \(\delta\).} A production's two readings (\cref{sec:delta-grammar}) agree: editing the control and reading it off gives the same \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}, up to sibling order, as applying the \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}-level effect to the read-off. That is, \(\lfloor \csem{\delta}(\mathtt{C}) \rfloor \eqR \psem{\delta}(\lfloor \mathtt{C} \rfloor)\).
Unlike the first two, this is not immediate, and it is not part of the definition either.
It is a theorem, proved one production at a time in \cref{sec:per-production} and collected as \cref{thm:interplay}.
The relation is again \(\eqR\), not \(=\), because \(\csem{\delta}\) may lay the edited node's children out in any sibling order.
\end{enumerate}

This is how we will discharge Obligation 1 of \cref{sec:intro}.
Even when the presently running \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} is just an intermediate policy that the runtime crafted on the way from the running policy to the operator's true request, we can still read off and report to the operator exactly what is running.
\cref{sec:interplay} takes up the third fact and proves it, as the ``commuting square'' these pieces trace out.

\subsection{Per-Production Soundness}\label{sec:per-production}

For each production, we must discharge five obligations.
The first three stem from \emph{hardware-realizability} (the substrate can install \(\csem{\delta}\) atomically, so the production rule must say what \(\csem{\delta}\) does and must produce a well-formed control with precisely specified state).
The fourth stems from \emph{pol-explainability} (the edit must mean exactly what the operator's \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} change asked).
Together these four certify that the production is a \emph{correct} edit.
Correctness, though, is not enough: the stop-the-world reset of \cref{sec:intro} is realizable and explainable too, and it is the baseline we wish to beat.
We beat it with the property of \emph{non-disruption}: installing \(\delta\) may disturb previously buffered traffic \emph{only} in service of the newly installed policy; the \emph{very act of changing policies} never introduces arbitrary artifacts such as drops, reorders, or rewrites among buffered packets.
Throughout, we write \texttt{C} for the pre-edit control and \texttt{C'} for the post-edit control, and \(\mathtt{p} = \lfloor \mathtt{C} \rfloor\), \(\mathtt{p'} = \lfloor \mathtt{C'} \rfloor\) for the policies they realize.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Definition.} Specify \(\csem{\delta} : \mathit{control} \rightharpoonup \mathit{control}\) together with the preconditions under which it is defined.
\item \emph{Preservation of \(\vdash\).} \(\vdash \mathtt{C}\) implies \(\vdash \mathtt{C'}\).
Any packets or index entries that \(\csem{\delta}\) drops or adds must leave the per-node pifo and packet counts in balance.
\item \emph{Preservation of state.} At every node structurally shared between \texttt{C} and \texttt{C'} and outside the production's local edit site, the local \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} is left unchanged.
At the edit site, and at every node of a freshly-spawned subtree, the state is exactly what the production's \(\mathit{init\_node}_{D}\) / \(\mathit{init\_slot}_{D}\) (\cref{sec:policy-dsl}) invocation prescribes.
\item \emph{Characterization.} Give \(\psem{\delta}\) in closed form and prove that reading the policy off the edited control agrees, up to \(\eqR\), with applying \(\psem{\delta}\) to the policy read off the original: \(\mathtt{p'} \eqR \psem{\delta}(\mathtt{p})\).
\item \emph{Non-disruption of in-flight traffic.} Applying \(\delta\) does not rewrite, reorder, or drop any packet already admitted to \texttt{C}:
each already-enqueued entry keeps the rank it was given when it was \texttt{push}ed, so nothing buffered is lost, corrupted, or reordered relative to the rest of the backlog.
Buffered packets may of course be affected by the new policy in force (e.g., after \(\WFQ(1{:}A,\, 9{:}B) \to \WFQ(99{:}A,\, 9{:}B)\), a newly arriving \(A\)-packet can overtake previously buffered \(B\) packets), but that is an example of the reconfigured policy \emph{correctly} governing new arrivals, not a disturbance of the backlog.
\cref{lem:nondisruption} below reduces this obligation, for every production, to a check on the production's \emph{footprint} (the nodes and fields the production touches, named per production below).
\end{itemize}

The last four obligations assume \(\csem{\delta}(\mathtt{C})\) is defined; outside the region its Definition carves out, \(\csem{\delta}(\mathtt{C})\) is undefined.

Non-disruption need not be reproved from scratch at each production; it follows from a single fact about \texttt{pop}.

\begin{lemma}[Non-disruption reduces to \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}}-content preservation]\label{lem:nondisruption}
A \texttt{pop} reads only \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} contents: it pops the root index-\ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} to a child, recurses, and finally emits a leaf packet, never consulting any \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} or \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} (\cref{sec:runtime-model}).
Hence if \(\csem{\delta}\) leaves the contents of every non-empty \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} of \texttt{C} unchanged (by never rewriting, reordering, and removing a live entry and by introducing only empty \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}}s), then no previously buffered packet in \texttt{C} is dropped, reordered, or re-ranked in \texttt{C'}. That is, the buffered backlog is preserved entry-for-entry, and \(\delta\) is non-disruptive.
\end{lemma}

We therefore discharge non-disruption per production by naming the production's footprint and checking that it contains no live \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} entry.
Only the productions that add or drop a live \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} entry, \Designate, \Undesignate, and \ChangeRoot{} (and the idioms of \cref{sec:sequences}), need an argument beyond naming the footprint; the rest follow from \cref{lem:nondisruption} directly.

The lemma can read counterintuitively. Inverting the two priorities of \(\Strict(\mathtt{hi}: A, \mathtt{lo}: B)\) with \ChangeMeta{} does \emph{not} change the next \texttt{pop}: ranks are frozen at \texttt{push} time, so the parent's index-\ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} already holds \(A\) at \texttt{hi} and \(B\) at \texttt{lo}, and \ChangeMeta{} rewrites neither.
Only packets pushed after the change are ranked under the new priorities.

\paragraph{Notation}

The pol-level rules below read, overwrite, and shrink child lists.
We write \texttt{ts[i]} for the \texttt{i}-th child and \texttt{ts[t/i]} for \texttt{ts} with its \texttt{i}-th child overwritten by \texttt{t}; this leaves the arity unchanged.
\texttt{ts[-/k]} drops the \texttt{k}-th child; later children shift one slot to the left.
Indices follow the \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} convention of \cref{sec:delta-grammar}.

The control-level rules walk controls the same way.
We write an internal control node as \(\langle\mathit{state}, \mathit{pifo}, \mathit{z}\rangle(\mathtt{cs})\),
its triple \((\mathit{state}, \mathit{pifo}, \mathit{z})\) of \cref{sec:controls} together with its child list \texttt{cs}, and reuse \(\mathtt{cs}[\mathtt{C'}/i]\) and \(\mathtt{cs}\mathbin{+\!\!+}[\mathtt{C'}]\) for the list operations above.
The discipline is not a field of its own; the ranker carries it (\cref{sec:controls}).

Two invariants link a node's classifier to the tree, and every production preserves both.
First, \(\mathrm{dom}(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves})\) at a node is exactly the set of flows served by the leaves beneath it, and \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves}\) sends each of them to the child that leads on toward its leaf.
Second, \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{admit} \subseteq \mathrm{dom}(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves})\), with the containment strict exactly where a flow has been silenced but not yet removed, and admission is path-consistent (\cref{sec:controls}).
Splicing in a new leaf, for instance, means extending both parts at each node along that leaf's path so the new flow is served and admitted.
Those nodes' \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}}, \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_states}}, and ranker are left untouched.

A production's path names a target node, but it is often more convenient to work with the target's parent and the index the parent uses to reach it.
We recover both by splitting the given path as \(\pi \mathbin{+\!\!+} [k]\): \(\pi\) is the path to the parent, and \texttt{k} is the child index by which the parent reaches the target.

Most productions edit only the node that their path points to.
For such a production we use a small combinator: given a node transformer \(g\) and a path \(\pi\), write \(\mathtt{at}(\pi, g)\) for the operation that walks to the node at \(\pi\), applies \(g\) there, and copies every other node of the tree unchanged:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
\mathtt{at}([\,], g)\,(\mathtt{N}) = g(\mathtt{N})\\
\mathtt{at}(i \mathbin{::} \mathtt{rest}, g)\,\langle\mathit{state},\mathit{pifo},\mathit{z}\rangle(\mathtt{cs}) =\\
\quad \langle\mathit{state},\mathit{pifo},\mathit{z}\rangle(\mathtt{cs}[\,\mathtt{at}(\mathtt{rest}, g)(\mathtt{cs}[i]) / i\,])
\end{array}
\]
The recursive case walks one step down and writes the result back, carrying \(\langle\mathit{state},\mathit{pifo},\mathit{z}\rangle\) unchanged; the same combinator serves on \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}, where a node is \(D(\mathtt{ts})\).

As a warm-up, we discharge the five obligations in some detail for the production \Add.
For the rest we give the closed-form \(\psem{\cdot}\), the per-node rule, and only the points where an argument departs from \Add's.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\Add(\pi, D, \mathit{arm}, \mathit{meta?})\)}{Add}}\label{sec:add}

\Add{}'s arguments are \(\pi : \mathit{path}\), the node that will receive the new arm as its last child; \(D\), the discipline that node runs; \(\mathit{arm} : \mathit{pol}\), the policy of the new arm; and \ensuremath{\mathit{meta?}}, the per-arm metadata \ensuremath{D} requires (\cref{sec:policy-dsl}), present exactly when \(\mathit{init\_slot}_{D}\) needs it.

\paragraph{Definition}

We must define \(\csem{\Add(\pi, D, \mathit{arm}, \mathit{meta?})} : \mathit{control} \rightharpoonup \mathit{control}\) together with the preconditions on the already-running control such that \Add{} is defined.

Say the presently running control is \texttt{C}.
Intuitively, \Add{} inserts a freshly compiled subtree \(\lceil \mathit{arm} \rceil\) as the new last child of the subtree \texttt{C@}\(\pi\).
In addition, for traffic to reach the new arm, the flows it serves (those bound for leaves inside it) must be admitted along the whole path: every node from the root down to \texttt{C@}\(\pi\) must begin to accept the new flows and route them toward the arm. Before, those flows were accepted nowhere and dropped; \Add{} extends the admission at each node on the path, and touches nothing else.

\(\csem{\Add(\pi, D, \mathit{arm}, \mathit{meta?})}(\mathtt{C})\) is defined when three things hold: the path \(\pi\) resolves in \texttt{C} to an internal node running discipline \ensuremath{D}; the payload \ensuremath{\mathit{meta?}} matches the slot-initialization schema of \ensuremath{D} (present when \(\mathit{init\_slot}_{D}\) requires it); and \ensuremath{\mathit{arm}}'s leaf labels are disjoint from those of \texttt{C}.
The disjointness precondition rules out \emph{splitting} an already-running flow.
Going from a policy with a single \texttt{gmail} leaf to one that splits it into \texttt{gmail\_business} and \texttt{gmail\_personal} would have to re-sort the packets already buffered under \texttt{gmail} into the two new leaves, relocating admitted traffic; \Add{} only introduces flows disjoint from what is running, so it never has to relocate admitted traffic.

We define \(\csem{\Add}\) by recursion on \(\pi\) (\cref{fig:add-rules}, top).
The discipline \ensuremath{D} and new arm \ensuremath{\mathit{arm}} thread unchanged through the recursion, so the rules carry them; \ensuremath{\mathit{meta?}} is consumed only at the base case, which seeds the new slot, so only that rule mentions it.
Two abbreviations appear.
\(\mathtt{flows}(\mathit{arm})\) is the set of flows at \(\lceil \mathit{arm} \rceil\)'s leaves.
For \(\mathtt{admit}\), recall that a node's discipline fixes a packet's rank from the local \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} and the chosen child alone, not from the packet itself.
Admitting a flow at an already-running node therefore calls for no new rank rule: the node's ranker already covers the child that the flow will route to.
So \(\mathtt{admit}(\mathit{z}, F, i)\) touches the classifier and nothing else.
It records that child \(i\) is where \(F\) is served, and adds \(F\) to the admitted set:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
\mathtt{admit}(\mathit{z}, F, i).\mathit{serves} = \mathit{z}.\mathit{serves}\,[\,f \mapsto i \;\text{for}\; f \in F\,],\\[3pt]
\mathtt{admit}(\mathit{z}, F, i).\mathit{admit} = \mathit{z}.\mathit{admit} \cup F,\\[3pt]
\mathtt{admit}(\mathit{z}, F, i).\mathit{rank} = \mathit{z}.\mathit{rank}.
\end{array}
\]
Since \texttt{C@}\(\pi\) has \(k\) children before the splice, appending the new one lands at the next free index \(k\); and because \(\mathtt{flows}(\mathit{arm})\) lies outside \(\mathrm{dom}(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves})\), each \(\mathtt{admit}\) is conflict-free.

The base case (\(\pi = []\)) has reached the parent of the new arm: it appends \(\lceil \mathit{arm} \rceil\) as the last child (slot \texttt{k}), appends that slot's bookkeeping \(\mathit{init\_slot}_{D}(\mathit{node\_state}, \mathit{meta?})\) (with \ensuremath{D}, the discipline the production carries), and extends its transaction to \(\mathtt{admit}(\mathit{z}, \mathtt{flows}(\mathit{arm}), k)\), sending the new flows to the new child; \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}} and \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} are untouched.
Appending the arm is legitimate because our arm-order freedom (\cref{sec:policy-dsl}) lets us place the new child last.
The recursive case steps into the on-path child \texttt{i}, writes the result back, and extends this ancestor's transaction to \(\mathtt{admit}(\mathit{z}, \mathtt{flows}(\mathit{arm}), i)\), sending the new flows one step further down toward \(\pi\); its \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}}, \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_states}}, and \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} are carried unchanged.

So every node on the path from the root to \texttt{C@}\(\pi\) has its transaction extended to admit and route the new flows, and nothing else about those nodes changes.
The new flows were admitted nowhere before (by disjointness), so each extension is conflict-free and no in-flight packet's route moves.

\paragraph{Preservation of \(\vdash\)}

We must show that \(\vdash \mathtt{C}\) implies \(\vdash \mathtt{C'}\).
That is, any pifo entries or packets that \(\csem{\Add(\pi, D, \mathit{arm}, \mathit{meta?})}\) introduces must leave the per-node pifo and packet counts in balance.

The on-path ancestors have only their transactions extended; but \(\vdash\) balances \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} entries against buffered packets, and editing \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} adds neither an entry nor a packet,
so the only nodes that can affect it are \texttt{C@}\(\pi\) and the freshly spawned \(\lceil \mathit{arm} \rceil\).
At \texttt{C'@}\(\pi\),
the \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} is unchanged (\(\mathtt{C'@}\pi.\mathit{pifo} = \mathtt{C@}\pi.\mathit{pifo}\)) and holds no entry equal to the new slot \texttt{k}, since no pre-existing entry can name a slot that did not exist in \texttt{C}; the new arm at slot \texttt{k} holds no packets, so slot \texttt{k}'s count reads \(0 = 0\).
Every other slot at \texttt{C@}\(\pi\) keeps its index, its packets, and its entries unchanged, so its count is inherited.
Inside \(\lceil \mathit{arm} \rceil\), \(\vdash\) holds because \(\lceil \cdot \rceil\) compiles a well-formed control whose pifos start empty.
Every other node is untouched.
Nothing needs repair.

\paragraph{Preservation of state}

We must show that at every node structurally shared between \texttt{C} and \texttt{C'} and outside the edit site, the local \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} is left unchanged; and that at the edit site, and at every node of the freshly spawned subtree, the \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} is exactly what \(\mathit{init\_node}_{D}\) / \(\mathit{init\_slot}_{D}\) (\cref{sec:policy-dsl}) prescribes.

The recursion carries every shared node's \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} and \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} unchanged,\footnote{Such a node's \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} is extended, as described in the Definition, but \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} is not part of \ensuremath{\mathit{state}}.} including at each proper ancestor of \(\pi\), where only the child list and the transaction are touched.
At \texttt{C@}\(\pi\), \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}} is unchanged and \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_states}} is appended with exactly \(\mathit{init\_slot}_{D}(\mathtt{C@}\pi.\mathit{node\_state}, \mathit{meta?})\), as required at the edit site.
Inside \(\lceil \mathit{arm} \rceil\), every node's state is what its own discipline's \ensuremath{\mathit{init\_node}} / \ensuremath{\mathit{init\_slot}} prescribes, by construction of \(\lceil \cdot \rceil\) (\cref{sec:policy-dsl}).

\paragraph{Characterization}

The Definition said what \Add{} does to the running control; Characterization says what it \emph{means} to the network operator.
The two readings live in different worlds.
\(\csem{\Add}\) is the rewrite the hardware installs, a partial map \(\mathit{control} \rightharpoonup \mathit{control}\).
\(\psem{\Add}\), by contrast, is a closed-form map \(\mathit{pol} \rightharpoonup \mathit{pol}\) on the operator's source language; it names how the policy the operator sees changes.
Characterization bridges them: running \(\csem{\Add}\) on the control realizes, at \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}-level, exactly the edit \(\psem{\Add}\) names, which is what lets the runtime echo the new policy back to the operator (\cref{sec:equivalences}).
Concretely, we must state \(\psem{\Add(\pi, D, \mathit{arm}, \mathit{meta?})}\) in closed form and prove \(\mathtt{p'} \eqR \psem{\Add(\pi, D, \mathit{arm}, \mathit{meta?})}(\mathtt{p})\).

We define \(\psem{\cdot}\) by recursion on \(\pi\) (\cref{fig:add-rules}, bottom).
Its node patterns \(\mathtt{D\ ts}\) and \(\mathtt{E\ ts}\) split a \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} node into its discipline and child list \texttt{ts}.
The base case reuses the payload name \ensuremath{D}, firing only when the reached node runs that discipline; an ancestor's discipline \texttt{E} is unconstrained and simply carried along.
The metadata \ensuremath{\mathit{meta?}} plays no role in \(\psem{\cdot}\): it lands in \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_states}}, which \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) does not read (a pure \ChangeMeta{} leaves \(\mathtt{p}\) unchanged, \cref{sec:changemeta}), so only \(\csem{\Add}\) uses it, at \(\mathit{init\_slot}_{D}\).

\emph{Proof.}
Both recursions descend \(\pi\) in the same shape, so we induct on \(\pi\).
At the base (\(\pi = []\)), \(\psem{\cdot}\) appends \ensuremath{\mathit{arm}} and \(\csem{\Add}\) appends \(\lceil \mathit{arm} \rceil\); since \(\lfloor \lceil \mathit{arm} \rceil \rfloor \eqR \mathit{arm}\), the two agree on the new child up to \(\eqR\), and every existing child is untouched.
At a recursive step, both descend into child \texttt{i} and write the result back, so the claim follows from the induction hypothesis; the ancestor itself adds nothing to reconcile, since \(\csem{\Add}\) leaves its \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} and \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} unchanged and its \ensuremath{\mathit{z}}-extension is confined to the classifier of an internal node, which \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) does not read.
So the two sides agree all the way down the spine and can differ only inside \(\lceil \mathit{arm} \rceil\), where sibling order is free; that is exactly the \(\eqR\)-slack the claim allows, giving \(\mathtt{p'} \eqR \psem{\Add(\pi, D, \mathit{arm}, \mathit{meta?})}(\mathtt{p})\).

\paragraph{Non-disruption}

\(\csem{\Add}\)'s footprint is the new empty slot \texttt{k} at \texttt{C@}\(\pi\), the freshly spawned empty subtree \(\lceil \mathit{arm} \rceil\), and the on-path \ensuremath{\mathit{z}}-extensions.
None of these is a live \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} entry: the new arm holds no packets, and a \ensuremath{\mathit{z}}-edit changes no \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}}.
So \Add{} is non-disruptive by \cref{lem:nondisruption}.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\ChangeMeta(\tau, \mathit{meta})\)}{ChangeMeta}}\label{sec:changemeta}

\ChangeMeta{} retunes a single already-running arm, resetting the per-arm metadata its parent schedules it by (a \Strict{} priority, a \WFQ{} weight, or whatever the parent's discipline reads off a slot), without moving a packet or disturbing any sibling.

\(\tau : \mathit{path}\) is the target arm.
It is non-empty (an arm has a parent), and we destruct it as \(\pi \mathbin{+\!\!+} [k]\), so \texttt{k} is the arm's slot at its parent \texttt{C@}\(\pi\).
The payload \(\mathit{meta}\) is a fresh value of whatever per-arm metadata the parent's discipline reads off a slot (\cref{sec:policy-dsl}); for our three disciplines that is a \Strict{} priority or a \WFQ{} weight.

\paragraph{Definition}

\[
\csem{\ChangeMeta(\pi \mathbin{+\!\!+} [k], \mathit{meta})} = \mathtt{at}(\pi,\, \mathtt{setmeta}_k),
\]
defined when the parent at \(\pi\) runs a discipline that carries per-arm metadata (\Strict{} or \WFQ{} among our three; \RR{}, having none, is never a target).
\(\mathtt{setmeta}_k(\mathit{meta})\) is the node transformer that overwrites the per-arm metadatum in \(\mathit{slot\_states}[\mathtt{k}]\) with \(\mathit{meta}\) and leaves every other field of the node unchanged.
Under \Strict{} the slot is just its priority, which becomes \(\mathit{meta}\).
Under \WFQ, \texttt{init\_slot\_WFQ} (\cref{sec:policy-dsl}) seeds the slot with its weight and a last-finish tag; \(\mathtt{setmeta}_k\) resets the weight to \(\mathit{meta}\) and leaves that tag untouched (the Note below says why).

\paragraph{Characterization}

\[
\psem{\ChangeMeta(\tau, \mathit{meta})}\,\mathtt{p} = \mathtt{p}.
\]
\(\psem{\cdot}\) is the identity because the rewritten field lives in \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_states}}, which \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) does not read (\cref{sec:equivalences}), so \(\mathtt{p'} = \mathtt{p}\).

\paragraph{Preservation and non-disruption}

\(\mathtt{setmeta}_k\) touches one \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_state}} field and no child, so \(\vdash\)-counts and every node's structure are inherited and \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} elsewhere is unchanged.
Its footprint is that single field, which is not a live \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} entry, so \ChangeMeta{} is non-disruptive by \cref{lem:nondisruption}: an in-flight entry's rank was fixed when it was pushed, and the new metadata governs only future pushes.

\paragraph{Note: Why the virtual-finish tag is preserved (WFQ)}

When the parent runs WFQ, the virtual-finish tag at slot \texttt{k} is deliberately \emph{preserved} and not \emph{reset}: resetting would either reward or penalize the targeted arm by yanking it out of the current round, whereas \texttt{init\_slot\_WFQ} (\cref{sec:policy-dsl}) was designed precisely to let a fresh arm ``join the current round'' without disturbing siblings, and the same principle applies to a weight change on an already-running arm.
Under Strict the slot carries no comparable round-bookkeeping field, so the question does not arise.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\Quiesce(\tau)\)}{Quiesce}}\label{sec:quiesce}

\(\tau : \mathit{path}\) is the path to the target subtree to silence.

Say the presently running control is \texttt{C}.
\Quiesce{} closes the gate on every flow served under \texttt{C@}\(\tau\), so that no packet bound for a leaf there is admitted anywhere, and changes nothing else.
In particular it leaves every node's routing in place, so buffered packets can still be popped out.

\paragraph{Definition}

\(\csem{\Quiesce(\tau)}(\mathtt{C})\) is defined whenever \(\tau\) resolves in \texttt{C}; the empty path silences the whole tree, and there is no precondition on the subtree's contents.
Let \(L = \mathtt{flows}(\texttt{C@}\tau)\) be the flows to silence.
Write \(\mathit{z} \setminus L\) for \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} with \(L\) struck from its admitted set, so \((\mathit{z} \setminus L).\mathit{admit} = \mathit{z}.\mathit{admit} \setminus L\).
Its service map and its ranker are unchanged.
The nodes that those flows traverse are the spine down to \(\tau\) together with \(\tau\)'s whole subtree:
\[
V_\tau = \{\, \texttt{C@}\nu \;:\; \nu \sqsubseteq \tau \,\} \;\cup\; \{\, \text{every node of } \texttt{C@}\tau \,\}.
\]
\Quiesce{} narrows the admitted set at each of these nodes and touches nothing else:
\[
\csem{\Quiesce(\tau)}(\mathtt{C}) = \mathtt{at}\big(V_\tau,\ \langle\mathtt{s},\mathtt{q},\mathit{z}\rangle(\mathtt{cs}) \mapsto \langle\mathtt{s},\mathtt{q},\ \mathit{z} \setminus L\rangle(\mathtt{cs})\big),
\]
where \(\mathtt{at}(V, g)\) applies \(g\) at every node in the set \(V\) and copies every node outside \(V\) unchanged, the set analogue of the single-site \(\mathtt{at}(\pi, g)\) above.
Every field other than \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{admit}\), and every node outside \(V_\tau\), is left unchanged; the Note below says why the restriction must reach all of \(V_\tau\) rather than the root alone.

\paragraph{Characterization}

As with \ChangeMeta, \(\psem{\Quiesce(\tau)}\) is the identity on \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}: \Quiesce's whole effect is on the admitted sets \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{admit}\), which \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) does not read (\cref{sec:equivalences}).
Every leaf under \texttt{C@}\(\tau\) still serves the flow it served before, since \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves}\) is untouched, so \(\mathtt{p'} = \mathtt{p}\).
Silencing closes the gate and leaves the mapping, so a silenced leaf keeps both its way out and its identity.

\paragraph{Preservation and non-disruption}

Every node's \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}}, \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_states}}, \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}}, and children are unchanged, so \(\vdash\) and \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} are preserved.
The footprint is the admitted-set restrictions across \(V_\tau\), which touch no \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} entry, so \Quiesce{} is non-disruptive by \cref{lem:nondisruption}: packets already buffered under \texttt{C@}\(\tau\) keep their entries and still drain on \texttt{pop}; only future \texttt{push}es are refused.

\paragraph{Note: Why touch every node on every quiesced push path}

Under \cref{sec:policy-dsl}'s parallel \texttt{push}, every node on the path from a packet's destination leaf to the system root mints an index via its local \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} independently of the others.
If we restricted \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} only at a strict subset of those nodes (only the root of the tree, or only inside \texttt{C@}\(\tau\)), the rest would happily mint indices and enqueue them.
The tree would then be malformed: some node's index-\ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} would hold an entry with no matching packet in the subtree below it (or, symmetrically, a buffered packet with no index above it), breaking \(\vdash\); a later \texttt{pop} that followed such an entry could descend toward a leaf with nothing to emit and get stuck.
\Quiesce{} therefore narrows uniformly, at the leaf (so it refuses to enqueue) and at every ancestor of every quiesced leaf (so no node mints a stray index).
The cost is touching \(|T|\) leaves and the union of their ancestor chains; the gains are two: the rejection preserves \(\vdash \mathtt{C'}\), and admission stays a purely node-local decision, so the massively parallel \texttt{push} of \cref{sec:policy-dsl} keeps working unchanged, with no node having to coordinate with another.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\Remove(\tau)\)}{Remove}}\label{sec:remove}

As before, \(\tau : \mathit{path}\) is the non-empty target arm; destruct it as \(\pi \mathbin{+\!\!+} [k]\), \texttt{k} its slot at parent \texttt{C@}\(\pi\).
\Remove{} unhooks that arm and renumbers higher siblings down by one; it fires only when the arm's subtree is empty (see note below).

\paragraph{Definition}

Write \(R = \mathtt{flows}(\texttt{C@}\tau)\) for the flows that the doomed arm serves, and \(\mathit{z} \setminus\!\!\setminus R\) for \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} with \(R\) struck from its service map (and hence, by the containment of \cref{sec:controls}, from its admitted set too), with its ranker unchanged.
\Remove{} drops the arm at its parent and strikes \(R\) from the service map of every proper ancestor above that parent:
\[
\csem{\Remove(\pi \mathbin{+\!\!+} [k])} = \mathtt{at}\big(A_\pi,\ \mathit{z} \setminus\!\!\setminus R\big) \circ \mathtt{at}(\pi,\, \mathtt{drop}_k),
\]
where \(A_\pi = \{\, \texttt{C@}\nu \;:\; \nu \sqsubset \pi \,\}\) is the spine strictly above the parent.
It is defined when \(\tau\) resolves in \texttt{C}, the subtree at \(\tau\) is empty, and \texttt{C@}\(\tau\) does not run the discipline \(\Strictstar\) (the planner reaches a \(\Strictstar\) only through \Undesignate).
\(\mathtt{drop}_k\) removes child \texttt{k} and its \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_states}} entry, shifts siblings \(> k\) down by one, and adjusts the node's classifier: the flows that slot \texttt{k} served leave \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves}\) and \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{admit}\) alike, and a surviving entry naming \(i > k\) comes to name \(i-1\).
The ranker is untouched.
Emptiness and \(\vdash \mathtt{C}\) force \(\mathtt{C@}\pi.\mathit{pifo}\) to hold \emph{no entry} equal to \texttt{k}, so \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} loses no entry, and surviving entries keep their child.
\xxx[AM to Zhiyuan]{Does Rio's substrate actually key children by stable handles, so \Remove{} renumbers no live entry? If so, we can say so plainly here. The parenthetical that used to make the claim in passing is gone for now, since it pointed forward into \cref{sec:hardware}.}
\ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}}, and every field of every node outside \texttt{C@}\(\pi\) and the removed subtree other than the ancestor service maps just named, is unchanged.

\paragraph{Note: Why the ancestors are retracted too}

Dropping the arm at its parent alone would leave every node above still mapping \(R\) toward it.
A later \texttt{push} of an \(R\)-packet would then be admitted at an ancestor and enqueue an index there, while the parent, which no longer serves \(R\), would enqueue nothing.
This would leave \(\vdash\) broken.
In the \Retire{} idiom (\cref{sec:idioms}) a preceding \Quiesce{} has already closed those gates, so the retraction is redundant there.
\Remove{} performs it unconditionally, so that it is sound on its own, as when it undoes an \Add{} whose arm never carried traffic.


\paragraph{Characterization}

\[
\psem{\Remove(\pi \mathbin{+\!\!+} [k])} = \mathtt{at}(\pi,\; D(\mathtt{ts}) \mapsto D(\mathtt{ts}[-/k])),
\]
dropping arm \texttt{k} from the parent \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} node; the emptiness precondition is operational, invisible at \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}.
As with \Add, outside the parent every node is structurally intact, the parent keeps its ranker and so still reads as the same \ensuremath{D}, and its child list loses slot \texttt{k}; the ancestor retractions touch internal-node classifiers only.
So \(\mathtt{p'} \eqR \psem{\Remove(\tau)}(\mathtt{p})\).

\paragraph{Preservation and non-disruption}

Preservation of \(\vdash\): emptiness and \(\vdash \mathtt{C}\) leave \(\mathtt{C@}\pi.\mathit{pifo}\) with no entry equal to \texttt{k}, so dropping slot \texttt{k} deletes no entry; the ancestor retractions delete no entry either, since they narrow admission for future pushes only. 
Every surviving child keeps its own subtree and its own entries, relabeled \(i \mapsto i-1\) for \(i > k\), so each count still matches.
Ancestors and siblings are unchanged.
Preservation of state: \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_states}} drops slot \texttt{k}, every other entry is unchanged in its shifted position, and no \texttt{init} fires.
Non-disruption: \(\mathtt{drop}_k\) removes only an \emph{empty} slot and renumbers the parent's service map, and the ancestor retractions rewrite classifiers, so together they touch no live \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} entry and \Remove{} is non-disruptive by \cref{lem:nondisruption}; the restriction applies only to future \texttt{push}es.

Deleting an \emph{occupied} child has no canonical local realization.
Consider the tree \texttt{P(Q(A, B), C)} of \cref{fig:remove-orig}, whose subtrees \texttt{A}, \texttt{B}, \texttt{C} are drawn blue, orange, and green.
Here \texttt{Q} refers to \texttt{A} using the index \texttt{0}, and to \texttt{B} using \texttt{1}.
\texttt{P} refers to \texttt{Q} using \texttt{0} and to \texttt{C} using \texttt{1}.
The tree is well-formed.
\texttt{P} appears to be running a round-robin style policy and \texttt{Q} appears to be prioritizing \texttt{B} strictly over \texttt{A}.
Say the user asks us to delete \texttt{B}, dropping its packets.
To restore well-formedness, we need to remove two instances of the index \texttt{1} from \texttt{Q}'s PIFO and two instances of the index \texttt{0} from \texttt{P}'s PIFO.

At \texttt{Q} the edit is unambiguous.
Well-formedness of the original tree forces \texttt{Q}'s PIFO to have exactly two instances of \texttt{1}, and we delete those two.
The trouble is one level up.
\texttt{P}'s PIFO originally had four instances of \texttt{0}, and well-formedness demands that we remove two of them.
But \emph{which} two?
No entry in \texttt{P} carries the meta-information ``I was enqueued when a packet was inserted into \texttt{B}''; an entry \texttt{0} in \texttt{P} only means ``when this index is popped, recursively ask subtree \texttt{Q} to emit the next packet'' (see \cref{sec:hierarchical}).
This absence of meta-information is a \emph{feature} of PIFO trees, not a defect: an entry is deliberately detached from the packet it was enqueued with, which is what lets each node schedule its own discipline in isolation and lets a subtree be reconfigured without rewriting its ancestors.
We could make deletion unambiguous by tagging each entry at \texttt{push} with the leaf it is destined for, but that would discard the abstraction: every internal node would then have to track the entire downstream structure, and the composability that makes PIFO trees scale would be lost.

Given this, there is no obvious or operator-provided answer: whichever two instances of \texttt{0} we drop, the choice is itself a scheduling decision, since it affects how \texttt{P} intermixes \texttt{C} and \texttt{Q} traffic.
Keeping the two leftmost \texttt{0}s (\cref{fig:remove-leftmost}), five pops drain \texttt{P} and yield \texttt{c1, c2, a1, c3, a2}: \texttt{A} is pushed toward the back, its packets emerging only after \texttt{C}'s run is underway.
Keeping the two rightmost \texttt{0}s instead (\cref{fig:remove-rightmost}), the same five pops yield \texttt{a1, c1, a2, c2, c3}: this is exactly the round-robin schedule \texttt{P} was configured for, alternating \texttt{A} and \texttt{C} until \texttt{A} is exhausted and \texttt{C} drains alone.
The \emph{choice of which} \texttt{0}s to drop \emph{silently reschedules \texttt{A} against \texttt{C}}, and nothing in \texttt{p1} or the operator's deletion request records what the operator wanted.

We do not wish to make a scheduling decision for the operator, so our only other option is to reconstruct the tree as if \texttt{B} had never been admitted: drain the tree completely, filter the \texttt{B}-packets, and re-push, recomputing every rank and cursor along the way.
This would be a stop-the-world rebuild.

Our grammar sidesteps the question entirely: by draining \texttt{B} to empty before removing it (\cref{sec:sequences}), every \texttt{pop} that served a \texttt{B}-packet already removed the matching entries from \texttt{Q} and \texttt{P} in the ordinary course of draining.
At the instant \Remove{} fires there is nothing left to reconcile, and the structural deletion is unambiguous.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\Designate(\tau, \mathit{survivor})\)}{Designate}}\label{sec:designate}

\(\tau : \mathit{path}\) is the path to the target subtree being wrapped, and \(\mathit{survivor} : \mathit{pol}\) is the designated-survivor policy that becomes the wrap's low-priority arm.

Say the presently running control is \texttt{C}.
Intuitively, \Designate{} replaces \texttt{C@}\(\tau\) in place with \(\Strictstar(\mathtt{C@}\tau, \lceil \mathit{survivor} \rceil)\).
It is the mechanism behind non-disruptive in-place replacement (\cref{fig:designate}): the wrap lets \ensuremath{\mathit{survivor}} begin accepting new traffic on a low-priority arm while the old subtree keeps draining, untouched, on a high-priority one; once the old subtree is empty, \Undesignate{} (\cref{sec:undesignate}) removes it and promotes \ensuremath{\mathit{survivor}} in its place.
That is why the production exists: no single edit can swap one subtree for another without either dropping the old subtree's backlog or stalling the new arm, but the \(\Strictstar\) wrap lets both coexist for the length of a drain.

\(\Strictstar\) is the same discipline as \(\Strict\), distinguished only by a one-bit \texttt{designated} flag that each node carries: \Designate{} sets the bit, \Undesignate{} clears it.
Semantically the two are identical; every push, pop, and well-formedness check treats \(\Strictstar(A, B)\) exactly as \(\Strict(A, B)\), so the obligations below are stated against the ordinary \(\Strict\) semantics of \cref{sec:policy-dsl}.
The star exists only so that \Undesignate's precondition (``\(\tau\) lands on a \(\Strictstar\)'') can be stated structurally.
The \cref{sec:policy-dsl} DSL in which the operator writes policies can parse only \(\Strict\), never \(\Strictstar\), so a \(\Strictstar\) is unreachable in any user-written \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} and arises only in the middle of a planner sequence, between a \Designate{} and its eventual \Undesignate.
The wrap is therefore \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}-visible only as a \(\Strict\).

\paragraph{Definition}

\(\csem{\Designate(\tau, \mathit{survivor})}(\mathtt{C})\) is defined when \(\tau\) resolves in \texttt{C} and \ensuremath{\mathit{survivor}}'s leaf labels are disjoint from \texttt{C}'s outside \texttt{C@}\(\tau\); overlap with \texttt{C@}\(\tau\)'s own leaves is allowed (see Note).
The figure draws the target subtree in blue and the survivor in orange; below we call them the old and new arms.
Write \(M = \texttt{C@}\tau\) for the target, and let \(P_0\) be the number of packets buffered under it (\(P_0 = 3\) in \cref{fig:designate-before}).
\Designate{} has two parts, both already seen in \Add{} (\cref{sec:add}): locally it wraps the target, \(\mathtt{at}(\tau, \mathtt{wrap})\); along the spine it extends each proper ancestor's transaction by \(\mathtt{admit}(\mathit{z}, \mathtt{flows}(\mathit{survivor}), i)\) toward the on-path child \(i\), admitting \texttt{new}'s flows one step at a time down to \(\tau\).
The transformer \(\mathtt{wrap}\) replaces \(M\) with a fresh \(\Strictstar\) node \(N\) over the two arms \([\,M,\ \lceil\mathit{survivor}\rceil\,]\), giving the old arm strict priority over the new one (\(\mathtt{hi} < \mathtt{lo}\)):
{\small
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
\mathtt{wrap}(M) = N = \langle \mathtt{s}_N,\ \mathtt{q}_N,\ \mathit{z}_N \rangle\big([\,M,\ \lceil\mathit{survivor}\rceil\,]\big),\ \textbf{where}\\[3pt]
\quad \mathtt{s}_N = (\,\mathtt{init\_node\_Strict}(),\\
\quad\qquad\ [\,\mathtt{init\_slot\_Strict}(\mathtt{hi}),\ \mathtt{init\_slot\_Strict}(\mathtt{lo})\,]\,),\\[3pt]
\quad \mathtt{q}_N = \text{the \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} of } P_0 \text{ copies of the entry } (0,\ \mathtt{hi}),\\[3pt]
\quad \mathit{z}_N(p) = (1,\ \mathtt{lo})\ \text{if}\ \mathit{flow}(p) \in \mathtt{flows}(\mathit{survivor}),\ \text{else}\ (0,\ \mathtt{hi}).
\end{array}
\]
}
In the vocabulary of \cref{sec:controls}, \(\mathit{z}_N\) serves \ensuremath{\mathit{survivor}}'s flows at arm \texttt{1} and everything else the node serves at arm \texttt{0}, and admits both.
Its ranker names \(\Strictstar\) rather than \Strict, which is where the \texttt{designated} bit of \cref{sec:undesignate} lives.
Arm \texttt{0} is \(M\) (\texttt{old}), unchanged down to its whole subtree and buffered packets; arm \texttt{1} is the freshly compiled \(\lceil\mathit{survivor}\rceil\) (\texttt{new}), initially empty.
This is \cref{fig:designate-after}: \(N\)'s index-\ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} holds \(P_0\) entries for arm \texttt{0} and none for arm \texttt{1}, one per packet buffered under \texttt{old}, so that backlog keeps popping through the wrap ahead of any later arrival, unchanged (see \(\vdash\) below), while \texttt{new} fills only as fresh traffic arrives (\cref{fig:designate-drain}).
Because \(\mathtt{at}\) writes \(N\) back into the parent's slot \texttt{k}, that slot's inherited per-arm metadata is unchanged, so the wrap is transparent to the parent.
If \(\tau\) is empty the whole control becomes \(N\), with the old root as arm \texttt{0}.

\paragraph{Note: Overlap between the old subtree and \ensuremath{\mathit{survivor}}}

Overlap of leaf labels between \texttt{C@}\(\tau\) and \ensuremath{\mathit{survivor}} is explicitly permitted.
The moment \(\delta\) fires becomes the boundary between old traffic (which continues to drain from arm 0, \texttt{C@}\(\tau\)) and new traffic (which arm 1 admits): from this instant on, an arriving packet whose label appears in \(\lceil \mathit{survivor} \rceil\) is routed by \(N.\mathit{z}\) to slot 1, while the residual packets already under arm 0 drain on their existing \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} entries.
The two arms thus stay operationally disjoint even when their label sets overlap, which is the very property that lets \Designate{} express in-place subtree replacement (\cref{sec:sequences}).

\paragraph{Characterization}

{\small
\[
\psem{\Designate(\tau, \mathit{survivor})} = \mathtt{at}(\tau,\; M \mapsto \Strict(M, \mathit{survivor})),
\]
}
wrapping the \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} subtree at \(\tau\); \(\psem{\cdot}\) yields \(\Strict\), not \(\Strictstar\) (the designated bit is \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}-invisible).
As with \Add, outside the wrap every node is structurally intact, arm 1 is \(\lceil \mathit{survivor} \rceil\), and the ancestor \ensuremath{\mathit{z}}-extensions touch only internal-node classifiers, so \(\mathtt{p'} \eqR \psem{\Designate(\tau, \mathit{survivor})}(\mathtt{p})\).
The fresh wrapper \(N\) runs \(\Strictstar\), the one discipline \cref{sec:equivalences} did not have in view when it defined \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\).
Its ranker names \(\Strictstar\), and \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) reports it as \Strict, since the designated bit is \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}-invisible.
That is what makes \(\mathtt{p'}\) match \(\psem{\cdot}\) above.

\paragraph{Preservation and non-disruption}

Preservation of \(\vdash\): at \(N\), the \(P_0\) slot-\texttt{0} entries match the packet count under arm 0, and arm 1 holds no packet and no slot-\texttt{1} entry; arm 0 is unchanged; at \texttt{C@}\(\pi\), \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} is unchanged and slot \texttt{k}'s count \(P_0\) matches what \texttt{C@}\(\tau\) carried.
Preservation of state: arm 0 unchanged, arm 1 is \(\lceil \mathit{survivor} \rceil\), \(N\)'s state comes from the \texttt{init} calls above, everything else unchanged.
Non-disruption takes a real argument here, since \(\mathtt{wrap}\) inserts the live node \(N\) with a non-empty \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}}, so \cref{lem:nondisruption} does not apply directly.
Every in-flight packet lies under arm 0, which is \texttt{C@}\(\tau\) carried over unchanged, so its own entries are untouched.
If \(P_0 > 0\), every entry in \(N.\mathit{pifo}\) is slot \texttt{0}, so any \texttt{pop} reaching \(N\) descends into arm 0 and returns exactly what it would have before; if \(P_0 = 0\), \(\vdash \mathtt{C}\) means no \texttt{pop} ever reaches \(N\).
Arm 1 sees traffic only on a future \texttt{push}, so no buffered packet is dropped, reordered, or re-ranked, and \Designate{} is non-disruptive.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\Undesignate(\tau)\)}{Undesignate}}\label{sec:undesignate}

\(\tau : \mathit{path}\) targets the \(\Strictstar\) node to unwrap.
\Undesignate{} is the structural inverse of \Designate: it drops a \(\Strictstar(A, B)\) whose \(A\)-arm is empty, leaving \(B\) (the last step of \cref{fig:designate}).

\paragraph{Definition}

\[
\csem{\Undesignate(\tau)} = \mathtt{at}(\tau,\, \mathtt{unwrap}),
\]
defined when \texttt{C@}\(\tau\) is a \(\Strictstar\) (it carries the \texttt{designated} bit, so an earlier \Designate{} made it) and its arm 0 is empty.
\(\mathtt{unwrap}\) returns arm 1 of the node, \(B\), with its subtree and contents unchanged; the node \(N = \texttt{C@}\tau\) and its empty arm-0 stub are discarded.
Since \(\mathtt{at}\) writes \(B\) back into the parent's slot \texttt{k}, that slot keeps its inherited per-arm metadata and \(B\) now hangs directly where \(N\) did.

\paragraph{Characterization}

\[
\psem{\Undesignate(\tau)} = \mathtt{at}(\tau,\; \Strict(A, B) \mapsto B),
\]
collapsing the wrapper (which reads as \(\Strict\) at \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}-level); the empty-arm-0 precondition is operational.
As with \Remove, nothing structural outside \(N\) moves and the wrapper is dropped, so \(\mathtt{p'} \eqR \psem{\Undesignate(\tau)}(\mathtt{p})\).

\paragraph{Preservation and non-disruption}

Preservation of \(\vdash\): with arm 0 empty, \(N.\mathit{pifo}\) carried \(|B|\) slot-\texttt{1} entries, and \(\mathtt{C@}\pi.\mathit{pifo}\)'s slot-\texttt{k} entries carried the same count one level up; after the unwrap those \texttt{k}-entries route straight to \(B\), whose count is still \(|B|\), so \texttt{C@}\(\pi\) reads \(|B| = |B|\) and \(B\) reads as before.
Preservation of state: unchanged outside \(N\); \(N\)'s state vanishes.
Non-disruption: the only entries \Undesignate{} drops are \(N\)'s slot-\texttt{1} entries, and with arm 0 empty every \texttt{pop} reaching \(N\) was already forced to slot \texttt{1} \(\to B\); eliding \(N\) removes that redundant hop and returns the same packet, and no entry inside \(B\) moves, so \Undesignate{} is non-disruptive.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\ChangeRoot(\nu)\)}{ChangeRoot}}\label{sec:changeroot}

\(\nu : \mathit{path}\) is the subtree promoted to the new root; \ChangeRoot{} discards every ancestor above \texttt{C@}\(\nu\).
It is the only production that erases structural ancestors; the mechanics are short but the Notes below are substantive.

\paragraph{Definition}

\[
\csem{\ChangeRoot(\nu)}(\mathtt{C}) = \texttt{C@}\nu,
\]
defined when \(\nu\) is non-empty, resolves in \texttt{C}, and every node strictly above \texttt{C@}\(\nu\) is \emph{unary} with its sole arm continuing toward \texttt{C@}\(\nu\) (so the discarded chain carries no off-path traffic).
\texttt{C@}\(\nu\) and its subtree are kept unchanged; every proper ancestor's \((\mathit{state}, \mathit{pifo}, \mathit{z})\) is discarded.

\paragraph{Characterization}

\[
\psem{\ChangeRoot(\nu)}\,\mathtt{p} = \mathtt{p@}\nu.
\]
\(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) is a structural recursion, so it commutes with subtree projection: \(\lfloor \texttt{C@}\nu \rfloor = \mathtt{p} \mathbin{@} \nu\), giving \(\mathtt{p'} \eqR \psem{\ChangeRoot(\nu)}(\mathtt{p})\).

\paragraph{Preservation and non-disruption}

Preservation of \(\vdash\): \(\vdash \mathtt{C}\) is a conjunction of per-node obligations, and those on \texttt{C@}\(\nu\)'s nodes are exactly \(\vdash \texttt{C@}\nu = \vdash \mathtt{C'}\); the discarded ancestors' obligations are forgotten.
Preservation of state: unchanged within \texttt{C@}\(\nu\); ancestor state is gone.
Non-disruption: every in-flight packet sits in a \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} within \texttt{C@}\(\nu\) (the discarded ancestor pifos held routing entries, not packets).
Those entries are redundant: a \texttt{pop} before \(\delta\) descends the unary chain, and each ancestor pifo has the single slot \texttt{0}, so every such pop is forced to slot \texttt{0} and lands at \texttt{C@}\(\nu\); a \texttt{pop} after \(\delta\) acts on \texttt{C@}\(\nu\) directly and returns the same packet.
So \ChangeRoot{} is non-disruptive.

\paragraph{Notes}

\emph{Why the unary precondition.}
The precondition rules out, by construction, any silently-dropped packet-bearing siblings.
A non-unary ancestor would have arms branching off the path to \texttt{C@}\(\nu\); those subtrees would vanish with their ancestor, taking their packets with them.
Allowing this would make \ChangeRoot{} a structural deletion of arbitrarily many off-path subtrees masquerading as a root change, with no semantic story for those packets.
The richer reconfiguration of pruning to a subtree whose ancestor chain branches off into packet-bearing relatives is realized as the \PruneDownTo{} idiom (\cref{sec:idioms}), which first drains and \Remove{}s those siblings in sequence, reducing the chain above \texttt{C@}\(\nu\) to the unary shape that \ChangeRoot{} then accepts.

\emph{What the chain carries, and what is discarded.}
An internal node's \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}}, rank function, and \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} ordering exist to pick among siblings.
At a unary node the sibling-picking role degenerates: with one arm, every pop is forced into it.

What can remain active at a unary node is rank computation that depends on per-packet attributes rather than on sibling identity.
If a chain's disciplines do no such per-packet reordering, each unary node along the chain is operationally a passthrough, and the immediate-pop observation-preservation above extends to every subsequent pop and push.
If they do (LSTF~\cite{Mittal16} is a canonical example, with rank determined by each packet's deadline), the chain is actively shaping traffic, and \ChangeRoot{} removes that shaping.
That is precisely the production's intended role: the atomic step by which the operator says ``promote \(\mathtt{p1@}\nu\) to the root and discard the ancestor shaping above it.''
The \PruneDownTo{} idiom packages this with the upstream draining and \Remove{}s that make the chain unary in the first place, so the operator's request (``prune to this subtree'') is realized as a sequence whose final step discards exactly the ancestor influence that the operator has chosen to abandon.

\emph{Pol-level effect.}
The \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} changes from the unary chain wrapping \(\mathtt{p1@}\nu\) (e.g., \(\Strict(\mathtt{p1@}\nu)\) or \(\mathtt{LSTF}(\mathtt{p1@}\nu)\)) to just \(\mathtt{p1@}\nu\).
This is \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}-visible: the root discipline observably changes, which is the substantive content of the production.
Whether that pol-visible change reorders in-flight traffic depends on whether the discarded chain was doing per-packet reordering (see previous Note).

\section{The Interplay of \(\psem{\delta}\) and \(\csem{\delta}\)}\label{sec:interplay}

The preceding sections introduced all the moving parts: the runtime object \texttt{C} (\cref{sec:runtime-model}), the source \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} together with the compilation \(\lceil \cdot \rceil\) and the reading-off \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) (\cref{sec:grammar}), the atomic-diff grammar \(\delta\), and its two readings \(\psem{\delta}\) and \(\csem{\delta}\).
This section states formally how they fit together.
The interplay is what lets the runtime, at every step, echo to the operator the \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} that the live control realizes.

% read-offs (dotted, arced, up)
\draw[->,dotted] (c1)  to[bend right=45] node[pos=0.5,right]{$\lfloor\cdot\rfloor$} (p1p);
\draw[->,dotted] (c2p) to[bend right=45] node[pos=0.5,right]{$\lfloor\cdot\rfloor$} (p2p);
% the two readings
\draw[->] (p1p) -- node[above]{$\psem{\delta}$} (p2);
\draw[->] (c1p) -- node[above]{$\csem{\delta}$} (c2p);

The operator writes \texttt{p1}, which is compiled into control \(\mathtt{C1} := \lceil \mathtt{p1} \rceil\).
Recall that \(\lfloor \mathtt{C1} \rfloor \eqR \mathtt{p1}\) by compiler correctness (\cref{sec:equivalences}).
We echo \(\mathtt{p1'} := \lfloor \mathtt{C1} \rfloor\) back to the user.
\texttt{p1'} is important because it faithfully represents to the user the arm ordering that the compiler chose.
Push and pop operations then carry \texttt{C1} to \texttt{C1'}.
We know that \(\mathtt{C1} \sim \mathtt{C1'}\), since \texttt{push} and \texttt{pop} changed only live contents, and \(\lfloor \mathtt{C1'} \rfloor = \lfloor \mathtt{C1} \rfloor\) by insensitivity to live contents, so the live \texttt{C1'} still realizes both \texttt{p1} and \texttt{p1'}.

The operator writes \texttt{p2}, and the \emph{transition planner} (\cref{sec:planner}), the tool that infers atomic edits between two \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}s, produces a \(\delta\) such that \(\psem{\delta}(\mathtt{p1'}) \eqR \mathtt{p2}\).
The \(\delta\) is expressed with paths into the running policy \texttt{p1'}, so it is \texttt{p1'} that \(\psem{\delta}\) transforms, not the operator's original \texttt{p1} (whose arm order may differ).
Applying \(\csem{\delta}\) to \texttt{C1'} brings us to control \texttt{C2'}.
By closure under \(\delta\) (\cref{thm:interplay} below) we have \(\lfloor \mathtt{C2'} \rfloor \eqR \psem{\delta}(\mathtt{p1'})\).
Chaining this with the previously established \(\psem{\delta}(\mathtt{p1'}) \eqR \mathtt{p2}\) gives \(\lfloor \mathtt{C2'} \rfloor \eqR \mathtt{p2}\).
We again echo \(\mathtt{p2'} := \lfloor \mathtt{C2'} \rfloor\) to the user.

The transformation is complete at this point, but we need to ground ourselves.
\(\mathtt{C2} := \lceil \mathtt{p2} \rceil\) is the control that the state-of-the-art would have built, since all it can do is compile a new control from scratch.
\emph{We} do not actually construct \texttt{C2}, but it is the correct reference point and it is crucial that we now relate \texttt{C2'} (which we have just produced) to \texttt{C2} (which the state-of-the-art would have produced).

By compiler correctness, \(\lfloor \mathtt{C2} \rfloor \eqR \mathtt{p2}\), hence \(\lfloor \mathtt{C2'} \rfloor \eqR \lfloor \mathtt{C2} \rfloor\).
However, we would like to relate the \emph{controls} directly, not just relate their \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}-level projections via \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\).
The control-level relation we can write is \(\mathtt{C2'} \simR \mathtt{C2}\), which absorbs two gaps at once.
\texttt{C2'} carries the live \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}}/\ensuremath{\mathit{state}} accumulated since \texttt{C1}, while \texttt{C2} is freshly compiled and bare (the \(\sim\) component covers this); and \(\csem{\delta}\) and \(\lceil \cdot \rceil\) are free to pick different sibling orders at internal nodes, so \texttt{C2'} and \texttt{C2} may also differ in child arrangements (the R-closure covers this).
The formal statement follows.

\begin{theorem}[Interplay of \(\psem{\delta}\) and \(\csem{\delta}\)]\label{thm:interplay}
Let \texttt{C} be a well-formed control, and let \(\delta\) be a production of the grammar (\cref{sec:delta-grammar}) such that \(\csem{\delta}(\mathtt{C})\) is defined.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\csem{\delta}(\mathtt{C})\) is well-formed.
\item \(\lfloor \csem{\delta}(\mathtt{C}) \rfloor \eqR \psem{\delta}(\lfloor \mathtt{C} \rfloor)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Each clause is an aggregation of per-production obligations already discharged in \cref{sec:per-production}.
Clause 1 is exactly \emph{Preservation of \(\vdash\)}; clause 2 is exactly \emph{Characterization}.
Clause 2 has content because \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) is fixed in advance, by structural recursion on the control (\cref{sec:equivalences}), rather than extended along \(\delta\).
Each production's Characterization must show that the recursion, rerun on the edited control, lands where \(\psem{\delta}\) says it should.
\end{proof}

The diagram above is the unit cell of a fuller picture: a real reconfiguration is a \emph{sequence} of edits, not one \(\delta\), and \cref{sec:idioms} lines up copies of this cell end to end, each intermediate control carrying its own \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) meaning.

\paragraph{A worked example}

The operator requests \texttt{p1 = Strict(hi: zoom, lo: gmail)}.
The compiler uses its degree of freedom to yield control \texttt{C1} such that \(\lfloor \mathtt{C1} \rfloor = \mathtt{Strict(lo: gmail, hi: zoom)}\); the children have been reordered for some compiler-internal reason.
We echo back
\[
\mathtt{p1'} := \lfloor \mathtt{C1} \rfloor = \mathtt{Strict(lo: gmail, hi: zoom)}.
\]
We know that \(\mathtt{p1} \eqR \mathtt{p1'}\), so the scheduler that the operator gets is still the one they asked for; only the slot numbering differs.
As the control serves pushes and pops, \texttt{C1} transforms into \texttt{C1'}: the same tree with its \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}}s and \ensuremath{\mathit{state}}s advanced, so it is still the case that \(\mathtt{C1} \sim \mathtt{C1'}\) and \(\lfloor \mathtt{C1'} \rfloor = \mathtt{p1'}\).

Later, the operator requests \texttt{p2 = Strict(lo: gmail, mid: spotify, hi: zoom)}.
The runtime can again use its freedom: instead of literally splicing \texttt{spotify} in between running arms \texttt{gmail} and \texttt{zoom}, it chooses to append \texttt{spotify} to the end.
This converts the running control \texttt{C1'} into control \texttt{C2'} such that
\[
\lfloor \mathtt{C2'} \rfloor = \mathtt{Strict(lo: gmail, hi: zoom, mid: spotify)},
\]
and we echo \(\mathtt{p2'} := \lfloor \mathtt{C2'} \rfloor\) back to the user.

This is \cref{thm:interplay} in miniature: the operator wrote \texttt{p1} then \texttt{p2}; the runtime, exercising its arm-order freedom, produced \texttt{C1'} then \texttt{C2'}; and at each step we read off and echoed the running representative (\texttt{p1'}, then \texttt{p2'}), with \(\lfloor \mathtt{C2'} \rfloor \eqR \mathtt{p2}\), so the operator's request is faithfully realized despite the different slot order.

\subsection{Preserving this proof down to hardware}\label{sec:hw-preservation}

\cref{sec:per-production} proves soundness at the \(\delta\) level, where each production (\Add, \Quiesce, \Remove, \ldots) carries a well-formed tree to a well-formed tree.
To run on hardware, each edit is lowered, by a simple and mechanical compilation, into a sequence of fine-grained instructions in our IR.
The IR alphabet and the substrate machinery are the subject of \cref{sec:hardware}; here we only need that the IR has fine-grained verbs (we will name \texttt{spawn}, \texttt{adopt}, \texttt{emancipate}, \texttt{assoc}, \texttt{deassoc}, and the like) and that a single such verb, unlike a whole production of \(\delta\), \emph{can} leave the tree malformed: a node freshly created by \texttt{spawn} has not yet been wired to its parent by \texttt{adopt}, for example.

We do not prove soundness at the IR level, but instead informally make the case for why the \cref{sec:per-production} proof survives the lowering.
There are two reasons.
First, the compilation is \emph{faithful}: each production of \(\delta\) expands to a fixed instruction sequence that, when run to completion, realizes exactly that production's effect.
We give the translation from each \(\delta\) production to its IR instruction sequence and take its faithfulness to be uncontroversial.
Second, our substrate runs each such sequence as a single \emph{transactional commit}: a group of IR instructions, fenced by the commit's open and close, that installs as one instant from the user's perspective, with no \texttt{push} or \texttt{pop} interleaved between them (\S7.1' spells out the substrate machinery that makes this so).
The transiently-malformed intermediate trees are therefore never observed by the network.
That commit is precisely how the substrate \emph{realizes} the atomicity property of \cref{sec:grammar}: atomicity asked for an instantaneous control replacement between two user operations, and the commit collapses a multi-instruction lowering into one such instant.
Every \texttt{push}/\texttt{pop} therefore still lands on a well-formed control (\texttt{p1}, some transitionary policy, or \texttt{p2}), exactly as \cref{sec:per-production} proved; the IR's transient malformedness lives entirely inside commits, invisible to the user.

The same argument carries from the IR down to hardware: the hardware executes a committed sequence atomically with respect to user operations, so what it exhibits is again what \cref{sec:per-production} proved.
The compilation itself, and the substrate machinery that makes a commit atomic, are the subject of \cref{sec:hardware}.

\section{Realizing Reconfigurations as Guarded Sequences}\label{sec:sequences}

This section composes the productions of \(\delta\) (\cref{sec:grammar}) into \emph{guarded sequences} \((\varphi ; \delta)^{*}\), where a guard \(\varphi\) is a predicate on the state of the live control.
Guarded sequences realize changes to the live control that no single \(\delta\) can express.
We first show that the transitionary period such a sequence passes through needs no new semantics (\cref{sec:links}), then give the grammar of guarded sequences and the idioms operators build from them (\cref{sec:idioms}).

\subsection{Transitionary Policies and Their Semantics}\label{sec:links}

A guarded sequence threads the live control through a chain of intermediate controls.
We call each such intermediate control a \emph{transitionary policy}, writing \(\mathtt{link}_i\) for the control on which the \texttt{i}-th \(\delta\) (zero-indexed) fires; \(\mathtt{link}_0 = \mathtt{C}\) is the starting control, and \(\mathtt{link}_{i+1} = \csem{\delta_i}(\mathtt{link}_i)\) is what the \texttt{i}-th \(\delta\) leaves behind (\cref{sec:idioms} makes this precise).

The main observation of the section, developed in \cref{sec:idioms}, is that each transitionary policy is itself an ordinary \cref{sec:runtime-model}-style control, so the ``transitionary period'' \emph{needs no new semantics}: this is Obligation 1 of \cref{sec:intro}, discharged locally by the per-production obligations of \cref{sec:per-production}.
Moreover, since every production of \cref{sec:grammar} is \emph{pol-explainable} (each \(\delta\) has a \(\psem{\delta}\) that tracks its pol-level effect), we can echo to the operator, at every step of the sequence, the \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} that the live control actually realizes.
The chain of \(\psem{\cdot}\)s starting from \(\lfloor \mathtt{C} \rfloor\) gives \(\lfloor \mathtt{link}_i \rfloor\) for each \texttt{i}, so the operator is never in the dark about what policy is running.

\paragraph{Follow-up requests}
The transition planner engages with one reconfiguration at a time.
If the operator submits a follow-up \texttt{p3} while a \(\mathtt{p1} \to \mathtt{p2}\) sequence is still mid-flight (some guard \(\varphi_i\) has not yet become true), we queue \texttt{p3}, and the planner does not begin work on it until the in-flight sequence completes and the live control \(C_z\) satisfies \(\lfloor C_z \rfloor \eqR \mathtt{p2}\).
At that instant the planner pulls \texttt{p3} from the queue, treats \(\lfloor C_z \rfloor\) as the new starting point, and produces a fresh sequence to reach \texttt{p3}.
A more aggressive strategy is possible: splicing or canceling the in-flight sequence to converge on \texttt{p3} directly, sometimes at lower total cost than running \(\mathtt{p1} \to \mathtt{p2} \to \mathtt{p3}\) in series.
We do not pursue this here; the splicing analysis introduces its own correctness obligations (atomicity of the splice, what transitionary policy the operator observes between the splice and \texttt{p3}) that are out of scope of this paper.

\subsection{Guarded Sequences and Their Idioms}\label{sec:idioms}

\paragraph{Grammar}

We write \texttt{gseq} for a guarded sequence (\cref{fig:gseq-idioms}, top).
A guard \(\varphi\) is either trivially satisfied (\texttt{true}) or asserts that the subtree at \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} is empty; it is always paired with exactly one \(\delta\) drawn from \cref{sec:grammar}.
Those two guard forms have sufficed for every sequence we have needed; the framework does not depend on this minimality, and a richer predicate language can be slotted in without disturbing anything else.

\paragraph{Intuition}

The hardware substrate (\cref{sec:hardware}) does two things beyond ordinary \texttt{push}/\texttt{pop}: it waits for a guard \(\varphi\) to become true on the live control, and it fires a single \(\delta\) as an atomic replacement.
A guarded sequence chains these: wait for \(\varphi_0\), fire \(\delta_0\), then wait for \(\varphi_1\) (now evaluated on the post-\(\delta_0\) control), fire \(\delta_1\), and so on.
Each intermediate control between two consecutive \(\delta\) firings is an ordinary \cref{sec:runtime-model} control, so the ``transitionary period'' has no new semantics; safety is entirely local, following from the per-production obligations of \cref{sec:per-production}.

\paragraph{Reaching the target}

Safety guarantees that every intermediate control is well-formed, but not that the sequence \emph{arrives} at the intended \texttt{p2}.
We check arrival at the pol level, folding each production's \(\psem{\delta_i}\) (\cref{sec:per-production}) along the sequence: starting from \(\mathtt{ip}_0 = \lfloor \mathtt{C} \rfloor\), the pol we echoed to the operator, set \(\mathtt{ip}_{i+1} := \psem{\delta_i}(\mathtt{ip}_i)\).
The sequence is admissible iff every \(\psem{\delta_i}\) is defined on \(\mathtt{ip}_i\) and the final \(\mathtt{ip}_{n+1} \eqR \mathtt{p2}\); that is, it actually carries \(\lfloor \mathtt{C} \rfloor\) to \texttt{p2}.
This is the Interplay theorem (\cref{sec:interplay}) iterated across the sequence: each \(\psem{\delta_i}\) matches its operational counterpart \(\csem{\delta_i}\) (\cref{sec:per-production}), so a fold that lands at \(\mathtt{ip}_{n+1} \eqR \mathtt{p2}\) certifies, cell-by-cell, that the operational chain ends in a control whose \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) is \texttt{p2}.
Guards play no role in this check: they govern \emph{when} each \(\delta_i\) fires on the live control and are pol-invisible (\cref{sec:per-production}), so a \((\mathtt{empty}(\mathit{path}); \Remove(\mathit{path}))\) step folds exactly as if its guard were \texttt{true}.
If the chain fails to reach \texttt{p2}, or some \(\psem{\delta_i}\) is undefined on its \(\mathtt{ip}_i\), the sequence is rejected, and the rejection identifies the offending step: the first \(\delta_i\) whose \(\psem{\delta_i}\) was undefined, or else the final mismatch \(\mathtt{ip}_{n+1} \not\eqR \mathtt{p2}\).
Both clients of the framework rely on this check: the planner (\cref{sec:planner}) uses it as a backstop on the sequences it assembles, and an operator authoring by hand (\cref{sec:imperative}) uses it to validate theirs.

\paragraph{Liveness}

Safety is unconditional; liveness is not, and cannot be.
A guarded sequence progresses only as its guards become true, and a guard like \(\mathtt{empty}(\mathit{path})\) becomes true only once the traffic under \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} drains, which is up to the network, not up to us.
A persistent or adversarial flow can keep a subtree busy indefinitely, and then any step waiting on that subtree's emptiness never fires.
This is inherent, not an engineering gap: draining a subtree gracefully means letting its buffered packets leave on their own, and no reconfiguration can hurry that along without dropping them, which the grammar refuses to do.
So completing a sequence takes the operator's cooperation, for instance quiescing a flow at its source or accepting a harder reconfiguration that resets more of the tree but drops nothing.
The live control stays safe throughout; it simply may not reach the target until the traffic allows.
Follow-up requests submitted during a stalled sequence are queued (\cref{sec:links}).

\paragraph{Idioms}

\emph{Idioms} are a named vocabulary for frequently-used guarded sequences, whether the planner assembles them or an operator writes them by hand.

An idiom is a macro over the grammar \(\delta\) (and, recursively, over other idioms).
It expands into a fixed \((\varphi; \delta)^{*}\) sequence: a list of productions with the guards between them spelling out what the system waits for.
Soundness is compositional: each step of the expansion is sound by \cref{sec:per-production}, and the sequence inherits the sequence-level reasoning above.
An idiom expansion that would hit an undefined production on the current control is rejected: the soundness checks fire on the expanded sequence just as they would on a hand-written one.

We name four starter idioms, with their expansions collected in \cref{fig:gseq-idioms}.
New ones can be added later without changing the framework, since an idiom is just a named \((\varphi; \delta)^{*}\).

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\Retire(\ensuremath{\mathit{path}})} quiesces the subtree at \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}, waits for it to drain to empty, then \Remove{}s it.
This is the operator-facing way to say ``tear this subtree down gracefully.''

\item \textbf{\SlowRetire(\ensuremath{\mathit{path}})} waits for the subtree at \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} to drain, then \Remove{}s it.
The operator may use this if the subtree at \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} needs to receive more traffic but is generally being phased out.

\item \textbf{\Replace(\ensuremath{\mathit{path}}, \texttt{B})} phases \texttt{B} in as the survivor of \(\mathit{pol}@\mathit{path}\): it \Designate{}s \texttt{B}, quiesces and drains the original subtree, then removes it and the \(\Strictstar\) wrapper, leaving \texttt{B} alone at \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} (\cref{fig:gseq-idioms}).
At the pol level the effect is wholesale replacement of \(\mathit{pol}@\mathit{path}\) by \texttt{B}.
Its footprint is the subtree at \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}, the wrap node \Designate{} inserts, and the \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} chain at the ancestors of \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}; \cref{sec:planner} frames confinement against exactly this set.

\item \textbf{\PruneDownTo(\ensuremath{\mathit{path}})} retires every off-path subtree along the route from the root to \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} (call them \(\pi_a, \ldots, \pi_z\)), then \ChangeRoot{}s to \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}.
This idiom is the operator-facing way to say ``abandon everything except this subtree.''

Each \Retire{} removes one off-path subtree; once they have all fired, every ancestor along the path is unary, satisfying \ChangeRoot's precondition (\cref{sec:changeroot}).
The \Retire{}s on disjoint off-path subtrees commute, so any ordering suffices; we use root-first as a stable convention.

For instance, suppose the running tree is \texttt{A(B(D(F, G), E), C)}, with edges labeled by the parent-side index:

The operator wants to retain only \texttt{E}, so they issue \PruneDownTo(\texttt{[0, 1]}).
The off-path subtrees are \texttt{D(F, G)} (at \texttt{[0, 0]}) and \texttt{C} (at \texttt{[1]}).
So \PruneDownTo(\texttt{[0, 1]}) expands to: \Retire(\texttt{[0, 0]) ; Retire([1]) ; (true; ChangeRoot([0, 0])}).

Note that the path \texttt{[0, 0]} appears twice in this expansion with different referents: in \Retire(\texttt{[0, 0]}) it points to \texttt{D(F, G)} (the operand at the moment that \Retire{} fires), and in the final \ChangeRoot(\texttt{[0, 0]}) it points to \texttt{E}, which moved from \texttt{[0, 1]} to \texttt{[0, 0]} once \texttt{D(F, G)} was retired.

\emph{On the natural-feeling opposite.}
\PruneDownTo{} keeps one subtree and abandons everything around it. The opposite move would be to keep the running tree and splice fresh structure \emph{above and around} it (with the running root becoming a child of a newly-introduced context).
We do not support this, as either a production or an idiom.
The issue is that any fresh ancestor would need a \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}}, per-arm \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_state}}s, a populated index \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}}, and a \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} whose history matches the live traffic already flowing through the subtree it now wraps, and there is no honest way to synthesize the state it would have carried had it been present all along.
\end{itemize}

\section{The Transition Planner}\label{sec:planner}

The operator hands the runtime \texttt{p1} (the presently-running policy) and \texttt{p2} (their new desire); the transition planner's job is to propose a \((\varphi ; \delta)^{*}\) sequence whose pol-level fold takes \texttt{p1} to \texttt{p2} up to \(\eqR\).
The operator accepts or rejects the proposal; they do not author it directly.

Every sequence the planner emits is safe by the per-production guarantees of \cref{sec:per-production}, aggregated across the sequence as sketched in \cref{sec:idioms}, regardless of how the planner assembled it.
The planner aims to land at \texttt{p2} by construction: each case below emits a \(\delta\) or idiom whose \(\psem{\cdot}\) realizes the locally-observed pol-level difference.
A misstep in the case analysis could in principle yield a sequence whose fold \(\psem{\delta_n} \circ \ldots \circ \psem{\delta_1} (\mathtt{p1})\) misses \texttt{p2}'s \(\eqR\)-class; the pol-level check of \cref{sec:idioms} is the backstop and rejects any such sequence before commit.
Optimality is a separate matter, and not guaranteed: the planner may fail to see a superior sequence that the user has in mind.

\cref{sec:fallback} sets up \emph{confinement} as a metric for excellence, presents the always-available worst-case \Replace{} fallback, and frames the planner's strategy as descending the tree to find a tight production (Add, Remove, ChangeMeta) that captures the difference locally.
\cref{sec:planner-emits} catalogs the cases the planner recognizes and emits a tight sequence for.
\cref{sec:imperative} shows how an operator can instead author a sequence by hand, admitted by the same pol-level check.
\cref{sec:planner-nonfeatures} lists deliberate non-features and candidate future work.

\subsection{The Always-Available Fallback, and Confinement to do Better}\label{sec:fallback}

To reach any \texttt{p2} from any \texttt{p1}, the planner can issue \(\Replace([], \mathtt{p2})\), the \cref{sec:idioms} idiom that expands to

\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
(\mathtt{true};\ \Designate([], \mathtt{p2}))\ ;\ (\mathtt{true};\ \Quiesce([0]))\ ;\\[3pt]
\qquad (\mathtt{empty}([0]);\ \Undesignate([]))
\end{array}
\]
This wraps \texttt{p1} and \texttt{p2} into \(\Strictstar(\mathtt{hi}: \mathtt{p1}, \mathtt{lo}: \mathtt{p2})\) at the root (so pops still drain \texttt{p1} while pushes go to \texttt{p2}), and, once \texttt{p1} is empty, collapses the wrapper to leave \texttt{p2} alone.
Nothing is dropped and the sequence is safe by \cref{sec:per-production}, but \emph{no part of the scheduler is left undisturbed}.

\paragraph{Footprint}

To measure how much of the tree a sequence touches, associate each production \(\delta\) with a \emph{footprint} on the live control it acts on.
Define \(\mathtt{footprint}(\delta, \mathtt{C})\) as the set of nodes of \texttt{C} whose \ensuremath{\mathit{state}}, \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}}, or \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} differs between \texttt{C} and \(\csem{\delta}(\mathtt{C})\).
The set is read off the per-production Preservation paragraphs of \cref{sec:per-production}: it includes the edit site, the new or removed node where applicable, and every proper ancestor whose \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} is extended or restricted (e.g., the ancestors touched by \Add, \Quiesce, \Remove, \Designate).
For a guarded sequence \((\varphi_0 ; \delta_0) ; \ldots ; (\varphi_n ; \delta_n)\), the sequence-level footprint is \(\bigcup_i \mathtt{footprint}(\delta_i, \mathtt{link}_i)\).
The root-level \(\Replace([], \mathtt{p2})\) above has the maximum possible footprint: the whole tree.

\paragraph{The planner's strategy}

The metric by which we judge the planner is \emph{confinement}: a good plan disturbs as little of the running scheduler as possible, leaving parts of the tree that did not need to change running undisturbed.
A sequence is well-confined when its footprint is small relative to the symmetric pol-level difference between \texttt{p1} and \texttt{p2}.

The planner's goal is to recognize whether the difference between \texttt{p1} and \texttt{p2} is captured by a tight production from \cref{sec:delta-grammar} (an \Add, \Remove, or \ChangeMeta) and to emit that production wherever in the tree it sits, leaving everything else untouched.
The planner descends through the tree as long as \texttt{p1} and \texttt{p2} agree at the current level, looking for a recognized shape one level down.
When it finds one, it emits the matching production at that depth.
Only when the descent ends without a match does the planner fall back to \Replace.
But the good news is that, by then, the recursion has localized the difference to the deepest slot at which the policies disagree, so the resulting \Replace{} wraps only that slot.

An example will help.
Suppose
\[
\mathtt{p1} = \mathtt{P}(\mathtt{zoom}, \mathtt{Q}(\mathtt{gmail}, \mathtt{netflix}))
\]
for some scheduling disciplines \(\mathtt{P}\) and \(\mathtt{Q}\), and the operator requests
\[
\mathtt{p2} = \mathtt{P}(\mathtt{zoom}, \mathtt{Q}(\mathtt{gmail}, \mathtt{netflix}, \mathtt{spotify})).
\]
The planner has two ways to realize this, shown in \cref{fig:planner-add-vs-replace}.

\subsection{The Planner's Case Analysis}\label{sec:planner-emits}

The planner has two parts: a top-level pass over \((\mathtt{p1}, \mathtt{p2})\) as whole trees, and a \emph{per-level comparator} it falls through to.
Whatever the comparator emits is lifted back to the right depth by prepending the slot indices the recursion descended through.

\paragraph{The top-level pass}

The pass tries three cases in order and stops at the first match.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\mathtt{p1} = \mathtt{p2}\). Emit \texttt{[]}.
The test is literal equality on the normalized \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} terms.
The compiler (\cref{sec:policy-dsl}) runs every operator-supplied policy through \texttt{Pol.normalize}, which rewrites a \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} to a canonical representative of its \(\eqR\)-class (a fixed sibling ordering), before reaching the planner, so two \(\eqR\)-equivalent policies arrive here as identical terms.
The live control is already serving the requested policy and there is nothing to install.

\item \texttt{p2} embeds in \texttt{p1} at \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}. Emit \(\PruneDownTo(\mathit{path})\).
A structural sub-policy test walks \texttt{p1} looking for a subtree equal to \texttt{p2}; if it finds one, \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} names where it sits.
The match is unique: by \cref{sec:policy-dsl}'s leaf-partition validity each flow in \texttt{p2} may appear at most once in \texttt{p1}, so \texttt{p1} can host \texttt{p2} as a subtree in at most one position.
This test runs only at the top level.

\item Otherwise. Hand \((\mathtt{p1}, \mathtt{p2})\) to the per-level comparator.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What the comparator emits}

The comparator compares the roots of \texttt{p1} and \texttt{p2}.
If the roots' constructors disagree (say \texttt{Strict} against \texttt{RR}, or two \texttt{FIFO}s with different classes), it emits a \Replace{}: the always-available \cref{sec:fallback} idiom.
If the constructors agree, the whole task reduces to \emph{pairing up the two child lists}, and each pairing yields one of four kinds of edit.
An arm of \texttt{p2} with no partner in \texttt{p1} is a fresh \Add{}; an arm of \texttt{p1} with no partner in \texttt{p2} is a \Retire{}.
A matched pair whose two arms are identical is set aside and contributes nothing; a matched pair whose arms agree but whose slot metadata differs contributes a lone \ChangeMeta{}; and a matched pair whose arms differ triggers a recursion into the pair to localize the change further, with a trailing \ChangeMeta{} appended when the metadata differs too.

\paragraph{Pairing the child lists}

The comparator pairs the arms by the tightest strategy that applies, dropping to a more general one only when the tighter fails.
All three strategies lean on the upstream normalization pass: because \texttt{Pol.normalize} (\cref{sec:policy-dsl}) canonicalizes sibling order before the planner runs, arms that genuinely correspond appear in the same relative order on both sides, which is what lets the positional strategies pair by position at all.

\textbf{By slot, at equal arity.}
When the child lists have the same length, the comparator pairs arm \(i\) with arm \(i\).
A slot whose arm and metadata both agree contributes nothing, and a slot whose metadata alone differs contributes a \ChangeMeta{}.
A slot whose arm differs recurses: it emits whatever the recursion into that arm localizes, and, when the slot's metadata also changed, a trailing \ChangeMeta{} on top of it.
One shape is caught before the slot walk: at \texttt{Strict} or \texttt{WFQ}, if \texttt{p1} and \texttt{p2} hold the same arms in a different order, the difference is a pure metadata reshuffle (a \texttt{Strict} priority swap, say, which \texttt{Pol.normalize}'s rank ordering surfaces as a reordering), and the comparator emits one \ChangeMeta{} per slot whose metadata changed.

\textbf{By sub-sequence, at unequal arity.}
When one child list embeds in the other as an in-order sub-sequence, the surplus arms are the whole of the change: normalization has already aligned the shared arms in a common order, so their appearing in order is exactly the signal that none of them moved or morphed and only the extra arms are new or gone.
If \texttt{p1}'s arms are an in-order sub-sequence of \texttt{p2}'s, each surplus arm of \texttt{p2} is a fresh \Add{}.
\cref{sec:add}'s leaf-disjointness precondition holds automatically, since a surplus arm's leaves are leaves of \texttt{p2} absent from \texttt{p1} and hence disjoint by \cref{sec:policy-dsl}'s leaf-partition validity.
If instead \texttt{p2}'s arms are an in-order sub-sequence of \texttt{p1}'s, each surplus arm of \texttt{p1} \Retire{}s (\cref{sec:idioms}).
Either way, a stable arm whose metadata changed picks up a trailing \ChangeMeta{}.

\textbf{By shared label, in general.}
When neither list embeds in the other, the comparator pairs arms by the flows they carry rather than by position.
A class label identifies a unique flow across the whole policy (by \cref{sec:policy-dsl}'s leaf-partition validity), so a label shared between an arm of \texttt{p1} and an arm of \texttt{p2} is evidence that one host arm morphed in place, while an arm whose labels appear on no opposing arm is fresh or vanishing.
This \emph{label-overlap walk} greedily pairs each \texttt{p1} arm with the unique \texttt{p2} arm with which it shares a label, then recurses on the pair; unpaired \texttt{p1} arms are \Retire{}d and unpaired \texttt{p2} arms are \Add{}ed.
The walk takes the first alignment that satisfies the label constraint, with no notion of cost (\cref{sec:planner-nonfeatures}); if no shared label yields even one pairing, it fails and the comparator gives up with a slot-level \Replace{}.
At equal arity the walk still earns its keep.
The slot strategy pairs strictly by position, so an arm that has both slid to a new slot \emph{and} changed its subtree gets lined up against whatever now occupies its old slot, and the change reads as two unrelated edits rather than one morph.
The label walk instead follows the shared flow to the arm's new slot and sees the single morph, so the comparator runs it alongside the slot walk and, whenever it exposes such a cross-slot pairing, takes it instead.

Consider an example over an abstract discipline \(\mathtt{P}\) whose arms carry per-arm metadata, with a nested discipline \(\mathtt{Q}\) (the exact disciplines are immaterial to the walk).

In the settled tree \texttt{slack} sits beside the morphed \texttt{gmail} subtree in an order that does not match \texttt{p2} literally, but sibling order carries no semantics (\cref{sec:policy-dsl}), so the reordering is invisible to any observer.

\paragraph{Indexing a mixed emission}

When a comparison yields \Add{}s, \Retire{}s, and recursions at once (the label-overlap walk is the case that does), the planner uses the reorder freedom of \cref{sec:policy-dsl} to keep the indexing trivial: the \Add{}s are appended at the parent's tail, the \Retire{}s fire at their original \texttt{p1} indices in descending order so a higher-index drain does not shift the lower indices still pending, and each matched-pair recursion lands at its position among the matches, which is also its slot once the \Retire{}s drain.
Issuing the \Add{}s first is deliberate: an \Add{} is instantaneous while a \Retire{} waits for its subtree to drain, so the new arms begin carrying traffic immediately instead of waiting on the retirements.

\paragraph{Where the descent localizes the difference}

Every recursion descends through a slot where the two trees agree or where the label-overlap walk has paired them.
So by the time the per-level comparator gives up and emits a \Replace{} at depth \texttt{k}, every shallower level has already matched a tight case, and the \Replace{} lands at the deepest slot to which the planner could isolate the difference, leaving every ancestor's other subtrees running undisturbed.
The slot-level \Replace{} is the \cref{sec:fallback} idiom with a non-empty path: it wraps only the slot's subtree, quiesces only that subtree, and rewires only the parent edge into it.
The worst case is the root-level \(\Replace([], \mathtt{p2})\) of \cref{sec:fallback}, reached only when the per-level comparator gives up at the root with no recursive descent.

\subsection{Imperative Authoring}\label{sec:imperative}

The transition planner so far has realized what we call \emph{declarative} authoring: the operator states a starting policy \texttt{p1} and a target \texttt{p2}, and the planner proposes a guarded sequence that achieves the change, which the operator either accepts (we apply it to the running control) or declines.
Operators may instead author \emph{imperatively}, writing their desired \texttt{p2} together with a \((\varphi; \delta)^{*}\) sequence intended to reach it, possibly built from idioms (\cref{sec:idioms}).
Imperative authoring gives the operator hands-on control over the transitionary policies the live control passes through, rather than delegating their choice to the planner.
The two modes are not formally distinct: they produce sequences over the same substrate, discharge the same per-production obligations of \cref{sec:per-production}, and are admitted by the same pol-level check of \cref{sec:idioms}.

Because an imperative sequence carries \emph{paths}, those paths bind to the running representative, not to the \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} the operator first wrote, which is exactly why we echo that representative back (\cref{sec:interplay}).
The worked example of \cref{sec:interplay} is the case in point: there the running control realizes \texttt{p2'} while the operator had written \texttt{p2}:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathtt{p2'} &= \mathtt{Strict(lo: gmail, hi: zoom, mid: spotify)},\\
\mathtt{p2}  &= \mathtt{Strict(lo: gmail, mid: spotify, hi: zoom)}.
\end{aligned}
\]
An imperative edit \(\Quiesce([2])\) resolves against \texttt{p2'}: slot \texttt{2} there is \texttt{mid: spotify}, so \texttt{spotify} is quiesced.
Had the operator read the path off their own \texttt{p2}, slot \texttt{2} is \texttt{hi: zoom}, a different arm; the runtime interprets the path against what is running and has no way to detect the mismatch.

Before committing a user-authored sequence we run it through the pol-level check of \cref{sec:idioms}, folding \(\psem{\delta_i}\) from \(\lfloor \mathtt{C} \rfloor\) and accepting only if the fold is everywhere defined and lands at \texttt{p2} up to \(\eqR\).
The intermediate pols it produces are not transient artifacts of the proof: each is a valid pol with an explainable scheduling semantics.
For instance, after the first step of \Replace(\ensuremath{\mathit{path}}, \texttt{B})'s expansion the intermediate pol holds \(\Strict(\mathit{pol}@\mathit{path}, B)\) at \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}: a temporary strict-priority node favoring the outgoing \(\mathit{pol}@\mathit{path}\) over the incoming \texttt{B}, with a readable scheduling interpretation in its own right.

\subsection{What the planner does not do today}\label{sec:planner-nonfeatures}

Two non-features for now; we hope to improve these in the next few meetings.
\emph{No cost model:} the case analysis is shape-based, not cost-weighted.
A cost-aware planner could prefer different decompositions (e.g., several small \Add{}s vs.\ a single root-level \Replace) based on per-flow drop and delay tolerances or per-PE budgets.
Presently we take the first admissible alignment without ranking competitors.
\emph{No imperative-mode service:} the planner serves declarative mode only.
Imperative authoring (\cref{sec:imperative}) goes through its own pol-level check directly, with the operator naming each \(\delta\) themselves; the planner is not consulted.

\section{Rio Hardware Implementation}\label{sec:hardware}

A PIFO tree is a clean scheduling abstraction, but instantiating one dynamically on line-rate switching hardware needs more than a fixed set of hardwired PIFOs.
Existing designs~\cite{TODO} make a logical-to-physical unit mapping to materialize programmable scheduling: instead of hard-wiring a fixed set of PIFOs into hardware, the switch exposes programmable processing elements (PEs) hosting multiple virtual PIFOs.
It includes hardware (usually match-action pipelines) that maps packets to its virtual PIFOs and calculates the rank and next-hop destination, followed by one physical PIFO hosting all of the virtual PIFOs on the PE.

Packet scheduling on this substrate consists of pipelined, non-atomic enqueue and dequeue operations.
On enqueue, the switch must compute two pieces of information before issuing PE pushes: which virtual PIFO id the packet should enter, and what rank should be inserted at each of those virtual PIFOs.
The first is determined by packet classification, flow-to-child mappings; the second is computed by the virtual PIFO's scheduling policy from packet fields, flow state, and per-arm metadata.
For example, a WFQ-style virtual PIFO uses the child weight to compute a virtual finish time, while a Strict virtual PIFO interprets the metadata as a priority rank.
On dequeue, the switch starts from the output port's root virtual PIFO, pops the best-ranked entry, and follows the stored next-hop information until it reaches a packet to transmit or another virtual PIFO to pop.

This is sufficient for a static policy, but it does not by itself support dynamic policy updates in concurrent with packet enqueue and dequeue.
In such a case, a single packet operation can observe a mixture of old and new scheduler state.
For example, an enqueue may choose its virtual PIFO path using the old flow-to-child mapping but compute its rank using newly updated per-arm metadata, or a dequeue may pop from one version of the tree and then follow next-hop state from another.
As a result, existing PIFO-based substrates support only stop-the-world policy update.

\subsection{Transactional Update and Hardware Component Support}\label{sec:hw-transactional}

\emph{Vocabulary bridge to \S7.1' (temporary, until the two subsections are reconciled).}
The ``flow-to-child mapping'', ``next-hop mapping'', and ``match-action tables'' of this subsection are the tables that \S7.1''s \texttt{map} / \texttt{assoc} / \texttt{adopt} opcodes write to; the ``virtual PIFOs'' of this subsection are the PIFOs of \S7.1', one per PIFO-tree node.

Rio enables online policy update by packaging multiple hardware state updates into a single ``commit'' and making them visible atomically in hardware: every enqueue or dequeue request observes either the whole commit applied, or none of it.
Each \(\delta\) (\cref{sec:delta-grammar}) lowers to one \texttt{commit} under this abstraction.

In Rio hardware, we achieve this by packaging multiple policy updates into a single ``transaction'' and making minimal hardware changes in virtual PIFO, rank, and next-hop mapping to enable transactional commit of multiple updates.

First, Rio builds transactional mapping tables for virtual PIFO and next-hop mapping.
Each PE stores its flow-to-child mapping and next-hop state in a specialized table that accepts a stream of update instructions, and a commit signal.
Updates before the signal are staged but remain invisible to packet processing.
Only when the commit marker is applied does the table immediately switch to the new version.
We implement this using double buffering: update instructions modify a shadow copy of the table, and the commit marker atomically swaps the active and shadow copies.
After the swap, the inactive copy is synchronized in the background to serve as the shadow for the next commit.

Second, Rio supports incremental updates to the rank-computing match-action tables.
Rio does not require arbitrary in-place replacement of a live scheduling policy.
Instead, policy-table updates correspond to adding metadata for newly admitted flows (whose packets are not allowed before the update is finished) or removing metadata for flows that have already been drained.
Because these updates do not change the interpretation of existing live packets, Rio applies them incrementally rather than through a full transactional table swap.
This keeps the hardware cost low while still preserving the atomicity needed for observable scheduling behavior.

\subsection*{7.1' The commit model and the ISA}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{7.1' The commit model and the ISA}

\emph{Vocabulary bridge from \cref{sec:hw-transactional} (temporary, until the two subsections are reconciled).}
\cref{sec:hw-transactional} speaks in ``flow-to-child mapping'', ``next-hop mapping'', ``match-action tables'', and ``virtual PIFOs''.
This subsection speaks in \texttt{map} / \texttt{assoc} (which write the flow-to-child mapping), \texttt{adopt} / \texttt{emancipate} (which install and detach parent/child edges, i.e., the next-hop mapping), the per-PIFO tables that these opcodes write to (\cref{sec:hw-transactional}'s match-action tables), and PIFOs (which correspond to \cref{sec:hw-transactional}'s virtual PIFOs, one per PIFO-tree node).

The substrate exposes its services to the planner as an atomic-commit interface.
The ISA has exactly one unit: a \texttt{commit}, which is a list of instructions that the substrate installs \emph{atomically}.
The entire list lands between two consecutive \texttt{push}/\texttt{pop} operations, with no intermediate state visible to the user's \texttt{pop} stream.
This is precisely the atomicity that \cref{sec:per-production}'s non-disruption obligation takes as given.
A commit's \emph{length} may depend on live control state (how many flows are admitted, how many siblings need shifting), but its \emph{atomicity} does not.

Each \(\delta\) (\cref{sec:delta-grammar}) lowers to one \texttt{commit}; that is the entire ISA-level contract.
The ISA itself is unconditional: each instruction describes what to do, not what to check.
The substrate trusts that its caller has issued a well-formed commit and acts accordingly.
Any situation-dependent reasoning (e.g., ``the subtree we are about to collapse is empty'') lives at \cref{sec:sequences}, in a guard \(\varphi\) that gated the firing of the corresponding \(\delta\), not in the substrate.

Four pieces of substrate vocabulary appear in the listing below:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{PIFO}: one node of the PIFO-tree, addressed by an opaque id \texttt{v}.
The substrate exposes one PIFO per tree node; how those PIFOs are mapped onto hardware is a substrate concern (see ``PE deployment'' later in this section).
\item \textbf{PE} (processing element): hosts one or more PIFOs and owns their per-node state and logic.
A single PE can host multiple PIFOs at once; in particular, sibling PIFOs commonly cohabit a PE.
\item \textbf{flow}: a traffic class.
\item \textbf{index}: opaque per-PIFO handle naming one of that PIFO's children; what a PIFO uses internally to refer to a child arm.
\end{itemize}

The ISA has thirteen opcodes, laid out in \cref{tab:isa-opcodes}.

Our single-purpose opcodes also mean that the tree can drift transiently malformed \emph{within} a commit, in ways that the commit's atomicity hides from any observer.
For an instance from \cref{sec:hw-compile}: \Designate's commit first adopts \texttt{loser} as a child of the freshly-spawned \texttt{sp\_v}, then emancipates \texttt{loser} from its original parent and adopts \texttt{sp\_v} in its place.
Between the first adopt and the emancipate, \texttt{loser} has two parents.
The atomic install means no \texttt{push} or \texttt{pop} ever sees this state, but the planner is free to issue commits whose intermediate frames would not satisfy the \cref{sec:runtime-model} well-formedness invariants.

One last piece of setup the lowerings in \cref{sec:hw-compile} will lean on: \emph{PE deployment}.
We treat the assignment of a PIFO to a PE as a deterministic function \texttt{pe(path)} of the PIFO's tree position, fixed at compile time.
A typical shape is depth-by-PE: PIFOs at the same tree depth share a PE~\cite{Sivaraman16}, so sibling PIFOs cohabit one PE by default.
The planner consults \texttt{pe(path)} when it needs a \texttt{spawn}'s \texttt{pe} argument; the function's definition is a property of the target substrate and is not otherwise observable at the ISA.

\subsection{Compiling Policy Differences}\label{sec:hw-compile}

Each production of \(\delta\) (\cref{sec:delta-grammar}) lowers to a list of op codes.
The lowering \emph{schema} is mechanical: each production names a fixed sequence of opcodes, and the planner reads live state at issue time to instantiate the schema's positional parameters (flow lists, chain shapes, routing indices) before dispatching the commit to the substrate.

Before working through the productions, we set up one structural convention.
A switch with \(P\) output ports has \(P\) separate trees, and at the ISA we put a uniform thin wrapper around each.
Every port hosts a reserved PIFO \texttt{port\_root}, allocated at boot on a reserved PE, whose sole child is the actual tree root.
\texttt{port\_root} runs a fixed 1-arm policy.
Its \texttt{Assoc} set mirrors that of the live tree's actual root; its \texttt{Map} sends each Assoc'd flow through its single index \texttt{port\_step}.
This \texttt{port\_root} state is load-bearing for \ChangeRoot, and it is maintained by two complementary mechanisms.
On every production except \ChangeRoot, the \texttt{walk(...)} calls that touch \texttt{Assoc}/\texttt{Map} start at \texttt{port\_root} and so update its tables in lockstep with the actual root's.
On \ChangeRoot, the lowering issues no flow-table writes against \texttt{port\_root} at all: the swap is exactly one \texttt{emancipate}/\texttt{adopt} pair against \texttt{port\_root}, and the existing \texttt{Assoc}/\texttt{Map} state survives the swap because \texttt{port\_step} is the index \emph{name}, unbound to any particular child.
The wrapper exists so that ``swap the root'' can be expressed as one \texttt{emancipate}/\texttt{adopt} pair against \texttt{port\_root}, in the same vocabulary as any child-level edit; there is no opcode for changing the root of the tree directly.

The classifier and ranker of \cref{sec:controls} line up one to one with this vocabulary.
A node's \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{admit}\) is its \texttt{Assoc} set, its \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves}\) is its \texttt{Map} table, and its \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{rank}\) is what \texttt{set\_policy} installs and \texttt{discipline(v)} reads back.
The correspondence slips in one place: the per-arm metadata the ranker consults sits at tree level in \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_states}} rather than in \ensuremath{\mathit{z}}, while the ISA writes it with \texttt{set\_arm\_meta} alongside the ranker.

The lowerings follow, one per production in the order of the \cref{sec:delta-grammar} grammar.
We fix a handful of reading conventions.
We identify a tree position with the PIFO that lives there.
Live queries against a node \texttt{v} (its \texttt{arity(v)}, \texttt{discipline(v)}, etc.) are read off the tree at issue time.
When an opcode names an unmentioned parameter at \texttt{v} (e.g., \texttt{set\_policy}'s discipline, \texttt{change\_arity}'s arity), it is the live value at \texttt{v}.
\texttt{spawn}'s \texttt{pe} argument is similarly elided: it is \texttt{pe(pos(v))}, drawn from \S7.1''s deployment convention.

Alongside the opcodes, the listings use a small codegen vocabulary:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{foreach x in S: BODY} emits \texttt{BODY} once per \texttt{x} drawn from \texttt{S}.
\item \texttt{walk(op, C, f)} emits \texttt{op(v, f, \ldots)} at each \(v \in C\); positional remainders such as \texttt{map}'s routing index \(i_{v,f}\) are read off the live tree.
\item \texttt{flows(path)} is the set of flows admitted by some leaf under \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}.
\item \texttt{chain(f)} is the sequence of PIFOs from \texttt{port\_root} to the leaf admitting \texttt{f}, and \texttt{internals(f)} is that sequence minus the leaf.
\item \texttt{nodes(T)} and \texttt{edges(T)} are the PIFOs and parent-child edges of a compiled subtree \texttt{T}; \texttt{root(T)} is \texttt{T}'s root PIFO.
\item \texttt{layout(T)} expands to \texttt{spawn}+\texttt{set\_policy} at every \(v \in \mathtt{nodes(T)}\), \texttt{adopt} at every internal edge in \(\mathtt{edges(T)}\), and \texttt{set\_arm\_meta} at each per-arm slot where \texttt{v}'s discipline requires it.
The parent-side \texttt{adopt} that attaches \(\mathtt{root(T)}\) is left to the caller.
\end{itemize}

Where a production destructures \(\delta\)'s path as \(\pi \mathbin{+\!\!+} [k]\), we write \texttt{p} for the parent's path and \texttt{k} for the child's local index.
For \(\mathit{path} = [\,]\), the parent is \texttt{port\_root} and \texttt{k} is \texttt{port\_step}.

\paragraph*{\(\ChangeMeta(\pi \mathbin{+\!\!+} [k], m)\)}
\begin{Verbatim}
set_arm_meta(p, k, m)
\end{Verbatim}

\paragraph*{\(\Quiesce(\mathit{path})\)}
\begin{Verbatim}
foreach f in flows(path):
    walk(deassoc, chain(f), f)
\end{Verbatim}

\paragraph*{\(\Add(\mathit{path}, D, \mathit{arm}, \mathit{meta?})\)}
\begin{Verbatim}
n := arity(path)
layout(arm)
change_arity(path, n+1)
if meta?: set_arm_meta(path, n, meta?)
adopt(n, path, root(arm))
foreach f in flows(arm):
    walk(assoc, chain(f), f)
    walk(map,   internals(f), f)
\end{Verbatim}
The new subtree occupies \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}'s rightmost slot (index \texttt{n}), so existing children's indices are undisturbed.

\paragraph*{\(\Remove(\pi \mathbin{+\!\!+} [k])\)}
\begin{Verbatim}
change_arity(p, arity(p) - 1)
emancipate(k, p)
foreach f in flows(p ++ [k]):
    walk(unmap, internals(f), f)
foreach v in nodes(p ++ [k]):
    gc(v)
\end{Verbatim}
\texttt{p}'s remaining children keep their indices: each was minted at adoption time, so no sibling-shift cascade is needed.

\paragraph*{\(\Designate(\mathit{path}, \mathit{survivor})\)}
\begin{Verbatim}
loser  := PIFO at path;   parent := PIFO at path's parent
k      := path's last index
surv   := root(survivor)
layout(survivor)
spawn(sp_v, pe(path))
set_policy(sp_v, Strict*, 2)
adopt(0, sp_v, loser); adopt(1, sp_v, surv)
set_arm_meta(sp_v, 0, 1.0)
set_arm_meta(sp_v, 1, 2.0)
emancipate(k, parent); adopt(k, parent, sp_v)
designate(loser, surv)
foreach f in flows(loser) \ flows(surv):
    assoc(sp_v, f); map(sp_v, f, 0)
foreach f in flows(surv):
    assoc(sp_v, f); map(sp_v, f, 1)
foreach f in flows(surv) \ flows(path):
    walk(assoc, chain(f), f)
    walk(map,   internals(f), f)
\end{Verbatim}
Reusing the same \texttt{k} for the rewire preserves \texttt{parent}'s per-arm metadata for that slot.
Flows in \(\mathtt{flows(surv)} \cap \mathtt{flows(path)}\) need no above-\texttt{sp\_v} wiring: the chain is already in place from \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}'s prior wiring.
Flows in \(\mathtt{flows(loser)} \setminus \mathtt{flows(surv)}\) keep their existing above-\texttt{sp\_v} Assoc/Map state and are routed to slot 0 by \texttt{sp\_v}.\ensuremath{\mathit{z}}, matching \cref{sec:designate}'s operational rule that loser-only labels drain on arm 0.
Operator-level silencing of those flows, when the operator ultimately wants it, comes from the \Quiesce{} step of the surrounding \Replace{} idiom (\cref{sec:sequences}), not from \Designate.

\emph{Same-PE invariant.}
The planner places \texttt{sp\_v}, \texttt{loser}'s root, and \texttt{surv}'s root on the same PE (a deterministic property of the lowering: all three sit at depth \(|\mathit{path}|\), and \texttt{pe(path)} returns one PE).
The substrate may exploit this to coalesce the three into a single physical slot, sparing itself from moving all of \texttt{loser} down one PE level.

\paragraph*{\(\Undesignate(\mathit{path})\)}
\begin{Verbatim}
sp_v    := super-node head at path
loser_v := its retiring arm
undesignate(loser_v)
gc(sp_v)
foreach v in nodes(loser_v):
    gc(v)
\end{Verbatim}
\texttt{undesignate} rewires the parent's slot past \texttt{sp\_v} but does not deallocate it, so an explicit \texttt{gc(sp\_v)} follows.

\paragraph*{\(\ChangeRoot(\mathit{path})\)}
Let the live tree be \(\mathtt{port\_root} \to a_0 \to a_1 \to \ldots \to a_m \to \mathit{path}\) (the \cref{sec:delta-grammar} restriction makes \(a_0 \ldots a_m\) a unary vine).
\begin{Verbatim}
emancipate(port_step, port_root)
adopt(port_step, port_root, path)
foreach a in {a_0, ..., a_m}:
    gc(a)
\end{Verbatim}

Three items in the list deserve unpacking.

\Quiesce{}'s wide reach (root-to-\ensuremath{\mathit{path}} chain, plus the subtree under \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}) is forced by \cref{sec:policy-dsl}'s parallel push: every node on a packet's path mints its own routing index independently, so dropping \texttt{f} only at some nodes would leave the rest happy to mint stray indices that would leave the tree malformed.
The chain walk is what \cref{sec:quiesce} calls narrowing the admitted set uniformly.
\Quiesce{} does not issue \texttt{unmap}: silenced flows' Map entries \emph{must outlive the silencing} so that buffered packets can be popped.
\Remove{} pairs \texttt{emancipate} with \texttt{unmap} precisely because it fires after the drain, when there is no longer any in-flight traffic to preserve.
One subtlety in the loser arm of a \(\Strictstar\) super-node (i.e., a \Quiesce{} that fires mid-\Replace{}): the lowering restricts the deassoc walk to loser-only classes, since \Designate{} routed the shared classes to the survivor's slot at commit time (\cref{sec:designate}), and deassoc'ing them here would silence live traffic on the survivor.

The lowering for \Designate{} matches \cref{sec:grammar}'s tree-level reading exactly: a fresh Strict-2 PIFO \texttt{sp\_v} is spawned, \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}'s previous occupant becomes its favored child (rank 1.0), and the survivor becomes its second child (rank 2.0).
The lowering rewires \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}'s parent edge from \texttt{loser} to \texttt{sp\_v}, reusing the same parent-side index \texttt{k}; the parent's per-arm metadata for the slot is therefore preserved across \Designate{} and across the eventual \Undesignate{} that retires the give-up.
\texttt{designate(loser, surv)} is the ISA-level marker that names the favored child and records the eventual successor; a later \texttt{undesignate} swings the parent's index over to \texttt{surv} and releases \texttt{sp\_v} along with the loser subtree.
A substrate that recognizes the same-PE invariant on \texttt{(sp\_v, loser, surv)} is free to coalesce the three into a single physical slot (the ``super-node'' optimization); the ISA does not pin this down, leaving room for substrates that prefer the literal Strict-2 form.

\ChangeRoot{} writes no flow tables: \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}'s \texttt{Assoc} and \texttt{Map} already describe the post-commit live tree, since collapsing the unary vine above \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} leaves the flows-to-leaves mapping unchanged.

\subsection{Substrate portability}\label{sec:substrate-portability}

These mechanisms are independent of the underlying PIFO implementation.
Our prototype uses a shift-register-based PIFO similar to the original design, but Rio only assumes the standard push-in-first-out interface.
Other PIFO implementations can be substituted.

\subsection{Executing \(\delta\) as a transaction}\label{sec:hw-transaction}

Rio implements a transactional update controller that executes each lowered \(\delta\) as an atomic hardware transaction.
In hardware, the controller executes commands in order.
Ordinary update commands are routed to the target PEs and staged locally.
When the controller reaches the commit instruction, it generates the hardware commit signals that make the staged updates visible.
These signals are delay-aligned across PEs according to the PIFO tree topology, so that no enqueue or dequeue operation can traverse a partially updated tree.
Intuitively, the controller treats the commit boundary as a wavefront: the new configuration becomes visible at different PEs at carefully chosen times, ensuring that any individual packet observes a consistent configuration along its entire path.

The controller also implements guarded transitions.
As described in \cref{sec:sequences}, some transitions are guarded by predicates such as the emptiness of a subtree.
Rio lowers such guards to blocking commands in the controller queue.
A blocking command remains at the head of the queue until its predicate becomes true; only then can the subsequent update instructions execute.
FIFO execution therefore ensures that instructions depending on the guard are not issued prematurely.

Our current implementation uses a single controller queue, which provides a simple global serialization point for updates.
This design is sufficient for correctness and avoids subtle ordering bugs between overlapping commits.
It can, however, introduce head-of-line blocking when a guarded transition waits for a long-running drain.
Supporting multiple independent queues for disjoint subtrees is a natural optimization and does not change Rio's ISA or correctness argument; we leave this extension to future work.

\section{Per-Discipline Slot Initialization}\label{app:init-slot}

\cref{sec:policy-dsl} fixes the design choice that a freshly spliced arm should ``join the current round'', entering with neither the penalty nor the advantage of having been absent.
Here are the \(\mathit{init\_slot}_{D}\) recipes that realize it for our three representative disciplines.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\texttt{RoundRobin}.} \texttt{init\_slot\_RoundRobin} returns the empty tuple; RR has no per-arm bookkeeping.
The round-robin cursor lives in \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}} instead, and at \Add{} we splice the new arm into the child list at the cursor's current position, so its first turn lands in the round already in progress.
The cursor itself is left pointing at the same physical arm it was pointing at before the splice.
\item \textbf{\texttt{Strict}.} \texttt{init\_slot\_Strict(\_, p) = p}: the arm's \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_state}} is just its priority rank, drawn from a dense total order such as the rationals so that a fresh priority can always be slotted strictly between two existing ones.
\item \textbf{\texttt{WFQ}.} The \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}} at a parent is the virtual time \texttt{vt}, and \texttt{init\_slot\_WFQ(vt, w) = (w, vt)}: a new arm carries its weight \texttt{w} and inherits the parent's current \texttt{vt} as its last-finish tag.
Any standard WFQ tagging that derives the next packet's tag from the slot's last-finish tag and the parent's \texttt{vt} (e.g., the GPS-style \texttt{max(virtual\_clock, vt) + 1/w}) then slots the first packet on this arm into the round that the established arms are currently in.
At compile time the parent's \texttt{vt} is freshly initialized (to zero), so all original arms get \texttt{(w, 0)} and the round is ``the zeroth''; at \Add{} the parent's \texttt{vt} is whatever the clock has advanced to.
\end{itemize}
