\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Programmable packet scheduling.
Emphasize that policies are often hierarchical, and that clients demand line rate.
% Let's just write this paragraph out. It's been a bullet for a long time.

Consider a small-office gateway running \(\Strict(\mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{lo}{:}\,\textsf{gmail})\), strictly prioritizing real-time \textsf{zoom} traffic over \textsf{gmail} traffic.
The network operator wants to add a new \textsf{spotify} flow, and is considering two candidate policies:
\(\Strict(\mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{mid}{:}\,\textsf{spotify},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{lo}{:}\,\textsf{gmail})\), extending the strict-priority list with \textsf{spotify}; or
\(\Strict(\mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{lo}{:}\,\RR(\textsf{gmail}, \textsf{spotify}))\), keeping \textsf{zoom} at the higher tier but having \textsf{gmail} and \textsf{spotify} share the lower tier via round-robin.
In either case, the state of the art~\cite{vPIFO} would stop the world, drop or recirculate buffered packets, recompile, and reinstall.
The costs are dropped or recirculated (and therefore delayed) packets, downtime, and the rebuilding of nodes that did not need to be rebuilt.
% The sentence above should be crunched and merged above.
% This feels like a good place to say something like "this paper's thesis is..."

The alternative is to reprogram a scheduler without stopping the world.
A transition from a running policy \(p_1\) to a requested policy \(p_2\) takes one of two shapes (\cref{fig:two-timelines}).
Sometimes the requested policy can be installed immediately, as a single atomic switch (case~1).
% let's not say "switch" casually in this paper. You will need to do a sweet all around, and add a rule/memory saying not to reintroduce it. Above you can just say "transition".
Sometimes it cannot, and the scheduler must first pass through a transitionary policy (case~2).
Our two candidates illustrate the two cases.

Moving to \(\Strict(\mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{mid}{:}\,\textsf{spotify},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{lo}{:}\,\textsf{gmail})\) is a case~1 transition.
Before time \(t_1\), \(\Strict(\mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{lo}{:}\,\textsf{gmail})\) is running; at time \(t_1\) the request to transition is received, and we immediately satisfy it.
The transition at \(t_1\) is \emph{atomic}, meaning that any interaction that the network had with the scheduler before \(t_1\) happened entirely in the old regime, and whatever interaction the network had with the scheduler after \(t_1\) happened entirely in the new regime.
It is also \emph{immediate}, in that no period of preparation precedes the atomic jump into \(\Strict(\mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{mid}{:}\,\textsf{spotify},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{lo}{:}\,\textsf{gmail})\).
The two concepts are distinct.

Moving to \(\Strict(\mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{lo}{:}\,\RR(\textsf{gmail}, \textsf{spotify}))\) is a case~2 transition.
As before, \(\Strict(\mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{lo}{:}\,\textsf{gmail})\) runs before \(t_1\) and the operator's request arrives at \(t_1\), but we cannot move to \(\Strict(\mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{lo}{:}\,\RR(\textsf{gmail}, \textsf{spotify}))\) immediately since there are still \textsf{gmail} packets buffered in the scheduler.
Switching immediately would mean re-sorting those buffered \textsf{gmail} packets into the new round-robin leaf, but their scheduling order was fixed the moment they were enqueued; we cannot rewrite that past without dropping or reordering them.
We atomically step instead into a \emph{transitionary period} that is \emph{not} the operator's desired policy.
% let's stop saying "transitionary period". Say "transitionary policy".
After \(t_1\) but before \(t_2\), the scheduler still accepts and emits packets under this transitionary regime.
At time \(t_2\), defined as the instant that a well-defined guard becomes true, we atomically step into \(\Strict(\mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{lo}{:}\,\RR(\textsf{gmail}, \textsf{spotify}))\).

Although the operator never specified the semantics of this transitionary period (\(t_1\) to \(t_2\)), the period is itself a \emph{de facto} packet scheduling regime.
The transition is unavoidable, it persists for nonzero time, and throughout it the scheduler must continue to serve the network by accepting and emitting packets.
So \emph{scheduling decisions} are being made during that window.
Once we refuse to stop the world, the interval between two policies is itself a scheduling policy, and that is a central thesis of this paper.
% "Once we refuse to stop the world" needs to be motivated earlier on, and then referred to. Right now it comes out of the blue
We call it the \emph{transitionary policy}, and treat it as a policy in its own right.
Some transitions are better than others from a network operator's point of view, and the state of the art has settled on a trivial stop-the-world transitionary policy.
Our contributions are to be clear about what the transitionary policy is and to improve on the state of the art.
% The two paragraphs above can be tightened significantly.

We take on two obligations.
\emph{Obligation 1: give a semantics to the transitionary policy.}
The transitionary period is shaped by hardware-level manipulations on the way to realizing \(p_2\), rather than by anything the operator wrote, so its semantics is not handed to us.
We develop a grammar of atomic edits (\cref{sec:grammar}) and a sequence planner (\cref{sec:sequences}) that carries us from the operator-supplied policy \(p_1\), through one or more transitionary policies, and finally to the operator-supplied policy \(p_2\).
The grammar is designed so that \emph{any transitionary policy it produces is ordinary scheduling}.
Such a policy has two valuable properties: it has a human-readable semantics just as the operator-written \(p_1\) and \(p_2\) do, \emph{and} the step into it is guaranteed to be one the hardware can commit in a single instant.

We will give the semantics of the stop-the-world transitionary policy and use it as a baseline.
Two practical goals guide the search for better ways to transition from a given \(p_1\) to a given \(p_2\).
We would like to shorten the transition period, and we would like to avoid dropping or delaying packets.

\section{Background \& Motivation}\label{sec:background}

\subsection{Hierarchical scheduling on hardware}\label{sec:hierarchical}

A \emph{PIFO} (push-in, first-out queue) is a priority queue that is additionally defined to break rank-ties in FIFO order.
Where a single PIFO expresses just one scheduling decision, a PIFO tree composes policies via parent-child relationships, capturing the hierarchical policies that operators actually deploy.
% Check my rewrite

A PIFO tree lowers to hardware by mapping each tree depth onto a dedicated processing element (PE), with siblings and cousins sharing the PE at their depth.
Pinning a depth level to a single PE has well-studied benefits for computation and storage, and it is the layout that Sivaraman et al.~\cite{Sivaraman16}, Mohan et al.~\cite{Mohan23}, Shrivastav~\cite{Shrivastav19}, and vPIFO~\cite{vPIFO} all build on.
% Just plain citations
The PE budget is fixed by the silicon, so the running tree's shape is constrained by what the deployed PEs can host.


\subsection{vPIFO, and the problem it leaves open}\label{sec:vpifo}

\xxx[AM]{Not sure if they get their own section}

vPIFO~\cite{vPIFO} is the closest related work, and it explicitly leaves our problem open.
It virtualizes a single physical PIFO into many logical ``PIFO instances,'' with a Scheduling Description Language (SDL) and compiler, so their PIFO Visor can \emph{flexibly establish} hierarchical PIFO trees of arbitrary shape on fixed hardware; its contribution is the reconfigurable substrate.
As published, however, vPIFO has no notion of a difference between old and new policies, no formal semantics for what a policy change means, and no account of in-flight packets during a change.
Their SDL IR computes ranks and is compiled to P4 or to a CPU.
The two grammars we introduce in \cref{sec:grammar} are structural instead: one describes the shape of a policy tree, and the other describes how that shape changes.

Our two running examples make the gap concrete.
In vPIFO's own terms, the Operation Generation Table and the PIFO Instance Address Table encode the running tree's shape and its per-instance storage.
The easy change, to \(\Strict(\mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{mid}{:}\,\textsf{spotify},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{lo}{:}\,\textsf{gmail})\), is the kind of edit vPIFO's substrate is architecturally close to absorbing.
It over-provisions logical instances and its Visor can assemble them into arbitrary tree shapes, so a targeted append to those tables would register the new traffic and its operations while leaving the running instances untouched.
vPIFO exposes no such edit at runtime, however: those tables are generated offline by its SDL compiler, so installing even this easy change on a running scheduler would require extending the substrate with an online-patch path it does not have today.
The harder restructuring, to \(\Strict(\mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{lo}{:}\,\RR(\textsf{gmail}, \textsf{spotify}))\), lies beyond what the vPIFO substrate can absorb in place.
It changes the running tree's shape, inserting a new internal \RR{} node and re-parenting \textsf{gmail} under it, and neither table exposes that as an in-place edit.
The closest vPIFO comes is a full reinitialization onto the new shape, which is precisely the stop-the-world baseline we want to improve on.
% If we have soundly rejected this as an option in the intro, then we can again strengthen this "we want to improve on"
vPIFO names runtime updating as future work, observing that the scheduling of packets during a modification's transitional phase is unresolved.

We are not competing with vPIFO and do not claim a better PIFO substrate.
We supply the layer \emph{above} a PIFO substrate: the formal transition between two policies, the small patch that realizes it, and the transitionary semantics.
The two layers compose.

\section{Policies and Controls}\label{sec:model}

This section introduces the two objects that the rest of the paper edits.
\cref{sec:policy-dsl} gives the small DSL an operator writes, \cref{sec:controls} gives the control a switch runs, and \cref{sec:compilation} compiles the first into the second.

\subsection{What the operator writes}\label{sec:policy-dsl}

An operator wants to say which flows exist and how they should share the link.
A switch runs something much lower level, a tree of queues carrying scheduling logic at every node, which \cref{sec:controls} makes precise.
We start on the operator's side, with a small policy DSL \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}, and \cref{sec:compilation} compiles it down.

\begin{figure}[t]
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
\mathit{flow} \in F\\[4pt]
D\ \text{a scheduling discipline, declaring a metadata set } M_{D}\\[8pt]
\mathit{pol} \mathrel{::=} \mathit{flow} \;\mid\; D(m_1{:}\,\mathit{pol}_1, \ldots, m_n{:}\,\mathit{pol}_n),\ m_i \in M_{D}
\end{array}
\]
\caption{The policy language \(\mathit{pol}\).}
\label{fig:pol-grammar}
\end{figure}

The operator writes a \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} (\cref{fig:pol-grammar}).
It is either a leaf labeled by a flow, or an internal node running a scheduling discipline \ensuremath{D} over child policies.
Each arm carries a metadatum that tells \ensuremath{D} how to schedule it, drawn from a set \(M_{D}\) that \ensuremath{D} declares.
\(M_{\Strict}\) is a set of priorities, and \(M_{\WFQ}\) is a set of weights.
\RR{} treats its arms alike and consults nothing about any one of them, so \(M_{\RR}\) has a single element.
% Demote the above to examples more than a comprehensive list.
That element carries no information, and we elide it when writing examples.
We read the arity off by counting children, so, e.g., \(\Strict(\mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{lo}{:}\,\textsf{gmail})\) is the 2-ary instance of the discipline \Strict.
Each leaf label denotes a \emph{flow}: a predicate over packets.

The family of disciplines is open.
\ensuremath{D} is any discipline equipped with the compilation recipe of \cref{sec:compilation}, and nothing that follows depends on which disciplines are available.
Our implementation instantiates \Strict, \RR, and \WFQ because they aid our exposition.

A \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} is written against a fixed \emph{flow universe} \(F\): a finite set of flow predicates declared by the operator before constructing the \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}.
A \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} is \emph{valid} when (a) every discipline is applied at the proper arity, (b) every arm's metadatum is drawn from its parent discipline's set \(M_{D}\), and (c) the leaves form a partition of a subset of \(F\): each leaf carries a flow drawn from \(F\), and no two leaves carry the same flow.
If a packet does not match any of the flows in the scheduling tree's leaves, the packet is dropped.

\subsection{The runtime model: controls}\label{sec:controls}

A PIFO tree fixes which queues sit where, and says nothing about how a packet's rank is chosen at each of them.
% Above you've said a bunch of stuff, written in the negative, about something the reader has just met
A \emph{control} attaches that logic to the tree, giving each node its own rule for ranking what it enqueues.
The name and the model are from Mohan et al.~\cite{Mohan23}, and we note our departures from them as they arise.
A control is the concrete state a programmable switch executes at line rate, realizable on an FPGA or SmartNIC (\cref{sec:hardware} lowers a control to exactly such a target).

\paragraph{Syntax}

A control \(C\) is a finite tree whose every node carries a triple:
\[
C \mathrel{::=} \langle \mathit{state}, \mathit{pifo}, \mathit{z} \rangle(C_1, \ldots, C_n), \qquad n \geq 0.
\]
A node with no children (\(n = 0\)) is a \emph{leaf}, and a node with children is \emph{internal}.
What a node enqueues into its \emph{pifo} is called its \emph{payload}: at an internal node the payload is an index into the child list, and at a leaf the payload is a packet.
% The paragraph above feels misplaced. It's talking about "node with no children (\(n = 0\))" but the reader doesn't even know how to read the triple. They don't know that the Cs are children, what n is, etc.

\begin{itemize}
\item \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} is a pair \((\mathit{node\_state}, \mathit{slot\_states})\): the scheduling discipline's per-node bookkeeping and a list of per-arm entries, one per child.
Both evolve as the node serves traffic.
When the node runs a discipline that keeps no per-arm bookkeeping, \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_states}} is empty.
\item \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} is a PIFO of ranked payloads.
An internal node's \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} therefore holds indices into its child list, and a leaf's \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} holds packets.
\item \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} is the node's \emph{scheduling transaction},
\[
\mathit{z} : \mathit{state} \times \mathit{Pkt} \rightharpoonup (\mathit{payload} \times \mathit{rank}) \times \mathit{state}.
\]
Given the node's \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} and an incoming packet, it returns the payload this node will enqueue for that packet, the rank to enqueue it at, and the node's updated \ensuremath{\mathit{state}}.
\ensuremath{\mathit{z}} is partial.
When it is undefined for a packet, nothing is enqueued at this node and \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} is unchanged.
\end{itemize}

% Graf below feels misplaced
\paragraph{Addressing a node}

We address node-local components of a control by a \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}, a possibly empty sequence of child indices read from the root.
The local triple at the node reached by following \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} from control \(C\)'s root is written \(C@\mathit{path}\), with fields \(C@\mathit{path}.\mathit{state}\), \(C@\mathit{path}.\mathit{pifo}\), and \(C@\mathit{path}.\mathit{z}\).
We also write \(C@\mathit{path}.\mathit{node\_state}\) and \(C@\mathit{path}.\mathit{slot\_states}\) for the two components of \(C@\mathit{path}.\mathit{state}\).

\paragraph{The many roles of \ensuremath{\mathit{z}}: classifying, annotating, and ranking}

We write \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} as a single partial function, but it actually has three parts.
Mohan et al.\ keep one total transaction for the whole tree, returning an entire root-to-leaf path of indices and ranks.
Ours is per-node and partial, and it does not pick the index.
Distinct leaves carry distinct flows (\cref{sec:policy-dsl} makes this a validity condition), so a packet's leaf is settled before \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} is consulted.
The payload is fixed by the tree's shape, and \ensuremath{\mathit{z}}'s job is only to rank it.

\begin{itemize}
\item The \emph{classifier} answers whether this node accepts a given packet and where it sends it.
It has a \emph{service map}, written \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves}\), which is a partial map from flows to payloads.  
At an internal node, \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves}(f)\) is a unique child index, and following it brings a packet of flow \(f\) one step closer to its leaf.
At a leaf the payload is the packet itself, so the map is the trivial one on the single flow that leaf carries.
Its \emph{admitted set}, written \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{admit} \subseteq \mathrm{dom}(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves})\), is the subset of the flows that the node currently accepts.
\item The \emph{per-arm metadata}, written \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{meta}\), records what this node's discipline needs to know about each of its arms, such as priorities or weights.
A leaf has no arms, and so no metadata.
\item The \emph{ranker}, written \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{rank}\), is the scheduling logic that the node's discipline contributes.
Its inputs are the node's \ensuremath{\mathit{state}}, the chosen payload, and that payload's arm metadatum.
From this it mints the rank and the updated \ensuremath{\mathit{state}}.
A node's ranker names the discipline that the node runs.
\end{itemize}

So \(\mathit{z}(\mathit{state}, p)\) is defined exactly when \(\mathit{flow}(p) \in \mathit{z}.\mathit{admit}\). 
It then finds \(p\)'s payload in \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves}\), and pairs that payload with the rank that \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{rank}\) computes.

\ensuremath{\mathit{state}} and \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} change as the node serves traffic, whereas \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} changes only when an edit rewrites it.
The per-arm metadata also changes only in the second way.

\paragraph{\push{} and \pop{}}

A control exposes exactly two operations to the network:
\[
\push{} : \mathit{control} \times \mathit{Pkt} \to \mathit{control},
\qquad
\pop{} : \mathit{control} \rightharpoonup \mathit{Pkt} \times \mathit{control}.
\]
A \push{} offers the packet in parallel to every node on its root-to-leaf path, and each of those nodes enqueues the payload its own \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} returns, at the rank \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} mints for it.
Ranks are fixed at \push{} time and never revised.
A \pop{} releases the best-ranked packet, resolving ``best-ranked'' from the top down.
It releases the best-ranked index at the root, follows that index to a child, recurses, and releases a packet once it reaches a leaf.
It consults \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} contents alone, never \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} or \ensuremath{\mathit{z}}.

Because an internal entry is only an index, the index enqueued on account of one packet may end up releasing a different, better-ranked packet that later joined the same leaf.
This deferral is what lets a PIFO tree reorder buffered traffic in ways a single PIFO cannot.
% The above is all true, but it's totally irrelevant stated here?
These two are the \emph{only} observable interactions with a scheduler, which is why we frame an \emph{atomic} transition in terms of \push{}/\pop{} observability.

\paragraph{Well-formedness}

A control \(C\) is \emph{well-formed} (written \(\vdash C\)) when, at every internal node of \(C\), the \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} has, for each legal child index \(i\), exactly as many occurrences of \(i\) as there are packets stored in the leaf pifos of the subtree under the \(i\)-th child.
A control that fails this holds an index at some node with no packet under the corresponding child, so a \pop{} that follows that index down reaches a leaf with nothing to emit.

\paragraph{What a discipline supplies}

A discipline \ensuremath{D} is the four-tuple of \cref{fig:discipline}: a metadata set \(M_{D}\), a node initializer, a slot initializer, and a ranker.
Supplying those four is the whole interface, and an operator can ``bring their own'' \ensuremath{D}.

\(\mathit{init\_node}_{D}\) runs once per node when a \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} is compiled into a control, seeding that node's \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}}.
\(\mathit{init\_slot}_{D}\) runs once per arm, at compile time and again whenever an edit splices a new arm under an already-running \ensuremath{D}-parent (\cref{sec:add}), taking the parent's current \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}} and the new arm's metadatum.

Two obligations attend the exchange.
The first is path-consistency of admission: a packet's flow must be admitted at every node on that flow's root-to-leaf path, or at none of them.
This is a global property rather than a concern of any node-local \ensuremath{\mathit{z}}, and it is what keeps \(\vdash\) true under compilation and under every \(\delta\).
The second is that \(\mathit{rank}_{D}\) records which discipline the node runs, and not merely how that discipline ranks.
Two disciplines that happen to schedule alike therefore stay distinct, which is what lets \cref{sec:equivalences} recover the operator's own \ensuremath{D} from a running control.

\subsection{A Grammar for Policy Tree Edits}\label{sec:delta-grammar}

We fix a small grammar \(\delta\) of \emph{policy tree edits}, which are structural rewrites that transform one running policy tree into another.
Every form has two readings, pinned down form by form in \cref{sec:edit-semantics}.
\(\csem{\delta} : \mathit{control} \rightharpoonup \mathit{control}\) rewrites the running control, and \(\psem{\delta} : \mathit{pol} \rightharpoonup \mathit{pol}\) names that same edit's effect on the operator's policy in closed form.

Three properties shape the choice of forms.
They are \emph{atomic}, since a form is admitted only if the hardware substrate can commit \(\csem{\delta}\) as a single instant (\cref{sec:hardware}).
They are \emph{explainable}, since \(\psem{\delta}\) states every form's effect back to the operator in the DSL they wrote, so the planner can report which policy change it is about to make and echo it back for approval (\cref{sec:planner}).
And they are \emph{complete}, since the grammar can express a sequence that reaches any \(p_2\) from any \(p_1\), which \cref{sec:fallback} shows by exhibiting one that always works.

An edit describes \emph{where} in the tree the change lands and \emph{what} the change is.
Throughout this section \(p_1\) is the presently running policy, 
and \(p_1@\mathit{path}\) is the subtree of \(p_1\) reached by following \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} down from its root.

\subsection{Equivalences, and Reading Off the Running Policy}\label{sec:equivalences}

We write \(C \sim C'\) for the equivalence on well-formed controls that holds when the two are identical except in their live \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} contents and \ensuremath{\mathit{state}}s.
Two \(\sim\)-related controls thus have the same tree shape and the same \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} at every node, and differ at most in the transient live state that traffic leaves behind.
Serving \push{}/\pop{} operations is one way controls come to be \(\sim\)-related.

We write \(p \eqR p'\) for the smallest congruence on \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} such that, at any internal \ensuremath{D}-node, permuting the child arms gives a congruent \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}.
Each arm's metadatum is tagged to the arm, so it travels with the arm under the permutation.
The metadata is what carries the scheduling-meaningful content, so such a permutation does not change the scheduler.

Because \(\eqR\) only permutes sibling arms, each \(\eqR\)-class has a canonical representative under any fixed ordering of the arms at a node.

We write \(C \simR C'\) for the equivalence obtained by closing \(\sim\) under sibling permutations.
Permuting a node's children renumbers the slots they sit in, so the parent's \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} entries, its \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_states}}, its \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves}\), and its \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{meta}\) are all renumbered to match.
The two controls present different positional layouts and realize the same scheduler.
Every \(\sim\)-equivalent pair is \(\simR\)-equivalent, but not conversely.

\paragraph{The bridge: \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\)}

We write \(\lfloor C \rfloor\) to mean ``the \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} that \(C\) realizes''.
Intuitively, \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) reads the policy straight off the control.
It walks the control's tree and recovers, node by node, the \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} term the control implements, discarding the live \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} and \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} contents that traffic happens to have left behind.
We define it by structural recursion on \(C\), with one clause for leaves and one for internal nodes.
Both clauses read out of \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} (\cref{sec:controls}).
A leaf is named by the flow its classifier serves.
An internal node is named by the discipline its ranker names, and its arms are annotated out of its metadata.

\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
\lfloor C \rfloor = f,\ \text{when \(C\) is a leaf and } \mathrm{dom}(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves}) = \{f\};\\[4pt]
\lfloor C \rfloor = D(m_1{:}\,\lfloor c_1 \rfloor, \ldots, m_n{:}\,\lfloor c_n \rfloor),\\
\qquad \text{when \(C\) is internal with children } c_1 \ldots c_n,\\
\qquad \mathit{z}.\mathit{rank} \text{ names } D, \text{ and } m_i = \mathit{z}.\mathit{meta}(i).
\end{array}
\]

The recursion runs on a finite tree and consults neither \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} nor \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} nor \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{admit}\), so it returns a single value.
\xxx[AM to AS]{You suggested defining \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) as the inverse of \(\lceil \cdot \rceil\), and then worried that \(\lceil \cdot \rceil\) is not invertible if one is strict about sibling order.
\(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) is now a structural recursion on \(C\), so it is total and single-valued by construction, and no inverse has to be argued for.
The three items below are then consequences rather than defining rules, and the third of them is a theorem.}
Every well-formed control therefore determines one \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}, independently of how that control was reached, and we may write \(\lfloor C \rfloor\) and use it as a function.

Three facts follow.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \emph{Compiler correctness.} Compiling a \(p\) to a control and reading it straight back recovers \(p\) itself, up to the sibling-order freedom the compiler enjoyed in laying it out: \(\lfloor \lceil p \rceil \rfloor \eqR p\).
This is immediate from the compilation of \cref{sec:compilation}, which installs \ensuremath{D}'s ranker at each internal node, copies each arm's annotation into \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{meta}\), and has each leaf serve its own flow.
The relation is \(\eqR\) rather than \(=\) precisely because the compiler can choose arm order when creating \(\lceil p \rceil\).
\item \emph{Insensitivity to live contents.} \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) reads only the tree shape, each leaf's served flow, and each internal node's ranker-discipline and per-arm metadata.
It never reads \ensuremath{\mathit{state}}, \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}}, or \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{admit}\).
So \(\sim\)-related controls, which share shape and \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} outright, are equal when read off using \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\). 
Thus, if \(C \sim C'\), then \(\lfloor C \rfloor = \lfloor C' \rfloor\).
\item \emph{Closure under \(\delta\).} A form's two readings (\cref{sec:delta-grammar}) agree.
Editing the control and reading it off gives the same \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}, up to sibling order, as applying the \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}-level effect to the read-off, so \(\lfloor \csem{\delta}(C) \rfloor \eqR \psem{\delta}(\lfloor C \rfloor)\).
This theorem is proved one form at a time in \cref{sec:per-form} and collected as \cref{thm:interplay}.
The relation is again \(\eqR\), not \(=\), because \(\csem{\delta}\) may lay the edited node's children out in any sibling order.
\end{enumerate}

This is how we will discharge Obligation 1 of \cref{sec:intro}.
Even when the presently running \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} is just an intermediate policy that the runtime crafted on the way from the running policy to the operator's true request, we can still read off and report to the operator exactly what is running.
\cref{sec:interplay} takes up the third fact and proves it, as the ``commuting square'' these pieces trace out.

\subsection{Edit Semantics}\label{sec:edit-semantics}

This section gives each form of \(\delta\) its two readings.
\(\csem{\delta}\) says what the form does to the running control, and \(\psem{\delta}\) says what it does to the operator's policy.
Both are stated here without proof, together with the preconditions under which the form is defined.

\cref{sec:correctness} takes up what has to be shown about them, form by form in \cref{sec:per-form}.

\paragraph{Notation}

The pol-level rules below read, overwrite, and shrink child lists.
A \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} node's child list \(\mathit{ts}\) holds \emph{annotated arms}, each a metadatum paired with a policy (\cref{sec:policy-dsl}).
We write \(\mathit{ts}[i]\) for the \(i\)-th arm, and \(\mathit{ts}[t/i]\) for \(\mathit{ts}\) with the \emph{policy} of its \(i\)-th arm overwritten by \(t\).
That arm keeps its annotation, and the arity does not change.
\(\mathit{ts}[-/k]\) drops the \(k\)-th arm, and later arms shift one slot to the left.
Indices follow the \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} convention of \cref{sec:delta-grammar}.

The control-level rules walk controls the same way.
Nodes are written \(\langle\mathit{state}, \mathit{pifo}, \mathit{z}\rangle(\mathit{cs})\), as in \cref{sec:controls}, and we reuse \(\mathit{cs}[C'/i]\) and \(\mathit{cs}\mathbin{+\!\!+}[C']\) for the list operations on the child list \(\mathit{cs}\).

Two invariants link a node's classifier to the tree, and every form preserves both.
First, \(\mathrm{dom}(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves})\) at a node is exactly the set of flows served by the leaves beneath it, and \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves}\) sends each of them to the child that leads on toward its leaf.
Second, \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{admit} \subseteq \mathrm{dom}(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves})\), with the containment strict exactly where a flow has been silenced but not yet removed, and admission is path-consistent (\cref{sec:compilation}).
Splicing in a new leaf, for instance, means extending both parts at each node along that leaf's path so the new flow is served and admitted.
Those nodes' \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}}, \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_states}}, ranker, and per-arm metadata are left untouched.
% The above is throat-clearing. Crunch it significantly and put it where applicable inside Add, Quiesce, Remove

A form's path names a target node, but it is often more convenient to work with the target's parent and the index the parent uses to reach it.
We recover both by splitting the given path as \(\pi \mathbin{+\!\!+} [k]\): \(\pi\) is the path to the parent, and \(k\) is the child index by which the parent reaches the target.
We also order paths by prefix, writing \(\nu \sqsubseteq \tau\) when \(\nu\) is a prefix of \(\tau\), so that \(C@\nu\) is an ancestor of \(C@\tau\) or is \(C@\tau\) itself, and \(\nu \sqsubset \tau\) for the strict version.
% I think you explanation will land more smoothly if you give the strict version first by example, and then give the loose version

Most forms edit only the node that their path points to.
For such a form we use a small combinator: given a node transformer \(g\) and a path \(\pi\), write \(\mathtt{at}(\pi, g)\) for the operation that walks to the node at \(\pi\), applies \(g\) there, and copies every other node of the tree unchanged:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
\mathtt{at}([\,], g)\,(N) = g(N)\\
\mathtt{at}(i \mathbin{::} \mathit{rest}, g)\,\langle\mathit{state},\mathit{pifo},\mathit{z}\rangle(\mathit{cs}) =\\
\quad \langle\mathit{state},\mathit{pifo},\mathit{z}\rangle(\mathit{cs}[\,\mathtt{at}(\mathit{rest}, g)(\mathit{cs}[i]) / i\,])
\end{array}
\]
The recursive case walks one step down and writes the result back, carrying \(\langle\mathit{state},\mathit{pifo},\mathit{z}\rangle\) unchanged.
The same combinator serves on \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}, where a node is \(D(\mathit{ts})\).
There the descent into arm \(i\) rewrites that arm's policy and carries its annotation along.
We also write \(\mathtt{at}(V, g)\) for the set analogue, applying \(g\) at every node in \(V\) and copying every node outside \(V\) unchanged.

Three operations recur across the forms, and the first two edit a single node's \ensuremath{\mathit{z}}.
A node's discipline fixes a packet's rank from the local \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} and the chosen child alone, not from the packet itself, so admitting a flow at an already-running node calls for no new rank rule.
\(\mathtt{extend}(\mathit{z}, F, i)\) therefore touches the classifier and nothing else, recording that child \(i\) is where \(F\) is served and adding \(F\) to the admitted set.
\(\mathtt{setmeta}(\mathit{z}, i, m)\) annotates arm \(i\) with \(m\) and leaves the classifier and the ranker alone:
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
\mathtt{extend}(\mathit{z}, F, i).\mathit{serves} = \mathit{z}.\mathit{serves}\,[\,f \mapsto i \;\text{for}\; f \in F\,],\\[3pt]
\mathtt{extend}(\mathit{z}, F, i).\mathit{admit} = \mathit{z}.\mathit{admit} \cup F,\\[3pt]
\mathtt{setmeta}(\mathit{z}, i, m).\mathit{meta} = \mathit{z}.\mathit{meta}\,[\,i \mapsto m\,].
\end{array}
\]
Every other field of \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} is carried through unchanged.
A form that admits a new set of flows must extend every node on the way down to its target, so \(\mathtt{extend}\) is also applied as a walk.
At each proper ancestor \(C@\nu\) of the target \(C@\tau\) the walk replaces \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} by \(\mathtt{extend}(\mathit{z}, F, i)\), where \(i\) is the index by which \(\nu\) continues toward \(\tau\), and it copies every other node unchanged.

\Add{} comes first and is given in full detail.
The six that follow lean on it, and we point out only where each one departs from \Add's pattern.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\Add(\pi, D, \mathit{arm}, m)\)}{Add}}\label{sec:add}


\Add{}'s arguments are \(\pi : \mathit{path}\), the node that will receive the new arm as its last child; \(D\), the discipline that node runs; \(\mathit{arm} : \mathit{pol}\), the policy of the new arm; and \(m \in M_{D}\), the metadatum that tells \ensuremath{D} how to schedule that arm (\cref{sec:policy-dsl}).

\paragraph{Definition}

We must define \(\csem{\Add(\pi, D, \mathit{arm}, m)} : \mathit{control} \rightharpoonup \mathit{control}\) together with the preconditions on the already-running control.

Say the presently running control is \(C\).
Intuitively, \Add{} inserts a freshly compiled subtree \(\lceil \mathit{arm} \rceil\) as the new last child of the subtree \(C@\pi\).
For traffic to reach the new arm, the flows it serves, those bound for leaves inside it, must be admitted along the whole path.
Every node from the root down to \(C@\pi\) must begin to accept the new flows and send them toward the arm.
% This should be swum up to the be the intuition paragraph after the arguments are explained

\(\csem{\Add(\pi, D, \mathit{arm}, m)}(C)\) is defined when three things hold.
The path \(\pi\) resolves in \(C\) to an internal node running discipline \ensuremath{D}.
The metadatum \(m\) is drawn from \(M_{D}\).
And \ensuremath{\mathit{arm}}'s leaf labels are disjoint from those of \(C\).
The disjointness precondition rules out \emph{splitting} an already-running flow.
Going from a policy with a single \textsf{gmail} leaf to one that splits it into \textsf{gmail\_business} and \textsf{gmail\_personal} would have to re-sort the packets already buffered under \textsf{gmail} into the two new leaves, relocating admitted traffic; \Add{} only introduces flows disjoint from what is running, so it never has to relocate buffered traffic.
% It would be good to give a forward pointer to the idiom section where this _does_ get done. And at that spot, make sure that we quickly handle this example.

We define \(\csem{\Add}\) by recursion on \(\pi\), writing \(\mathtt{flows}(\mathit{arm})\) for the set of flows at \(\lceil \mathit{arm} \rceil\)'s leaves:
{\small
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
\csem{\Add([\,],\ D,\ \mathit{arm},\ m)}\ \langle\mathit{state},\mathit{pifo},\mathit{z}\rangle(\mathit{cs}) \;=\\
\quad \langle\mathit{state'},\mathit{pifo},z'\rangle(\mathit{cs}\mathbin{+\!\!+}[\lceil\mathit{arm}\rceil])\\
\quad \textbf{where}\ k=|\mathit{cs}|\ \text{(the next free slot)}\\
% "(the next free slot)" in unacceptable in a figure
\quad \phantom{\textbf{where}}\ \mathit{state'}=(\mathit{node\_state},\ \mathit{slot\_states}\mathbin{+\!\!+}[s])\\
\quad \phantom{\textbf{where}}\ s=\mathit{init\_slot}_{D}(\mathit{node\_state},m)\\
\quad \phantom{\textbf{where}}\ z'=\mathtt{setmeta}(\mathtt{extend}(\mathit{z},\mathtt{flows}(\mathit{arm}),k),k,m)\\[8pt]
\csem{\Add(i\mathbin{::}\mathit{rest},\ D,\ \mathit{arm},\ m)}\ \langle\mathit{state},\mathit{pifo},\mathit{z}\rangle(\mathit{cs}) \;=\\
\quad \langle\mathit{state},\mathit{pifo},z'\rangle(\mathit{cs}[\,c/i\,])\\
\quad \textbf{where}\ c=\csem{\Add(\mathit{rest},\ D,\ \mathit{arm},\ m)}(\mathit{cs}[i])\\
\quad \phantom{\textbf{where}}\ z'=\mathtt{extend}(\mathit{z},\mathtt{flows}(\mathit{arm}),i)
\end{array}
\]
}
The arguments \ensuremath{D}, \ensuremath{\mathit{arm}}, and \(m\) thread unchanged through the recursion, and \(m\) is consumed only at the base case, which annotates the new slot and seeds its bookkeeping.
Since \(C@\pi\) has \(k\) children before the splice, appending the new one lands at the next free index \(k\), and because \(\mathtt{flows}(\mathit{arm})\) lies outside \(\mathrm{dom}(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves})\), each \(\mathtt{extend}\) is conflict-free.
% Idk why you write about k above; the recursive case is not interested in k
The recursive case is exactly the \(\mathtt{extend}\) walk of \cref{sec:edit-semantics}, carrying \(\mathtt{flows}(\mathit{arm})\) down to \(\pi\).

The base case (\(\pi = []\)) has reached the parent of the new arm, where it does three things.
It appends \(\lceil \mathit{arm} \rceil\) as the last child, at slot \(k\).
It appends that slot's bookkeeping, \(\mathit{init\_slot}_{D}(\mathit{node\_state}, m)\), using the discipline \ensuremath{D} that the form carries.
And it rewrites the node's transaction, extending the classifier to send the new flows to the new child and annotating slot \(k\) with \(m\).
Meanwhile, \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}} and \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} are untouched.
Appending the arm is legitimate because our arm-order freedom (\cref{sec:policy-dsl}) lets us place the new child last.
The recursive case steps into the on-path child \(i\), writes the result back, and extends this ancestor's transaction to \(\mathtt{extend}(\mathit{z}, \mathtt{flows}(\mathit{arm}), i)\), sending the new flows one step further down toward \(\pi\); its \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}}, \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_states}}, and \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} are carried unchanged.

So every node on the path from the root to \(C@\pi\) has its transaction extended to admit and route the new flows, and nothing else about those nodes changes.
The new flows were admitted nowhere before (by disjointness), so each extension is conflict-free and no in-flight packet's route moves.

\paragraph{Pol-level reading}

We define \(\psem{\cdot}\) by recursion on \(\pi\) as well:
{\small
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
\psem{\Add([\,],\ D,\ \mathit{arm},\ m)}\ (D\ \mathit{ts}) \;=\; D(\mathit{ts}\mathbin{+\!\!+}[m{:}\,\mathit{arm}])\\[4pt]
\psem{\Add(i\mathbin{::}\mathit{rest},\ D,\ \mathit{arm},\ m)}\ (E\ \mathit{ts}) \;=\\
\quad E(\mathit{ts}[\,\psem{\Add(\mathit{rest},\ D,\ \mathit{arm},\ m)}(\mathit{ts}[i])/i\,])
\end{array}
\]
}
Its node patterns \(D\ \mathit{ts}\) and \(E\ \mathit{ts}\) split a \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} node into its discipline and child list \(\mathit{ts}\).
The base case reuses the argument name \ensuremath{D}, firing only when the reached node runs that discipline; an ancestor's discipline \(E\) is unconstrained and simply carried along.
The metadatum \(m\) appears on both sides.
\(\csem{\Add}\) records it at \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{meta}(k)\), and \(\psem{\Add}\) writes it onto the new arm, so reading the edited control back recovers the annotation the operator asked for.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\ChangeMeta(\tau, m)\)}{ChangeMeta}}\label{sec:changemeta}


\ChangeMeta{} re-tunes a single already-running arm, resetting the metadatum its parent schedules it by, without moving a packet or disturbing any sibling.

\(\tau : \mathit{path}\) is the target arm.
The path is non-empty (the target arm is required to have a parent), and we destruct it as \(\pi \mathbin{+\!\!+} [k]\), so \(k\) is the arm's slot at its parent \(C@\pi\).
The argument \(m\) is a fresh value from \(M_{D}\), where \ensuremath{D} is the discipline the parent runs (\cref{sec:policy-dsl}).

\paragraph{Definition}

\ChangeMeta{} is defined when \(\tau\) resolves in \(C\) to an arm whose parent runs \ensuremath{D}, and \(m \in M_{D}\).
Its node transformer \(\mathtt{setmeta}_k\) rewrites the node's \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} and nothing else:
{\small
\[
\mathtt{setmeta}_k\ \langle\mathit{state},\mathit{pifo},\mathit{z}\rangle(\mathit{cs}) = \langle\mathit{state},\mathit{pifo},\ \mathtt{setmeta}(\mathit{z},k,m)\rangle(\mathit{cs}).
\]
}
The form applies that transformer at the parent and nowhere else:
\[
\csem{\ChangeMeta(\pi \mathbin{+\!\!+} [k],\, m)} = \mathtt{at}(\pi,\, \mathtt{setmeta}_k).
\]
Under \Strict{} the arm's metadatum is its priority, and under \WFQ{} it is its weight.
That value alone becomes \(m\).
The evolving per-arm bookkeeping beside it, \WFQ's last-finish tag in \(\mathit{slot\_states}[k]\), is left untouched (the Note below says why).

\paragraph{Pol-level reading}

\ChangeMeta{} reannotates arm \(k\) and leaves that arm's policy, its siblings, and the discipline \ensuremath{D} alone:
\[
\psem{\ChangeMeta(\pi \mathbin{+\!\!+} [k],\, m)} = \mathtt{at}\big(\pi,\; D(\ldots, m_k{:}\,t_k, \ldots) \mapsto D(\ldots, m{:}\,t_k, \ldots)\big).
\]

\paragraph{Note: Why the virtual-finish tag is preserved (WFQ)}

When the parent runs \WFQ, the virtual-finish tag at slot \(k\) is deliberately \emph{preserved} and not \emph{reset}.
Resetting it would remove the targeted arm from the current round, rewarding or penalizing it for no reason the operator asked for.
\(\mathit{init\_slot}_{\WFQ}\) (\cref{sec:compilation}) was designed to let a fresh arm join the current round without disturbing its siblings, and a weight change on an already-running arm deserves the same treatment.
\cref{sec:controls} already draws this line.
The weight is configuration and lives in \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{meta}\), while the tag is bookkeeping and lives in \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_states}}.
\ChangeMeta{} reaches only the first of the two.
Under \Strict{} the arm carries no comparable round-bookkeeping, so the question does not arise.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\Quiesce(\tau)\)}{Quiesce}}\label{sec:quiesce}


\(\tau : \mathit{path}\) is the path to the target subtree to silence.

Say the presently running control is \(C\).
\Quiesce{} strikes every flow served under \(C@\tau\) from the admitted sets of the nodes those flows traverse, so that no packet bound for a leaf there is admitted anywhere.
It leaves every node's routing in place, so buffered packets can still be popped out.

\paragraph{Definition}

\(\csem{\Quiesce(\tau)}(C)\) is defined whenever \(\tau\) resolves in \(C\) (the empty path silences the whole tree).
Let \(L = \mathtt{flows}(C@\tau)\) be the flows to silence.
Write \(\mathit{z} \setminus L\) for \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} with \(L\) struck from its admitted set, so \((\mathit{z} \setminus L).\mathit{admit} = \mathit{z}.\mathit{admit} \setminus L\).
Its service map and its ranker are unchanged.
The nodes that those flows traverse are the spine down to \(\tau\) together with \(\tau\)'s whole subtree:
\[
V_\tau = \{\, C@\nu \;:\; \nu \sqsubseteq \tau \,\} \;\cup\; \{\, \text{every node of } C@\tau \,\}.
\] 
\Quiesce{} narrows the admitted set at each of these nodes and touches nothing else, through the node transformer
\[
g_L\ \langle\mathit{state},\mathit{pifo},\mathit{z}\rangle(\mathit{cs}) = \langle\mathit{state},\mathit{pifo},\ \mathit{z} \setminus L\rangle(\mathit{cs}),
\]
applied at every node of \(V_\tau\):
\[
\csem{\Quiesce(\tau)} = \mathtt{at}(V_\tau,\ g_L).
\]
Every field other than \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{admit}\), and every node outside \(V_\tau\), is left unchanged; the Note below says why the restriction must reach all of \(V_\tau\) rather than the root alone.
% Just promote the note here and get it over with

\paragraph{Pol-level reading}

\(\psem{\Quiesce(\tau)}\) is the identity on \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}.

\paragraph{Note: Why touch every node on every quiesced push path}

Under \cref{sec:controls}'s parallel \push{}, every node on the path from the system root to a packet's destination leaf mints an index via its local \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} independently of the others.
If we restricted \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} only at a subset of those nodes (say, only the root of the tree, or only inside \(C@\tau\)), the rest would happily mint indices and enqueue them.
The tree would then be malformed, and future \pop{} would get stuck.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\Remove(\tau)\)}{Remove}}\label{sec:remove}

As before, \(\tau : \mathit{path}\) is the non-empty target arm; destruct it as \(\pi \mathbin{+\!\!+} [k]\).
\Remove{} unhooks that arm and renumbers higher siblings down by one; it fires only when the arm's subtree is empty (see note below).

\paragraph{Definition}

As in \cref{sec:quiesce}, let \(L = \mathtt{flows}(C@\tau)\), here the flows that the doomed arm serves.
Write \(\mathit{z} \setminus\!\!\setminus L\) for \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} with \(L\) struck from its service map, and hence, by the containment of \cref{sec:controls}, from its admitted set too.
% It would be good to find a nice glyph that looks like \setminus\!\!\setminus but more compressed together
Its ranker is unchanged.
\Remove{} drops the arm at its parent and strikes \(L\) from the service map of every proper ancestor above that parent, the set \(A_\pi = \{\, C@\nu \;:\; \nu \sqsubset \pi \,\}\).
It is defined when \(\tau\) resolves in \(C\) and the subtree at \(\tau\) is empty.
Dropping arm \(k\) shifts every later arm one slot to the left, and we name that shift \(\sigma_k\): the map on child indices that fixes every \(i < k\) and sends every \(i > k\) to \(i-1\).
The node transformer \(\mathtt{drop}_k\) unhooks child \(k\) and renumbers what named it:
{\small
\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
\mathtt{drop}_k\ \langle(\mathit{node\_state}, \mathit{ss}),\ \mathit{pifo},\ \mathit{z}\rangle(\mathit{cs}) \;=\\
\quad \langle(\mathit{node\_state},\ \mathit{ss}[-/k]),\ \sigma_k(\mathit{pifo}),\ z'\rangle(\mathit{cs}[-/k])\\
\quad \textbf{where}\ z'.\mathit{serves} = \sigma_k \circ (\mathit{z} \setminus\!\!\setminus L).\mathit{serves}\\
\quad \phantom{\textbf{where}}\ z'.\mathit{admit} = (\mathit{z} \setminus\!\!\setminus L).\mathit{admit}\\
\quad \phantom{\textbf{where}}\ z'.\mathit{meta} = \mathit{z}.\mathit{meta} \circ \sigma_k^{-1}\\
\quad \phantom{\textbf{where}}\ z'.\mathit{rank} = \mathit{z}.\mathit{rank}
\end{array}
\]
}
The form drops the arm at its parent and retracts \(L\) at every proper ancestor:
\[
\csem{\Remove(\pi \mathbin{+\!\!+} [k])} = \mathtt{at}(A_\pi,\ \mathit{z} \setminus\!\!\setminus L) \circ \mathtt{at}(\pi,\, \mathtt{drop}_k).
\]
Emptiness and \(\vdash C\) force \(C@\pi.\mathit{pifo}\) to hold \emph{no entry} equal to \(k\), so \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} loses no entry, and surviving entries keep their child.
\ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}}, and every field of every node outside \(C@\pi\) and the removed subtree other than the ancestor service maps just named, is unchanged.
\xxx[AM to Zhiyuan]{Does Rio's substrate actually key children by stable handles, so \Remove{} renumbers no live entry? If so, we can say so plainly here.}

\paragraph{Note: Why the ancestors are retracted too}

Dropping the arm at its parent alone would leave every node above still mapping \(L\) toward it.
A later \push{} of an \(L\)-packet would then be admitted at an ancestor and enqueue an index there, while the parent, which no longer serves \(L\), would enqueue nothing.
This would leave \(\vdash\) broken.
\Remove{} retracts unconditionally, so that it is sound on its own even when no \Quiesce{} has run, as when it undoes an \Add{} whose arm never carried traffic.

\paragraph{Pol-level reading}

\Remove{} drops arm \(k\) from the parent \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} node:
\[
\psem{\Remove(\pi \mathbin{+\!\!+} [k])} = \mathtt{at}(\pi,\; D(\mathit{ts}) \mapsto D(\mathit{ts}[-/k])).
\]

Deleting an \emph{occupied} child has no canonical local realization.
Consider the tree \(P(Q(A, B), C)\) of \cref{fig:remove-orig}, whose subtrees \(A\), \(B\), \(C\) are drawn blue, orange, and green.
Annotations on the arms show how a parent refers to a child.
The tree is well-formed.
Say the user asks us to delete \(B\), dropping its packets.
To restore well-formedness, we need to remove two instances of the index \(1\) from \(Q\)'s PIFO and two instances of the index \(0\) from \(P\)'s PIFO.

At \(Q\) the edit is unambiguous.
Well-formedness of the original tree forces \(Q\)'s PIFO to have exactly two instances of \(1\), and we delete those two.
The trouble is one level up.
\(P\)'s PIFO originally had four instances of \(0\), and well-formedness demands that we remove two of them.
But \emph{which} two?
No entry in \(P\) carries the meta-information ``I was enqueued when a packet was inserted into \(B\)''; an entry \(0\) in \(P\) only means ``when this index is popped, recursively ask subtree \(Q\) to emit the next packet'' (see \cref{sec:hierarchical}).
This absence of meta-information is a \emph{feature} of PIFO trees, not a defect: an entry is deliberately detached from the packet it was enqueued with, which is what lets each node schedule its own discipline in isolation and lets a subtree be reconfigured without rewriting its ancestors.

Given this, there is no obvious or operator-provided answer: whichever two instances of \(0\) we drop, the choice is itself a scheduling decision, since it affects how \(P\) intermixes \(C\) and \(Q\) traffic.
Keeping the two leftmost \(0\)s (\cref{fig:remove-leftmost}), five pops drain \(P\) and yield \(c_1, c_2, a_1, c_3, a_2\).

Keeping the two rightmost \(0\)s instead (\cref{fig:remove-rightmost}), the same five pops yield \(a_1, c_1, a_2, c_2, c_3\).
The \emph{choice of which} \(0\)s to drop \emph{silently reschedules \(A\) against \(C\)}, and nothing in the operator's deletion request records what the operator wanted.

We do not wish to make a scheduling decision for the operator, and the only way to avoid one is to drain the whole tree, filter the \(B\)-packets, and re-push, which is a stop-the-world rebuild.

Our grammar sidesteps the question entirely, by asking that a targeted subtree be empty before it is deleted (\cref{sec:sequences}).
At the instant \Remove{} fires, no entry above the doomed arm was enqueued on account of a packet inside it, so no entry has to be dropped and the structural deletion is unambiguous.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\Designate(\tau, \mathit{survivor})\)}{Designate}}\label{sec:designate}


\(\tau : \mathit{path}\) is the path to the target subtree being wrapped, and \(\mathit{survivor} : \mathit{pol}\) is the designated-survivor policy that becomes the wrap's low-priority arm.

Say the presently running control is \(C\).
Intuitively, \Designate{} replaces \(C@\tau\) in place with \(\Strictstar(C@\tau, \lceil \mathit{survivor} \rceil)\).
It is the mechanism behind non-disruptive in-place replacement (\cref{fig:designate}).
The wrap lets \ensuremath{\mathit{survivor}} begin accepting new traffic on a low-priority arm, while the old subtree keeps draining, untouched, on a high-priority one.
Once the old subtree is empty, \Undesignate{} (\cref{sec:undesignate}) removes it and promotes \ensuremath{\mathit{survivor}} in its place.
That is why the form exists: no single edit can swap one subtree for another without either dropping the old subtree's backlog or stalling the new arm, but the \(\Strictstar\) wrap lets both coexist for the length of a drain.

\(\Strictstar\) is the same discipline as \(\Strict\), distinguished only by its name: a node's ranker names \(\Strictstar\) where an ordinary strict-priority node would name \Strict{} (\cref{sec:controls}).
\Designate{} installs a ranker of the first kind, and \Undesignate{} retires the node that carries it.
Semantically the two are identical; every push, pop, and well-formedness check treats \(\Strictstar(A, B)\) exactly as \(\Strict(A, B)\), so the obligations below are stated against the ordinary \(\Strict\) semantics of \cref{sec:policy-dsl}.
The star exists only so that \Undesignate's precondition (``\(\tau\) lands on a \(\Strictstar\)'') can be stated structurally.
The \cref{sec:policy-dsl} DSL in which the operator writes policies can parse only \(\Strict\), never \(\Strictstar\), so a \(\Strictstar\) is unreachable in any user-written \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} and arises only in the middle of a planner sequence, between a \Designate{} and its eventual \Undesignate.
The wrap is therefore \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}-visible only as a \(\Strict\).

\Designate{} wraps the target and admits \ensuremath{\mathit{survivor}}'s flows at every ancestor on the way down.
That walk is \Add's, with \ensuremath{\mathit{survivor}}'s flows in place of the new arm's.
The two differ only at the target, since \Add{} appends an arm there while \Designate{} wraps the node it has reached.
The transformer \(\mathtt{wrap}\) replaces \(M\) with a fresh \(\Strictstar\) node \(N\) over the two arms \([\,M,\ \lceil\mathit{survivor}\rceil\,]\), giving the old arm strict priority over the new arm:

\quad s_N = (\,n_N,\ [\,\mathit{init\_slot}_{\Strict}(n_N, \mathtt{hi}),\\
\quad\qquad\qquad\ \ \mathit{init\_slot}_{\Strict}(n_N, \mathtt{lo})\,]\,),\\[3pt]
\quad q_N = \text{the \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} of } P_0 \text{ copies of the entry } (0,\ \mathtt{hi}),\\[3pt]
\quad \mathit{z}_N.\mathit{meta} = \{\, 0 \mapsto \mathtt{hi},\ 1 \mapsto \mathtt{lo} \,\},\\[3pt]
\quad \mathit{z}_N(p) = (1,\ \mathtt{lo})\ \text{if}\ \mathit{flow}(p) \in \mathtt{flows}(\mathit{survivor}),\ \text{else}\ (0,\ \mathtt{hi}).
\end{array}
\]
}
In the vocabulary of \cref{sec:controls}, \(\mathit{z}_N\) serves \ensuremath{\mathit{survivor}}'s flows at arm \(1\) and everything else the node serves at arm \(0\), and admits both.
Its ranker names \(\Strictstar\) rather than \Strict, which is what \cref{sec:undesignate}'s \Undesignate{} later looks for.
Arm \(0\) is \(M\) (\textsf{old}), unchanged down to its whole subtree and buffered packets; arm \(1\) is the freshly compiled \(\lceil\mathit{survivor}\rceil\) (\textsf{new}), initially empty.
This is \cref{fig:designate-after}.
\(N\)'s index-\ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} holds \(P_0\) entries for arm \(0\) and none for arm \(1\), one per packet buffered under \textsf{old}.
That backlog therefore keeps popping through the wrap ahead of any later arrival, unchanged, while \textsf{new} fills only as fresh traffic arrives (\cref{fig:designate-drain}).
Because \(\mathtt{at}\) writes \(N\) back into the parent's slot \(k\), the parent's \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{meta}(k)\) is unchanged, so the wrap is transparent to the parent.
If \(\tau\) is empty the whole control becomes \(N\), with the old root as arm \(0\).

Like \(\csem{\Add}\), the form is a recursion on the path, and only its base case is new:
\[
\csem{\Designate([\,],\ \mathit{survivor})}\ M = \mathtt{wrap}(M).
\]
Its recursive case is \(\csem{\Add}\)'s, carrying \(\mathtt{flows}(\mathit{survivor})\) rather than \(\mathtt{flows}(\mathit{arm})\) down toward \(\tau\).

\paragraph{Note: Overlap between the old subtree and \ensuremath{\mathit{survivor}}}

Overlap of leaf labels between \(C@\tau\) and \ensuremath{\mathit{survivor}} is explicitly permitted.
The moment when \(\delta\) fires becomes the boundary between old traffic and new.
Old traffic continues to drain from arm 0, which is \(C@\tau\) carried over; new traffic goes to arm 1.
From that instant on, an arriving packet whose label appears in \(\lceil \mathit{survivor} \rceil\) is sent by \(N.\mathit{z}\) to slot 1, while the residual packets already under arm 0 drain on their existing \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} entries.
The two arms thus stay operationally disjoint even when their label sets overlap.

\paragraph{Pol-level reading}

\Designate{} wraps the \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} subtree at \(\tau\):
\[
\psem{\Designate(\tau, \mathit{survivor})} = \mathtt{at}(\tau,\; t \mapsto \Strict(\mathtt{hi}{:}\,t,\ \mathtt{lo}{:}\,\mathit{survivor})).
\]
It yields \(\Strict\), not \(\Strictstar\), since the star is \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}-invisible.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\Undesignate(\tau)\)}{Undesignate}}\label{sec:undesignate}


\(\tau : \mathit{path}\) targets the \(\Strictstar\) node to unwrap.
\Undesignate{} is the structural inverse of \Designate.
It drops a \(\Strictstar\) node whose arm 0 is empty, leaving arm 1 behind (the last step of \cref{fig:designate}).
Below we write \(N\) for that node and \(B\) for its surviving arm, both as controls, and \(|B|\) for the number of packets buffered under \(B\).

\paragraph{Definition}

\Undesignate{} is defined when \(C@\tau\)'s ranker names \(\Strictstar\), and when its arm 0 is empty.
\(\mathtt{unwrap}\) returns arm 1 of the node, \(B\), with its subtree and contents unchanged; the node \(N = C@\tau\) and its empty arm-0 subtree are discarded.
Since \(\mathtt{at}\) writes \(B\) back into the parent's slot \(k\), the parent's \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{meta}(k)\) is unchanged and \(B\) now hangs directly where \(N\) did:
\[
\csem{\Undesignate(\tau)} = \mathtt{at}(\tau,\, \mathtt{unwrap}).
\]

\paragraph{Pol-level reading}

\Undesignate{} collapses the wrapper, which reads as \(\Strict\) at \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}-level, and leaves its low arm behind:
\[
\psem{\Undesignate(\tau)} = \mathtt{at}(\tau,\; \Strict(\mathtt{hi}{:}\,a,\ \mathtt{lo}{:}\,b) \mapsto b).
\]
That arm is \(\lfloor B \rfloor\).
The wrapper's own annotations go with it, and the surviving arm inherits the annotation the wrapper held at its parent.
The empty-arm-0 precondition is operational and leaves no trace at \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}-level.

\section{Correctness}\label{sec:correctness}

\cref{sec:edit-semantics} gave each form of \(\delta\) its two readings.
Two things have to be true of them.
The first is that the two readings agree, so that the \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} we echo to the operator is the one the live control realizes.
The second is that installing an edit costs the buffered backlog nothing.
This section states both as theorems and then proves them one form at a time.

\subsection{Agreement}\label{sec:interplay}

Four pieces are now in hand: the runtime object \(C\) (\cref{sec:controls}), the source \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} together with the compilation \(\lceil \cdot \rceil\) and the reading-off \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) (\cref{sec:grammar}), the grammar \(\delta\) of atomic edits, and its two readings \(\psem{\delta}\) and \(\csem{\delta}\).
We state here how they fit together.
Their interplay is what lets the runtime, at every step, echo to the operator the \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} that the live control realizes.

The situation we care about is a single \(\delta\) firing on a running control.
Consider the following commuting square, which we will read with an eye to the operator's experience.
Our final goal will be to correctly relate \(C_2'\) and \(C_2\).

\begin{center}

]

The operator writes \(p_1\), which is compiled into control \(C_1 := \lceil p_1 \rceil\).
Recall that \(\lfloor C_1 \rfloor \eqR p_1\) by compiler correctness (\cref{sec:equivalences}).
We echo \(p_1' := \lfloor C_1 \rfloor\) back to the operator.
\(p_1'\) matters because it faithfully reports the arm ordering that the compiler chose.
Push and pop operations then carry \(C_1\) to \(C_1'\).
We know that \(C_1 \sim C_1'\), since \push{} and \pop{} changed only live contents, and \(\lfloor C_1' \rfloor = \lfloor C_1 \rfloor\) by insensitivity to live contents, so the live \(C_1'\) still realizes both \(p_1\) and \(p_1'\).

The operator writes \(p_2\), and the \emph{transition planner} (\cref{sec:planner}), the tool that infers atomic edits between two \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}s, produces a \(\delta\) such that \(\psem{\delta}(p_1') \eqR p_2\).
The \(\delta\) is expressed with paths into the running policy \(p_1'\), so it is \(p_1'\) that \(\psem{\delta}\) transforms, not the operator's original \(p_1\) (whose arm order may differ).
Applying \(\csem{\delta}\) to \(C_1'\) brings us to control \(C_2'\).
By closure under \(\delta\) (\cref{thm:interplay} below) we have \(\lfloor C_2' \rfloor \eqR \psem{\delta}(p_1')\).
Chaining this with the previously established \(\psem{\delta}(p_1') \eqR p_2\) gives \(\lfloor C_2' \rfloor \eqR p_2\).
We again echo \(p_2' := \lfloor C_2' \rfloor\) to the operator.

The transformation is complete at this point, but it still needs a reference point.
\(C_2 := \lceil p_2 \rceil\) is the control that the state of the art would have built, since all it can do is compile a new control from scratch.
\emph{We} do not actually construct \(C_2\), and the remaining work is to relate \(C_2'\), which we have just produced, to \(C_2\).

By compiler correctness, \(\lfloor C_2 \rfloor \eqR p_2\), hence \(\lfloor C_2' \rfloor \eqR \lfloor C_2 \rfloor\).
However, we would like to relate the \emph{controls} directly, not just relate their \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}}-level projections via \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\).
The control-level relation we can write is \(C_2' \simR C_2\), which absorbs two gaps at once.
\(C_2'\) carries the live \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} and \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} accumulated since \(C_1\), while \(C_2\) is freshly compiled and bare, and the \(\sim\) component covers that.
\(\csem{\delta}\) and \(\lceil \cdot \rceil\) are also free to pick different sibling orders at internal nodes, so the two may differ in child arrangements, and the R-closure covers that.

\begin{theorem}[Interplay of \(\psem{\delta}\) and \(\csem{\delta}\)]\label{thm:interplay}
Let \(C\) be a well-formed control, and let \(\delta\) be a form of the grammar (\cref{sec:delta-grammar}) such that \(\csem{\delta}(C)\) is defined.
Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \(\csem{\delta}(C)\) is well-formed.
\item \(\lfloor \csem{\delta}(C) \rfloor \eqR \psem{\delta}(\lfloor C \rfloor)\).
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
Each clause aggregates one of the per-form checks of \cref{sec:per-form}.
Clause 1 is exactly \emph{Preservation of \(\vdash\)}, and clause 2 is exactly \emph{Characterization}.
Clause 2 has content because \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) is fixed in advance, by structural recursion on the control (\cref{sec:equivalences}), rather than extended along \(\delta\).
Each form's Characterization must show that the recursion, rerun on the edited control, lands where \(\psem{\delta}\) says it should.
\end{proof}

The diagram above is the unit cell of a fuller picture: a real reconfiguration is a \emph{sequence} of edits, not one \(\delta\), and \cref{sec:idioms} lines up copies of this cell end to end, each intermediate control carrying its own \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) meaning.

\paragraph{A worked example}

The operator requests \(p_1 = \Strict(\mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{lo}{:}\,\textsf{gmail})\).
The compiler uses its degree of freedom to yield control \(C_1\) such that \(\lfloor C_1 \rfloor = \Strict(\mathtt{lo}{:}\,\textsf{gmail},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom})\); the children have been reordered for some compiler-internal reason.
We echo back
\[
p_1' := \lfloor C_1 \rfloor = \Strict(\mathtt{lo}{:}\,\textsf{gmail},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom}).
\]
We know that \(p_1 \eqR p_1'\), so the scheduler that the operator gets is still the one they asked for; only the slot numbering differs.
As the control serves pushes and pops, \(C_1\) transforms into \(C_1'\): the same tree with its \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}}s and \ensuremath{\mathit{state}}s advanced, so it is still the case that \(C_1 \sim C_1'\) and \(\lfloor C_1' \rfloor = p_1'\).

Later, the operator requests \(p_2 = \Strict(\mathtt{lo}{:}\,\textsf{gmail},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{mid}{:}\,\textsf{spotify},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom})\).
The runtime can again use its freedom: instead of literally splicing \textsf{spotify} in between running arms \textsf{gmail} and \textsf{zoom}, it chooses to append \textsf{spotify} to the end.
This converts the running control \(C_1'\) into control \(C_2'\) such that
\[
\lfloor C_2' \rfloor = \Strict(\mathtt{lo}{:}\,\textsf{gmail},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{mid}{:}\,\textsf{spotify}),
\]
and we echo \(p_2' := \lfloor C_2' \rfloor\) back to the operator.

This is \cref{thm:interplay} on a small scale.
The operator wrote \(p_1\) then \(p_2\), the runtime exercised its arm-order freedom to produce \(C_1'\) then \(C_2'\), and at each step we read off and echoed the running representative, \(p_1'\) and then \(p_2'\).
Since \(\lfloor C_2' \rfloor \eqR p_2\), the operator's request is faithfully realized despite the different slot order.

\subsection{Non-disruption}\label{sec:nondisruption}

Agreement on its own is not enough.
The stop-the-world reset of \cref{sec:intro} is realizable and explainable too, and it is the baseline we wish to beat.
We beat it with the property of \emph{non-disruption}.
Installing \(\delta\) may disturb previously buffered traffic \emph{only} in service of the newly installed policy.
The \emph{very act of changing policies} never introduces arbitrary artifacts such as drops, reorders, or rewrites among buffered packets.
Each already-enqueued entry keeps the rank it was given when it was \push{}ed, so nothing buffered is lost, corrupted, or reordered relative to the rest of the backlog.

Buffered packets may of course be affected by the new policy in force.
After \(\WFQ(1{:}A,\, 9{:}B) \to \WFQ(99{:}A,\, 9{:}B)\), for instance, a newly arriving \(A\)-packet can overtake previously buffered \(B\) packets.
That is the reconfigured policy \emph{correctly} governing new arrivals, not a disturbance of the backlog.

It satisfies both clauses of \cref{thm:interplay}, since \(\lceil p_2 \rceil\) is well-formed and \(\lfloor \lceil p_2 \rceil \rfloor \eqR p_2\), and it is exactly the stop-the-world baseline: buffered packets dropped on the floor.

We state the property on what the network can observe, which is the sequence of packets that successive \pop{}s release.

\begin{theorem}[Non-disruption]\label{thm:nondisruption}
Let \(C\) be a well-formed control, and let \(\delta\) be a form of the grammar (\cref{sec:delta-grammar}) such that \(\csem{\delta}(C)\) is defined.
Then \(C\) and \(\csem{\delta}(C)\) release the same sequence of packets under any run of \pop{}s alone.
\end{theorem}

Most forms discharge this through a single fact about \pop{}, and need no argument of their own.

\begin{lemma}[Non-disruption reduces to \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}}-content preservation]\label{lem:nondisruption}
A \pop{} reads only \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} contents: it pops the root index-\ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} to a child, recurses, and finally emits a leaf packet, never consulting any \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} or \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} (\cref{sec:controls}).
So suppose \(\csem{\delta}\) leaves the contents of every non-empty \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} of \(C\) unchanged, never rewriting, reordering, or removing a live entry, and introducing only empty \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}}s.
Then no previously buffered packet in \(C\) is dropped, reordered, or re-ranked in \(C'\).
The buffered backlog is preserved entry-for-entry, and \(\delta\) is non-disruptive.
\end{lemma}

We therefore discharge non-disruption per form by naming the form's \emph{footprint}, the nodes and fields it touches, and checking that the footprint holds no live \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} entry.
Four of the six pass that check outright.
\Designate{} inserts a node whose \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} is not empty and \Undesignate{} drops one, so those two need an argument of their own.
Each such argument shows that the \pop{} sequence is unchanged, which is why \cref{thm:nondisruption} is stated on that sequence rather than on \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} contents.

The lemma can read counterintuitively.
Inverting the two priorities of \(\Strict(\mathtt{hi}: A, \mathtt{lo}: B)\) with \ChangeMeta{} does \emph{not} change the next \pop{}.
Ranks are frozen at \push{} time, so the parent's index-\ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} already holds \(A\) at \(\mathtt{hi}\) and \(B\) at \(\mathtt{lo}\), and \ChangeMeta{} rewrites neither.
Only packets pushed after the change are ranked under the new priorities.

\subsection{Form by Form}\label{sec:per-form}

Each form owes three checks, and together the six sets of three prove the two theorems above.
Two of the checks feed \cref{thm:interplay}, and the third feeds \cref{thm:nondisruption}.
Throughout, we write \(C\) for the pre-edit control and \(C'\) for the post-edit control, and \(p = \lfloor C \rfloor\), \(p' = \lfloor C' \rfloor\) for the policies they realize.
All three checks assume \(\csem{\delta}(C)\) is defined.
Outside the region \cref{sec:edit-semantics} specifies, \(\csem{\delta}\) is undefined.

\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Preservation of \(\vdash\).} \(\vdash C\) implies \(\vdash C'\).
Any packets or index entries that \(\csem{\delta}\) drops or adds must leave the per-node pifo and packet counts in balance.
\item \emph{Characterization.} Prove that reading the policy off the edited control agrees, up to \(\eqR\), with applying the \(\psem{\delta}\) of \cref{sec:edit-semantics} to the policy read off the original: \(p' \eqR \psem{\delta}(p)\).
\item \emph{Non-disruption.} Name the form's footprint and check that it holds no live \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} entry, so that \cref{lem:nondisruption} applies.
Where it does not apply, argue directly that the \pop{} sequence is unchanged.
\end{itemize}

A fourth check might be expected here, and is deliberately absent.
Nothing has to be proved about the \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} of the nodes an edit leaves alone, because every \(\csem{\delta}\) rule names each field of the node it produces, and \(\mathtt{at}(\pi, g)\) copies every off-path node by definition.
What state survives an edit is therefore read off \cref{sec:edit-semantics} rather than argued for here.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\Add(\pi, D, \mathit{arm}, m)\)}{Add}}

\paragraph{Preservation of \(\vdash\)}

The on-path ancestors have only their transactions extended; but \(\vdash\) balances \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} entries against buffered packets, and editing \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} adds neither an entry nor a packet,
so the only nodes that can affect it are \(C@\pi\) and the freshly compiled \(\lceil \mathit{arm} \rceil\).
At \(C'@\pi\),
the \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} is unchanged (\(C'@\pi.\mathit{pifo} = C@\pi.\mathit{pifo}\)) and holds no entry equal to the new slot \(k\), since no pre-existing entry can name a slot that did not exist in \(C\); the new arm at slot \(k\) holds no packets, so slot \(k\)'s count reads \(0 = 0\).
Every other slot at \(C@\pi\) keeps its index, its packets, and its entries unchanged, so its count is inherited.
Inside \(\lceil \mathit{arm} \rceil\), \(\vdash\) holds because \(\lceil \cdot \rceil\) compiles a well-formed control whose pifos start empty.
Every other node is untouched.
Nothing needs repair.

\paragraph{Characterization}
Both recursions descend \(\pi\) in the same shape, so we induct on \(\pi\).
At the base (\(\pi = []\)), \(\psem{\cdot}\) appends the annotated arm \(m{:}\,\mathit{arm}\), while \(\csem{\Add}\) appends \(\lceil \mathit{arm} \rceil\) at slot \(k\) and sets \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{meta}(k)\) to \(m\).
We have \(\lfloor \lceil \mathit{arm} \rceil \rfloor \eqR \mathit{arm}\), and \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) reads the annotation back off \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{meta}\).
So the two agree on the new arm up to \(\eqR\), and every existing arm is untouched.
At a recursive step, both descend into child \(i\) and write the result back, so the claim follows from the induction hypothesis.
The ancestor itself adds nothing to reconcile.
\(\csem{\Add}\) leaves its \ensuremath{\mathit{state}} and \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} unchanged, and its \ensuremath{\mathit{z}}-extension is confined to the classifier of an internal node, which \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) does not read.
So the two sides agree all the way down the spine and can differ only inside \(\lceil \mathit{arm} \rceil\), where sibling order is free; that is exactly the \(\eqR\)-slack the claim allows, giving \(p' \eqR \psem{\Add(\pi, D, \mathit{arm}, m)}(p)\).

\paragraph{Non-disruption}

\(\csem{\Add}\)'s footprint is the new empty slot \(k\) at \(C@\pi\), its metadatum, the freshly compiled empty subtree \(\lceil \mathit{arm} \rceil\), and the on-path \ensuremath{\mathit{z}}-extensions.
None of these is a live \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} entry: the new arm holds no packets, and a \ensuremath{\mathit{z}}-edit changes no \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}}.
So \Add{} is non-disruptive by \cref{lem:nondisruption}.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\ChangeMeta\), \(\Quiesce\), and \(\Remove\)}{ChangeMeta, Quiesce, Remove}}

These three follow \Add's pattern.
Each touches no live \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} entry, so \(\vdash\)-counts are inherited and \cref{lem:nondisruption} gives non-disruption directly.
Each departs from \Add{} in one place, and that place is all we give.

\emph{\ChangeMeta.} Its footprint is a single \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{meta}\) entry, so no child, no \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}}, and no count moves at all.
It is the one form whose two sides agree exactly rather than up to \(\eqR\), since no arm changes position: \(p' = \psem{\ChangeMeta(\tau, m)}(p)\).
An in-flight entry's rank was fixed when it was pushed, so the new metadatum governs only future pushes.

\emph{\Quiesce.} Its footprint is the admitted-set restrictions across \(V_\tau\), and every node's \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}}, \ensuremath{\mathit{slot\_states}}, \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}}, and children are untouched.
It is the one form whose \(\psem{\cdot}\) is the identity, because \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) does not read \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{admit}\) and \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves}\) is left in place, so a silenced leaf keeps both its way out and its identity.
Packets already buffered under \(C@\tau\) keep their entries and still drain, and only future \push{}es are refused.

\emph{\Remove.} Emptiness and \(\vdash C\) leave \(C@\pi.\mathit{pifo}\) with no entry equal to \(k\), so \(\mathtt{drop}_k\) deletes no entry, and every surviving child keeps its own entries under the shift \(\sigma_k\).
The ancestor retractions delete nothing either, since they narrow admission for future pushes only.
The parent keeps its ranker, so it still reads as the same \ensuremath{D} with slot \(k\) gone, giving \(p' \eqR \psem{\Remove(\tau)}(p)\).

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\Designate(\tau, \mathit{survivor})\)}{Designate}}

\paragraph{Characterization}
As with \Add, outside the wrap every node is structurally intact, arm 1 is \(\lceil \mathit{survivor} \rceil\), and the ancestor \ensuremath{\mathit{z}}-extensions touch only internal-node classifiers.
The fresh wrapper \(N\) is the one node whose discipline \cref{sec:equivalences} did not have in view when it defined \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\).
Its ranker names \(\Strictstar\), which \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) reports as \Strict, with the two arms annotated \(\mathtt{hi}\) and \(\mathtt{lo}\) out of \(\mathit{z}_N.\mathit{meta}\).
So \(p' \eqR \psem{\Designate(\tau, \mathit{survivor})}(p)\).

\paragraph{Preservation and non-disruption}

\emph{Preservation of \(\vdash\).} At \(N\), the \(P_0\) slot-\(0\) entries match the packet count under arm 0, and arm 1 holds no packet and no slot-\(1\) entry.
Arm 0 is unchanged.
At \(C@\pi\), \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}} is unchanged, and slot \(k\)'s count \(P_0\) matches what \(C@\tau\) carried.
Non-disruption takes a real argument here, since \(\mathtt{wrap}\) inserts the live node \(N\) with a non-empty \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}}, so \cref{lem:nondisruption} does not apply directly.
Every in-flight packet lies under arm 0, which is \(C@\tau\) carried over unchanged, so its own entries are untouched.
If \(P_0 > 0\), every entry in \(N.\mathit{pifo}\) is slot \(0\), so any \pop{} reaching \(N\) descends into arm 0 and returns exactly what it would have before; if \(P_0 = 0\), \(\vdash C\) means no \pop{} ever reaches \(N\).
Arm 1 sees traffic only on a future \push{}, so no buffered packet is dropped, reordered, or re-ranked, and \Designate{} is non-disruptive.

\subsubsection{\texorpdfstring{\(\Undesignate(\tau)\)}{Undesignate}}

\paragraph{Characterization}
As with \Remove, nothing structural outside \(N\) moves and the wrapper is dropped, so \(p' \eqR \psem{\Undesignate(\tau)}(p)\).

\paragraph{Preservation and non-disruption}

\emph{Preservation of \(\vdash\).} With arm 0 empty, \(N.\mathit{pifo}\) carried \(|B|\) slot-\(1\) entries, and \(C@\pi.\mathit{pifo}\)'s slot-\(k\) entries carried the same count one level up.
After the unwrap those \(k\)-entries lead straight to \(B\), whose count is still \(|B|\).
So \(C@\pi\) reads \(|B| = |B|\), and \(B\) reads as before.
\emph{Non-disruption.} The only entries \Undesignate{} drops are \(N\)'s slot-\(1\) entries, and with arm 0 empty every \pop{} reaching \(N\) was already forced to slot \(1\) and on to \(B\).
Eliding \(N\) removes that redundant hop and returns the same packet, and no entry inside \(B\) moves.
So \Undesignate{} is non-disruptive.

\paragraph{Taking stock}

That is all six forms, and together they discharge \cref{thm:interplay} and \cref{thm:nondisruption}.
The Characterizations have content because each was proved against a \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) fixed in advance, so they are six separate claims rather than six readings of one definition.
\subsection{Preserving this proof down to hardware}\label{sec:hw-preservation}

\cref{sec:per-form} proves soundness at the \(\delta\) level, where each form (\Add, \Quiesce, \Remove, \ldots) carries a well-formed tree to a well-formed tree.
To run on hardware, each edit is lowered, by a simple and mechanical compilation, into a sequence of fine-grained instructions in an intermediate representation (IR).
The IR's instruction alphabet and the substrate machinery are the subject of \cref{sec:hardware}.
Here we need only two things about that IR.
Its instructions are fine-grained, each doing one small thing such as allocating a node, attaching a node to a parent, or admitting a flow at a node.
And a single such instruction, unlike a whole form of \(\delta\), \emph{can} leave the tree malformed, since a node that has been allocated but not yet attached to a parent satisfies no well-formedness condition.

We do not prove soundness at the IR level, but instead informally make the case for why the \cref{sec:per-form} proof survives the lowering.
There are two reasons.
First, the compilation is \emph{faithful}: each form of \(\delta\) expands to a fixed instruction sequence that, when run to completion, realizes exactly that form's effect.
We give the translation from each \(\delta\) form to its IR instruction sequence and take its faithfulness to be uncontroversial.
Second, our substrate runs each such sequence as a single \emph{transactional commit}: a group of IR instructions, fenced by the commit's open and close, that installs as one instant, with no \push{} or \pop{} interleaved between them (\S7.1' spells out the substrate machinery that makes this so).
The transiently-malformed intermediate trees are therefore never observed by the network.
That commit is precisely how the substrate \emph{realizes} the atomicity property of \cref{sec:grammar}.
Atomicity asked for an instantaneous control replacement between two \push{}/\pop{} operations, and the commit collapses a multi-instruction lowering into one such instant.
Every \push{}/\pop{} therefore still lands on a well-formed control (\(p_1\), some transitionary policy, or \(p_2\)), exactly as \cref{sec:per-form} proved.

The same argument carries from the IR down to hardware.
The hardware executes a committed sequence atomically with respect to \push{} and \pop{}, so what it exhibits is again what \cref{sec:per-form} proved.
The compilation itself, and the substrate machinery that makes a commit atomic, are the subject of \cref{sec:hardware}.

\xxx[AM for Zhiyuan]{the \cref{sec:hw-preservation} argument leans on the substrate supporting \emph{atomic transactional commits}: a multi-instruction lowering must install as a single instant from the user's perspective, so that the transiently-malformed intermediate trees inside a commit are never observed.
Our own substrate provides this.
The open question for \cref{sec:hardware} is whether composition with a third-party substrate (e.g., vPIFO) requires the same property and, if so, whether vPIFO offers it.

\section{Realizing Reconfigurations as Guarded Sequences}\label{sec:sequences}

This section composes the forms of \(\delta\) (\cref{sec:grammar}) into \emph{guarded sequences} \((\varphi ; \delta)^{*}\), where a guard \(\varphi\) is a predicate on the state of the live control.
Guarded sequences realize changes to the live control that no single \(\delta\) can express.
We first show that the transitionary period such a sequence passes through needs no new semantics (\cref{sec:links}), then give the grammar of guarded sequences and the idioms operators build from them (\cref{sec:idioms}).

\subsection{Transitionary Policies and Their Semantics}\label{sec:links}

A guarded sequence threads the live control through a chain of intermediate controls.
We call each such intermediate control a \emph{transitionary policy}, and we write \(\mathit{link}_i\) for the control on which the \(i\)-th \(\delta\) fires, counting from zero.
So \(\mathit{link}_0 = C\) is the starting control, and \(\mathit{link}_{i+1} = \csem{\delta_i}(\mathit{link}_i)\) is what the \(i\)-th \(\delta\) leaves behind (\cref{sec:idioms} makes this precise).

Each transitionary policy is itself an ordinary \cref{sec:controls}-style control, so the transitionary period \emph{needs no new semantics}.
That is Obligation 1 of \cref{sec:intro}, discharged locally by the per-form checks of \cref{sec:per-form}.
Moreover, since every form of \cref{sec:grammar} is \emph{pol-explainable} (each \(\delta\) has a \(\psem{\delta}\) that tracks its pol-level effect), we can echo to the operator, at every step of the sequence, the \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} that the live control actually realizes.
The chain of \(\psem{\cdot}\)s starting from \(\lfloor C \rfloor\) gives \(\lfloor \mathit{link}_i \rfloor\) for each \(i\), so the operator always knows which policy is running.

\paragraph{Follow-up requests}
The transition planner engages with one reconfiguration at a time.
Suppose the operator submits a follow-up \(p_3\) while a \(p_1 \to p_2\) sequence is still in flight, some guard \(\varphi_i\) not yet having become true.
We queue \(p_3\), and the planner does not begin work on it until the in-flight sequence completes and the live control \(C_z\) satisfies \(\lfloor C_z \rfloor \eqR p_2\).
At that instant the planner pulls \(p_3\) from the queue, treats \(\lfloor C_z \rfloor\) as the new starting point, and produces a fresh sequence to reach \(p_3\).
A more aggressive strategy is possible: splicing or canceling the in-flight sequence to converge on \(p_3\) directly, sometimes at lower total cost than running \(p_1 \to p_2 \to p_3\) in series.
We do not pursue this here, since the splicing analysis introduces its own correctness obligations: the atomicity of the splice, and what transitionary policy the operator observes between the splice and \(p_3\).

\subsection{Guarded Sequences and Their Idioms}\label{sec:idioms}

\paragraph{Grammar}

\centering
\[

\mathit{gseq} \mathrel{::=} \epsilon \;\mid\; (\varphi\,;\, \delta)\,;\, \mathit{gseq}\\[8pt]

\quad (\mathtt{true};\, \Quiesce(\mathit{path} \mathbin{+\!\!+} [0]))\,;\\

\end{array}
\]
\caption{The grammar of guarded sequences and the three starter idioms, each a named \((\varphi;\delta)^{*}\).}
\label{fig:gseq-idioms}
\end{figure}

We write \(\mathit{gseq}\) for a guarded sequence (\cref{fig:gseq-idioms}, top).
A guard \(\varphi\) is either trivially satisfied (\(\mathtt{true}\)) or asserts that the subtree at \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} is empty; it is always paired with exactly one \(\delta\) drawn from \cref{sec:grammar}.
Those two guard forms have sufficed for every sequence we have needed.
The framework does not depend on this minimality, and a richer predicate language can take their place.

\paragraph{Intuition}

The hardware substrate (\cref{sec:hardware}) does two things beyond ordinary \push{}/\pop{}: it waits for a guard \(\varphi\) to become true on the live control, and it fires a single \(\delta\) as an atomic replacement.
A guarded sequence chains these: wait for \(\varphi_0\), fire \(\delta_0\), then wait for \(\varphi_1\) (now evaluated on the post-\(\delta_0\) control), fire \(\delta_1\), and so on.
Each intermediate control between two consecutive \(\delta\) firings is well-formed, and we call this property the sequence's \emph{safety}.
Safety is entirely local, following from the per-form checks of \cref{sec:per-form}.

\paragraph{Reaching the target}

Safety guarantees that every intermediate control is well-formed, but not that the sequence \emph{arrives} at the intended \(p_2\).
We check arrival at the pol level, folding each form's \(\psem{\delta_i}\) (\cref{sec:edit-semantics}) along the sequence.
Write \(\mathit{ip}_i\) for the \(i\)-th intermediate pol.
Starting from \(\mathit{ip}_0 = \lfloor C \rfloor\), the pol we echoed to the operator, set \(\mathit{ip}_{i+1} := \psem{\delta_i}(\mathit{ip}_i)\).
The sequence is admissible when every \(\psem{\delta_i}\) is defined on \(\mathit{ip}_i\) and the final \(\mathit{ip}_{n+1} \eqR p_2\).
This is the Interplay theorem (\cref{sec:interplay}) iterated across the sequence.
Each \(\psem{\delta_i}\) matches its operational counterpart \(\csem{\delta_i}\) (\cref{sec:per-form}), so a fold that lands at \(\mathit{ip}_{n+1} \eqR p_2\) certifies, cell by cell, that the operational chain ends in a control whose \(\lfloor \cdot \rfloor\) is \(p_2\).
Guards play no role in this check: they govern \emph{when} each \(\delta_i\) fires on the live control and are pol-invisible (\cref{sec:edit-semantics}), so a \((\mathtt{empty}(\mathit{path}); \Remove(\mathit{path}))\) step folds exactly as if its guard were \(\mathtt{true}\).
If the chain fails to reach \(p_2\), or some \(\psem{\delta_i}\) is undefined on its \(\mathit{ip}_i\), the sequence is rejected.
The rejection identifies the offending step, either the first \(\delta_i\) whose \(\psem{\delta_i}\) was undefined or else the final mismatch \(\mathit{ip}_{n+1} \not\eqR p_2\).
Both clients of the framework rely on this check: the planner (\cref{sec:planner}) uses it as a backstop on the sequences it assembles, and an operator authoring by hand (\cref{sec:imperative}) uses it to validate theirs.

\paragraph{Liveness}

Safety is unconditional; liveness is not, and cannot be.
A guarded sequence progresses only as its guards become true, and a guard like \(\mathtt{empty}(\mathit{path})\) becomes true only once the traffic under \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} drains, which is up to the network, not up to us.
A persistent or adversarial flow can keep a subtree busy indefinitely, and then any step waiting on that subtree's emptiness never fires.
This is inherent.
Draining a subtree gracefully means letting its buffered packets leave on their own, and the only way to speed that up is to drop them, which the grammar refuses to do.
So completing a sequence takes the operator's cooperation, for instance quiescing a flow at its source or accepting a harder reconfiguration that resets more of the tree but drops nothing.
The live control stays safe throughout, but may not reach the target until the traffic allows.
Follow-up requests submitted during a stalled sequence are queued (\cref{sec:links}).

\paragraph{Idioms}

\emph{Idioms} are a named vocabulary for frequently-used guarded sequences, whether the planner assembles them or an operator writes them by hand.

An idiom is a macro over the grammar \(\delta\) (and, recursively, over other idioms).
It expands into a fixed \((\varphi; \delta)^{*}\) sequence: a list of forms with the guards between them spelling out what the system waits for.
Soundness is compositional: each step of the expansion is sound by \cref{sec:per-form}, and the sequence inherits the sequence-level reasoning above.
An idiom expansion that would reach an undefined form on the current control is rejected, since the soundness checks run on the expanded sequence just as they would on a hand-written one.

We name three starter idioms, with their expansions collected in \cref{fig:gseq-idioms}.
New ones can be added later without changing the framework, since an idiom is only a named \((\varphi; \delta)^{*}\).

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\Retire(\ensuremath{\mathit{path}})} quiesces the subtree at \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}, waits for it to drain to empty, then \Remove{}s it.
This is the operator-facing way to say ``tear this subtree down gracefully.''

\item \textbf{\SlowRetire(\ensuremath{\mathit{path}})} waits for the subtree at \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} to drain, then \Remove{}s it.
The operator may use this if the subtree at \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} needs to receive more traffic but is generally being phased out.

\item \textbf{\Replace(\ensuremath{\mathit{path}}, \(B\))} phases \(B\) in as the survivor of \(\mathit{pol}@\mathit{path}\).
It \Designate{}s \(B\), quiesces and drains the original subtree, then removes that subtree and the \(\Strictstar\) wrapper, leaving \(B\) alone at \ensuremath{\mathit{path}} (\cref{fig:gseq-idioms}).
At the pol level the effect is the outright replacement of \(\mathit{pol}@\mathit{path}\) by \(B\).
Its footprint is the subtree at \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}, the wrap node \Designate{} inserts, and the \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} chain at the ancestors of \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}.
\cref{sec:planner} frames confinement against exactly this set.

\end{itemize}

\section{The Transition Planner}\label{sec:planner}

The operator hands the runtime \(p_1\), the presently running policy, and \(p_2\), the one they now want.
The transition planner's job is to propose a \((\varphi ; \delta)^{*}\) sequence whose pol-level fold takes \(p_1\) to \(p_2\) up to \(\eqR\).
The operator accepts or rejects the proposal; they do not author it directly.

Every sequence the planner emits is safe by the per-form guarantees of \cref{sec:per-form}, aggregated across the sequence as sketched in \cref{sec:idioms}, regardless of how the planner assembled it.
The planner aims to land at \(p_2\) by construction: each case below emits a \(\delta\) or idiom whose \(\psem{\cdot}\) realizes the locally-observed pol-level difference.
A misstep in the case analysis could in principle yield a sequence whose fold \(\psem{\delta_n} \circ \ldots \circ \psem{\delta_1} (p_1)\) misses \(p_2\)'s \(\eqR\)-class.
The pol-level check of \cref{sec:idioms} catches this, and rejects any such sequence before it is installed.
Optimality is a separate matter, and is not guaranteed, since the planner may fail to see a better sequence that the operator has in mind.

\cref{sec:fallback} sets up \emph{confinement} as the metric we judge a plan by, presents the always-available worst-case \Replace{} fallback, and frames the planner's strategy as descending the tree to find a tight form (\Add, \Remove, or \ChangeMeta) that captures the difference locally.
\cref{sec:planner-emits} catalogs the cases the planner recognizes and emits a tight sequence for.
\cref{sec:imperative} shows how an operator can instead author a sequence by hand, admitted by the same pol-level check.
\cref{sec:planner-nonfeatures} lists deliberate non-features and candidate future work.

\subsection{The Always-Available Fallback, and Confinement to do Better}\label{sec:fallback}

To reach any \(p_2\) from any \(p_1\), the planner can issue \(\Replace([], p_2)\), the \cref{sec:idioms} idiom that expands to

\[
\begin{array}{@{}l@{}}
(\mathtt{true};\ \Designate([], p_2))\ ;\ (\mathtt{true};\ \Quiesce([0]))\ ;\\[3pt]
\qquad (\mathtt{empty}([0]);\ \Undesignate([]))
\end{array}
\]
This wraps \(p_1\) and \(p_2\) into \(\Strictstar(\mathtt{hi}: p_1, \mathtt{lo}: p_2)\) at the root (so pops still drain \(p_1\) while pushes go to \(p_2\)), and, once \(p_1\) is empty, collapses the wrapper to leave \(p_2\) alone.
Nothing is dropped and the sequence is safe by \cref{sec:per-form}, but \emph{no part of the scheduler is left undisturbed}.

\paragraph{Footprint}

To measure how much of the tree a sequence touches, associate each form \(\delta\) with a \emph{footprint} on the live control it acts on.
Define \(\mathtt{footprint}(\delta, C)\) as the set of nodes of \(C\) whose \ensuremath{\mathit{state}}, \ensuremath{\mathit{pifo}}, or \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} differs between \(C\) and \(\csem{\delta}(C)\).
The set is read off the \(\csem{\delta}\) rules of \cref{sec:edit-semantics}, each of which names every field of every node it produces.
It includes the edit site, the new or removed node where applicable, and every proper ancestor whose \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} is extended or restricted, such as the ancestors touched by \Add, \Quiesce, \Remove, and \Designate.
For a guarded sequence \((\varphi_0 ; \delta_0) ; \ldots ; (\varphi_n ; \delta_n)\), the sequence-level footprint is \(\bigcup_i \mathtt{footprint}(\delta_i, \mathit{link}_i)\).
The root-level \(\Replace([], p_2)\) above has the maximum possible footprint: the whole tree.

\paragraph{The planner's strategy}

The metric by which we judge the planner is \emph{confinement}.
A good plan disturbs as little of the running scheduler as possible, leaving the parts of the tree that did not need to change still running.
A sequence is well-confined when its footprint is small relative to the symmetric pol-level difference between \(p_1\) and \(p_2\).

The planner's goal is to recognize whether the difference between \(p_1\) and \(p_2\) is captured by a tight form from \cref{sec:delta-grammar} (an \Add, \Remove, or \ChangeMeta) and to emit that form wherever in the tree it sits, leaving everything else untouched.
The planner descends through the tree as long as \(p_1\) and \(p_2\) agree at the current level, looking for a recognized shape one level down.
When it finds one, it emits the matching form at that depth.
Only when the descent ends without a match does the planner fall back to \Replace.
By then the recursion has localized the difference to the deepest slot at which the policies disagree, so the resulting \Replace{} wraps only that slot.

Suppose
\[
p_1 = P(\textsf{zoom}, Q(\textsf{gmail}, \textsf{netflix}))
\]
for some scheduling disciplines \(P\) and \(Q\), and the operator requests
\[
p_2 = P(\textsf{zoom}, Q(\textsf{gmail}, \textsf{netflix}, \textsf{spotify})).
\]
The planner has two ways to realize this, shown in \cref{fig:planner-add-vs-replace}.

\subsection{The Planner's Case Analysis}\label{sec:planner-emits}

The planner has two parts: a top-level pass over \((p_1, p_2)\) as whole trees, and a \emph{per-level comparator} that the pass defers to.
Whatever the comparator emits is lifted back to the right depth by prepending the slot indices the recursion descended through.

Both parts compare policies up to \(\eqR\), by comparing canonical representatives.
Each \(\eqR\)-class has one under any fixed ordering of the arms at a node (\cref{sec:equivalences}), and we call the pass that computes it \emph{normalization}.
The planner normalizes both sides before it compares them, the operator's request and the policy it reads off the running control.
The read-off is not canonical on its own, since an edit may splice a new arm into whatever slot the control has free rather than into the slot normalization would have chosen.
So the planner keeps the permutation that normalizing the read-off applied, and the slot indices it prepends name slots of the running control rather than of the normalized term.

\paragraph{The top-level pass}

The pass tries two cases in order and stops at the first match.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \(p_1 = p_2\). Emit \([\,]\).
The test is literal equality on the normalized \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} terms, so two \(\eqR\)-equivalent policies arrive here as identical terms.
The live control is already serving the requested policy and there is nothing to install.

\item Otherwise. Hand \((p_1, p_2)\) to the per-level comparator.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What the comparator emits}

The comparator compares the roots of \(p_1\) and \(p_2\).
If the two roots run different disciplines, or are leaves carrying different flows, it emits a \Replace{}, the always-available idiom of \cref{sec:fallback}.
If the roots agree, the task reduces to \emph{pairing up the two child lists}, and each pairing yields one of four kinds of edit.
An arm of \(p_2\) with no partner in \(p_1\) is a fresh \Add{}; an arm of \(p_1\) with no partner in \(p_2\) is a \Retire{}.
A matched pair whose two arms are identical is set aside and contributes nothing.
A matched pair whose arms agree but whose slot metadata differs contributes a lone \ChangeMeta{}.
A matched pair whose arms differ starts a recursion into the pair to localize the change further, with a trailing \ChangeMeta{} appended when the metadata differs too.

\paragraph{Pairing the child lists}

The comparator pairs the arms by the tightest strategy that applies, dropping to a more general one only when the tighter fails.
All three strategies lean on normalization.
Arms that genuinely correspond appear in the same relative order on both sides, which is what lets the positional strategies pair by position at all.

\textbf{By slot, at equal arity.}
When the child lists have the same length, the comparator pairs arm \(i\) with arm \(i\).
A slot whose arm and metadata both agree contributes nothing, and a slot whose metadata alone differs contributes a \ChangeMeta{}.
A slot whose arm differs recurses: it emits whatever the recursion into that arm localizes, and, when the slot's metadata also changed, a trailing \ChangeMeta{} on top of it.
One shape is caught before the slot walk.
At \Strict{} or \WFQ{}, if \(p_1\) and \(p_2\) hold the same arms in a different order, the difference is a pure metadata reshuffle, such as a \Strict{} priority swap that normalization's rank ordering presents as a reordering.
The comparator then emits one \ChangeMeta{} per slot whose metadata changed.

\textbf{By sub-sequence, at unequal arity.}
When one child list embeds in the other as an in-order sub-sequence, the surplus arms are the whole of the change.
Normalization has already aligned the shared arms in a common order, so their appearing in order is the signal that none of them moved or changed and only the extra arms are new or gone.
If \(p_1\)'s arms are an in-order sub-sequence of \(p_2\)'s, each surplus arm of \(p_2\) is a fresh \Add{}.
\cref{sec:add}'s leaf-disjointness precondition holds automatically, since a surplus arm's leaves are leaves of \(p_2\) absent from \(p_1\) and hence disjoint by \cref{sec:policy-dsl}'s leaf-partition validity.
If instead \(p_2\)'s arms are an in-order sub-sequence of \(p_1\)'s, each surplus arm of \(p_1\) \Retire{}s (\cref{sec:idioms}).
Either way, a stable arm whose metadata changed picks up a trailing \ChangeMeta{}.

\textbf{By shared label, in general.}
When neither list embeds in the other, the comparator pairs arms by the flows they carry rather than by position.
A leaf label identifies a unique flow across the whole policy, by \cref{sec:policy-dsl}'s leaf-partition validity.
A label shared between an arm of \(p_1\) and an arm of \(p_2\) is therefore evidence that one arm changed in place, while an arm whose labels appear on no opposing arm is fresh or vanishing.
This \emph{label-overlap walk} greedily pairs each \(p_1\) arm with the unique \(p_2\) arm with which it shares a label, then recurses on the pair; unpaired \(p_1\) arms are \Retire{}d and unpaired \(p_2\) arms are \Add{}ed.
The walk takes the first alignment that satisfies the label constraint, with no notion of cost (\cref{sec:planner-nonfeatures}); if no shared label yields even one pairing, it fails and the comparator gives up with a slot-level \Replace{}.
The walk is worth running even at equal arity.
The slot strategy pairs strictly by position, so an arm that has both moved to a new slot \emph{and} changed its subtree is compared against whatever now occupies its old slot, and the change reads as two unrelated edits rather than one.
The label walk instead follows the shared flow to the arm's new slot and sees the single change, so the comparator runs it alongside the slot walk and, whenever it exposes such a cross-slot pairing, takes it instead.

Consider an example over an abstract discipline \(P\) whose arms carry per-arm metadata, with a nested discipline \(Q\) (the exact disciplines are immaterial to the walk).
We write each arm's metadata after an \texttt{@}.

\begin{verbatim}
p1 = P(drive@m1, gmail@m2)
p2 = P(drive@m1, slack@m3, Q(gmail, x)@m4)
\end{verbatim}

The metadata moves in two different ways as this fires.
\textsf{slack}'s requested metadatum \(m_3\) is carried by the \Add.
It is the metadatum the form carries (\cref{sec:add}), so the new arm lands already carrying \(m_3\).
The changing arm is handled differently, since its metadatum moves from \(m_2\) to \(m_4\).
Nothing in the \Replace{} can carry that change, so the emission ends with a \ChangeMeta{} for the slot.
That step is sequenced after the guarded \Undesignate, so it fires only once the old subtree has drained and the wrapper has collapsed.
Until then the slot keeps \(m_2\).
\Designate{} leaves the wrapped slot's inherited metadata untouched (\cref{sec:designate}), so the transient \(\Strictstar\) arm sits in \(P\) still carrying the old metadatum, which is what panel (c) shows.
The example therefore exhibits both behaviors.
\(m_1\) and \(m_3\) come through untouched, while \(m_2\) is explicitly re-tuned to \(m_4\) once the drain is done.

Panels (c) and (d) of \cref{fig:label-overlap-example} show the two policies the control passes through: first the transient \(\Strictstar\) that drains the old \textsf{gmail} leaf beside the incoming \(Q\), then the settled tree once that drain completes.
In the settled tree \textsf{slack} sits beside the rebuilt \textsf{gmail} subtree in an order that does not match \(p_2\) literally, but sibling order carries no semantics (\cref{sec:policy-dsl}), so the reordering is invisible to any observer.

\paragraph{Indexing a mixed emission}

When a comparison yields \Add{}s, \Retire{}s, and recursions at once, as the label-overlap walk can, the planner uses the reorder freedom of \cref{sec:policy-dsl} to keep the indexing trivial.
The \Add{}s are appended at the parent's tail.
The \Retire{}s fire at their original \(p_1\) indices in descending order, so a higher-index drain does not shift the lower indices still pending.
Each matched-pair recursion lands at its position among the matches, which is also its slot once the \Retire{}s drain.
Issuing the \Add{}s first is deliberate: an \Add{} is instantaneous while a \Retire{} waits for its subtree to drain, so the new arms begin carrying traffic immediately instead of waiting on the retirements.

\paragraph{Where the descent localizes the difference}

Every recursion descends through a slot where the two trees agree or where the label-overlap walk has paired them.
So by the time the per-level comparator gives up and emits a \Replace{} at depth \(k\), every shallower level has already matched a tight case.
The \Replace{} lands at the deepest slot to which the planner could isolate the difference, leaving every ancestor's other subtrees running undisturbed.
The slot-level \Replace{} is the \cref{sec:fallback} idiom with a non-empty path: it wraps only the slot's subtree, quiesces only that subtree, and rewires only the parent edge into it.
The worst case is the root-level \(\Replace([], p_2)\) of \cref{sec:fallback}, reached only when the per-level comparator gives up at the root with no recursive descent.

\subsection{Imperative Authoring}\label{sec:imperative}

The transition planner so far has realized what we call \emph{declarative} authoring.
The operator states a starting policy \(p_1\) and a target \(p_2\), and the planner proposes a guarded sequence that achieves the change, which the operator either accepts, in which case we apply it to the running control, or declines.
Operators may instead author \emph{imperatively}, writing their desired \(p_2\) together with a \((\varphi; \delta)^{*}\) sequence intended to reach it, possibly built from idioms (\cref{sec:idioms}).
Imperative authoring gives the operator hands-on control over the transitionary policies the live control passes through, rather than delegating their choice to the planner.
The two modes are not formally distinct.
They produce sequences over the same grammar, discharge the same per-form checks of \cref{sec:per-form}, and are admitted by the same pol-level check of \cref{sec:idioms}.

Because an imperative sequence carries \emph{paths}, those paths bind to the running representative, not to the \ensuremath{\mathit{pol}} the operator first wrote, which is exactly why we echo that representative back (\cref{sec:interplay}).
The worked example of \cref{sec:interplay} makes the point, since there the running control realizes \(p_2'\) while the operator had written \(p_2\):
\[
\begin{aligned}
p_2' &= \Strict(\mathtt{lo}{:}\,\textsf{gmail},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{mid}{:}\,\textsf{spotify}),\\
p_2  &= \Strict(\mathtt{lo}{:}\,\textsf{gmail},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{mid}{:}\,\textsf{spotify},\allowbreak\ \mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom}).
\end{aligned}
\]
An imperative edit \(\Quiesce([2])\) resolves against \(p_2'\): slot \(2\) there is \(\mathtt{mid}{:}\,\textsf{spotify}\), so \textsf{spotify} is quiesced.
Had the operator read the path off their own \(p_2\), slot \(2\) would be \(\mathtt{hi}{:}\,\textsf{zoom}\), a different arm.
The runtime interprets the path against what is running and has no way to detect the mismatch.

Before committing an operator-authored sequence we run it through the pol-level check of \cref{sec:idioms}, folding \(\psem{\delta_i}\) from \(\lfloor C \rfloor\) and accepting only if the fold is everywhere defined and lands at \(p_2\) up to \(\eqR\).
The intermediate pols it produces are not transient artifacts of the proof.
Each is a valid pol with an explainable scheduling semantics.
After the first step of \Replace(\ensuremath{\mathit{path}}, \(B\))'s expansion, for instance, the intermediate pol holds \(\Strict(\mathit{pol}@\mathit{path}, B)\) at \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}.
That is a temporary strict-priority node favoring the outgoing \(\mathit{pol}@\mathit{path}\) over the incoming \(B\), and it has a readable scheduling interpretation in its own right.

\subsection{What the planner does not do today}\label{sec:planner-nonfeatures}

The planner has two deliberate non-features today.
\emph{No cost model.} The case analysis is shape-based, not cost-weighted.
A cost-aware planner could prefer different decompositions (e.g., several small \Add{}s vs.\ a single root-level \Replace) based on per-flow drop and delay tolerances or per-PE budgets.
Presently we take the first admissible alignment without ranking competitors.
\emph{No imperative-mode service.} The planner serves declarative mode only.
Imperative authoring (\cref{sec:imperative}) goes through its own pol-level check directly, with the operator naming each \(\delta\) themselves; the planner is not consulted.

\section{Rio Hardware Implementation}\label{sec:hardware}

A PIFO tree is a clean scheduling abstraction, but instantiating one dynamically on line-rate switching hardware needs more than a fixed set of hardwired PIFOs.
Existing designs~\cite{TODO} realize programmable scheduling through a mapping from logical scheduling units to physical ones.
Instead of hard-wiring a fixed set of PIFOs into hardware, the switch exposes programmable PEs, each hosting several \emph{virtual} PIFOs.
Each PE carries hardware, usually match-action pipelines, that assigns a packet to virtual PIFOs and computes its rank and next-hop destination, and behind that a single physical PIFO that hosts all of the PE's virtual PIFOs.

Packet scheduling on this substrate consists of pipelined, non-atomic enqueue and dequeue operations.
We call the hardware-level realizations of \texttt{push} and \texttt{pop} \emph{enqueue} and \emph{dequeue}.
On enqueue, the switch must compute two pieces of information before issuing PE pushes: which virtual PIFO id the packet should enter, and what rank should be inserted at each of those virtual PIFOs.
The first is determined by packet classification, flow-to-child mappings; the second is computed by the virtual PIFO's scheduling policy from packet fields, flow state, and per-arm metadata.
For example, a WFQ-style virtual PIFO uses the child weight to compute a virtual finish time, while a Strict virtual PIFO interprets the metadata as a priority rank.
On dequeue, the switch starts from the output port's root virtual PIFO, pops the best-ranked entry, and follows the stored next-hop information until it reaches a packet to transmit or another virtual PIFO to pop.

This is sufficient for a static policy, but it does not by itself support dynamic policy updates concurrent with packet enqueue and dequeue.
In such a case, a single packet operation can observe a mixture of old and new scheduler state.
For example, an enqueue may choose its virtual PIFO path using the old flow-to-child mapping but compute its rank using newly updated per-arm metadata, or a dequeue may pop from one version of the tree and then follow next-hop state from another.
As a result, existing PIFO-based substrates support only stop-the-world policy update.

\subsection{Transactional Update and Hardware Component Support}\label{sec:hw-transactional}

\emph{Vocabulary bridge to \S7.1' (temporary, until the two subsections are reconciled).}
The ``flow-to-child mapping'', ``next-hop mapping'', and ``match-action tables'' of this subsection are the tables that \S7.1''s \texttt{map} / \texttt{assoc} / \texttt{adopt} opcodes write to; the ``virtual PIFOs'' of this subsection are the PIFOs of \S7.1', one per PIFO-tree node.

Rio enables online policy update by packaging multiple hardware state updates into a single ``commit'' and making them visible atomically in hardware: every enqueue or dequeue request observes either the whole commit applied, or none of it.
Each \(\delta\) (\cref{sec:delta-grammar}) lowers to one \texttt{commit} under this abstraction.

We achieve this with small changes to three pieces of hardware: the virtual PIFOs, the rank computation, and the next-hop mapping.

First, Rio builds transactional tables for the flow-to-child and next-hop mappings.
Each PE stores those two mappings in a specialized table that accepts a stream of update instructions followed by a commit marker.
Updates arriving before the marker are staged but remain invisible to packet processing.
Only when the marker is applied does the table switch to the new version.
We implement this using double buffering: update instructions modify a shadow copy of the table, and the commit marker atomically swaps the active and shadow copies.
After the swap, the inactive copy is synchronized in the background to serve as the shadow for the next commit.

Second, Rio supports incremental updates to the rank-computing match-action tables.
Rio does not require arbitrary in-place replacement of a live scheduling policy.
Instead, policy-table updates add metadata for newly admitted flows, whose packets are not admitted until the update finishes, or remove metadata for flows that have already been drained.
Because these updates do not change the interpretation of existing live packets, Rio applies them incrementally rather than through a full transactional table swap.
This keeps the hardware cost low while still preserving the atomicity needed for observable scheduling behavior.

\subsection*{7.1' The commit model and the ISA}
\addcontentsline{toc}{subsection}{7.1' The commit model and the ISA}

\emph{Vocabulary bridge from \cref{sec:hw-transactional} (temporary, until the two subsections are reconciled).}
\cref{sec:hw-transactional} speaks in ``flow-to-child mapping'', ``next-hop mapping'', ``match-action tables'', and ``virtual PIFOs''.
This subsection speaks in \texttt{map} / \texttt{assoc} (which write the flow-to-child mapping), \texttt{adopt} / \texttt{emancipate} (which install and detach parent/child edges, i.e., the next-hop mapping), the per-PIFO tables that these opcodes write to (\cref{sec:hw-transactional}'s match-action tables), and PIFOs (which correspond to \cref{sec:hw-transactional}'s virtual PIFOs, one per PIFO-tree node).

The substrate exposes its services to the planner as an atomic-commit interface.
The ISA has exactly one unit: a \texttt{commit}, which is a list of instructions that the substrate installs \emph{atomically}.
The entire list lands between two consecutive \texttt{push}/\texttt{pop} operations, with no intermediate state visible to the network's \texttt{pop} stream.
This is precisely the atomicity that \cref{sec:delta-grammar} demanded of every form it admits.
A commit's \emph{length} may depend on live control state (how many flows are admitted, how many siblings need shifting), but its \emph{atomicity} does not.

Each \(\delta\) (\cref{sec:delta-grammar}) lowers to one \texttt{commit}; that is the entire ISA-level contract.
The ISA itself is unconditional: each instruction describes what to do, not what to check.
The substrate trusts that its caller has issued a well-formed commit.
Any situation-dependent reasoning, such as ``the subtree we are about to collapse is empty'', lives at \cref{sec:sequences}, in a guard \(\varphi\) that had to become true before the corresponding \(\delta\) fired.

Four pieces of substrate vocabulary appear in the listing below:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{PIFO}: one node of the PIFO-tree, addressed by an opaque id \texttt{v}.
The substrate exposes one PIFO per tree node.
How those PIFOs are mapped onto hardware is a substrate concern, settled by the deployment function \texttt{pe} below.
\item \textbf{PE} (processing element): hosts one or more PIFOs and owns their per-node state and logic.
A single PE can host multiple PIFOs at once; in particular, sibling PIFOs commonly cohabit a PE.
\item \textbf{flow}: a traffic class, as in \cref{sec:policy-dsl}.
\item \textbf{index}: opaque per-PIFO handle naming one of that PIFO's children; what a PIFO uses internally to refer to a child arm.
\end{itemize}

Our single-purpose opcodes also mean that the tree can become transiently malformed \emph{within} a commit, in ways that the commit's atomicity hides from any observer.
For an example from \cref{sec:hw-compile}, \Designate's commit first adopts \texttt{loser} as a child of the freshly-spawned \texttt{sp\_v}, then emancipates \texttt{loser} from its original parent and adopts \texttt{sp\_v} in its place.
Between the first adopt and the emancipate, \texttt{loser} has two parents.
The atomic install means no \texttt{push} or \texttt{pop} ever sees this state, but the planner is free to issue commits whose intermediate states would not satisfy the well-formedness conditions of \cref{sec:controls}.

We treat the assignment of a PIFO to a PE as a deterministic function \texttt{pe(path)} of the PIFO's tree position, fixed at compile time.
A typical shape is depth-by-PE: PIFOs at the same tree depth share a PE~\cite{Sivaraman16}, so sibling PIFOs cohabit one PE by default.
The planner consults \texttt{pe(path)} when it needs a \texttt{spawn}'s \texttt{pe} argument; the function's definition is a property of the target substrate and is not otherwise observable at the ISA.

\subsection{Compiling Policy Edits}\label{sec:hw-compile}

Each form of \(\delta\) (\cref{sec:delta-grammar}) lowers to a list of opcodes.
The lowering \emph{schema} is mechanical.
Each form names a fixed sequence of opcodes, and the planner reads live state at issue time to fill in the schema's positional parameters, such as flow lists, chain shapes, and routing indices, before dispatching the commit to the substrate.

Before working through the forms, we set up one structural convention.
A switch with \(P\) output ports has \(P\) separate trees, and at the ISA we put a uniform thin wrapper around each.
Every port hosts a reserved PIFO \texttt{port\_root}, allocated at boot on a reserved PE, whose sole child is the actual tree root.
\texttt{port\_root} runs a fixed 1-arm policy.
Its \texttt{Assoc} set mirrors that of the live tree's actual root; its \texttt{Map} sends each Assoc'd flow through its single index \texttt{port\_step}.
The \texttt{walk(...)} calls that touch \texttt{Assoc}/\texttt{Map} start at \texttt{port\_root}, so its tables stay in lockstep with the actual root's.

The three parts of \ensuremath{\mathit{z}} (\cref{sec:controls}) line up one to one with this vocabulary.
A node's \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{admit}\) is its \texttt{Assoc} set, its \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{serves}\) is its \texttt{Map} table, its \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{rank}\) is what \texttt{set\_policy} installs and \texttt{discipline(v)} reads back, and its \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{meta}\) is what \texttt{set\_arm\_meta} writes.
Each of \ensuremath{\mathit{z}}'s parts is thus an opcode's target, and none of them is state that \texttt{push} or \texttt{pop} advances.

The lowerings follow, one per form of \cref{sec:delta-grammar}.
We fix a handful of reading conventions.
We identify a tree position with the PIFO that lives there.
Live queries against a node \texttt{v} (its \texttt{arity(v)}, \texttt{discipline(v)}, etc.) are read off the tree at issue time.
When an opcode names an unmentioned parameter at \texttt{v} (e.g., \texttt{set\_policy}'s discipline, \texttt{change\_arity}'s arity), it is the live value at \texttt{v}.
\texttt{spawn}'s \texttt{pe} argument is similarly elided: it is \texttt{pe(pos(v))}, drawn from \S7.1''s deployment convention.

Alongside the opcodes, the listings use a small codegen vocabulary:
\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{foreach x in S: BODY} emits \texttt{BODY} once per \texttt{x} drawn from \texttt{S}.
\item \texttt{walk(op, C, f)} emits \texttt{op(v, f, \ldots)} at each \(v \in C\); positional remainders such as \texttt{map}'s routing index \(i_{v,f}\) are read off the live tree.
\item \texttt{flows(path)} is the set of flows admitted by some leaf under \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}.
\item \texttt{chain(f)} is the sequence of PIFOs from \texttt{port\_root} to the leaf admitting \texttt{f}, and \texttt{internals(f)} is that sequence minus the leaf.
\item \texttt{nodes(T)} and \texttt{edges(T)} are the PIFOs and parent-child edges of a compiled subtree \texttt{T}; \texttt{root(T)} is \texttt{T}'s root PIFO.
\item \texttt{layout(T)} expands to \texttt{spawn}+\texttt{set\_policy} at every \(v \in \mathtt{nodes(T)}\), \texttt{adopt} at every internal edge in \(\mathtt{edges(T)}\), and \texttt{set\_arm\_meta} at each per-arm slot where \texttt{v}'s discipline requires it.
The parent-side \texttt{adopt} that attaches \(\mathtt{root(T)}\) is left to the caller.
\end{itemize}

Where a form destructures \(\delta\)'s path as \(\pi \mathbin{+\!\!+} [k]\), we write \texttt{p} for the parent's path and \texttt{k} for the child's local index.
For \(\mathit{path} = [\,]\), the parent is \texttt{port\_root} and \texttt{k} is \texttt{port\_step}.

\paragraph*{\(\ChangeMeta(\pi \mathbin{+\!\!+} [k], m)\)}
\begin{Verbatim}
set_arm_meta(p, k, m)
\end{Verbatim}

\paragraph*{\(\Quiesce(\mathit{path})\)}
\begin{Verbatim}
foreach f in flows(path):
    walk(deassoc, chain(f), f)
\end{Verbatim}

\paragraph*{\(\Add(\mathit{path}, D, \mathit{arm}, m)\)}
\begin{Verbatim}
n := arity(path)
layout(arm)
change_arity(path, n+1)
set_arm_meta(path, n, m)
adopt(n, path, root(arm))
foreach f in flows(arm):
    walk(assoc, chain(f), f)
    walk(map,   internals(f), f)
\end{Verbatim}
The new subtree occupies \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}'s rightmost slot (index \texttt{n}), so existing children's indices are undisturbed.

\paragraph*{\(\Remove(\pi \mathbin{+\!\!+} [k])\)}
\begin{Verbatim}
change_arity(p, arity(p) - 1)
emancipate(k, p)
foreach f in flows(p ++ [k]):
    walk(unmap, internals(f), f)
foreach v in nodes(p ++ [k]):
    gc(v)
\end{Verbatim}
\texttt{p}'s remaining children keep their indices: each was minted at adoption time, so no sibling-shift cascade is needed.

\paragraph*{\(\Designate(\mathit{path}, \mathit{survivor})\)}
\begin{Verbatim}
loser  := PIFO at path;   parent := PIFO at path's parent
k      := path's last index
surv   := root(survivor)
layout(survivor)
spawn(sp_v, pe(path))
set_policy(sp_v, Strict*, 2)
adopt(0, sp_v, loser); adopt(1, sp_v, surv)
set_arm_meta(sp_v, 0, 1.0)
set_arm_meta(sp_v, 1, 2.0)
emancipate(k, parent); adopt(k, parent, sp_v)
designate(loser, surv)
foreach f in flows(loser) \ flows(surv):
    assoc(sp_v, f); map(sp_v, f, 0)
foreach f in flows(surv):
    assoc(sp_v, f); map(sp_v, f, 1)
foreach f in flows(surv) \ flows(path):
    walk(assoc, chain(f), f)
    walk(map,   internals(f), f)
\end{Verbatim}
Reusing the same \texttt{k} for the rewire preserves \texttt{parent}'s per-arm metadata for that slot.
Flows in \(\mathtt{flows(surv)} \cap \mathtt{flows(path)}\) need no above-\texttt{sp\_v} wiring: the chain is already in place from \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}'s prior wiring.
Flows in \(\mathtt{flows(loser)} \setminus \mathtt{flows(surv)}\) keep their existing above-\texttt{sp\_v} Assoc/Map state and are routed to slot 0 by \texttt{sp\_v}.\ensuremath{\mathit{z}}, matching \cref{sec:designate}'s operational rule that loser-only labels drain on arm 0.
Operator-level silencing of those flows, when the operator ultimately wants it, comes from the \Quiesce{} step of the surrounding \Replace{} idiom (\cref{sec:sequences}), not from \Designate.

\emph{Same-PE invariant.}
The planner places \texttt{sp\_v}, \texttt{loser}'s root, and \texttt{surv}'s root on the same PE (a deterministic property of the lowering: all three sit at depth \(|\mathit{path}|\), and \texttt{pe(path)} returns one PE).
The substrate may exploit this to coalesce the three into a single physical slot, so that it need not move all of \texttt{loser} down one PE level.

\paragraph*{\(\Undesignate(\mathit{path})\)}
\begin{Verbatim}
sp_v    := super-node head at path
loser_v := its retiring arm
undesignate(loser_v)
gc(sp_v)
foreach v in nodes(loser_v):
    gc(v)
\end{Verbatim}
\texttt{undesignate} rewires the parent's slot past \texttt{sp\_v} but does not deallocate it, so an explicit \texttt{gc(sp\_v)} follows.

Two of these lowerings need further comment.

\Quiesce{}'s wide reach, covering the root-to-\ensuremath{\mathit{path}} chain along with the subtree under \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}, is forced by \cref{sec:policy-dsl}'s parallel push.
Every node on a packet's path mints its own routing index independently, so dropping \texttt{f} at only some of those nodes would leave the rest minting stray indices, and the tree malformed.
The chain walk is what \cref{sec:quiesce} calls narrowing the admitted set uniformly.
\Quiesce{} does not issue \texttt{unmap}: silenced flows' Map entries \emph{must outlive the silencing} so that buffered packets can be popped.
\Remove{} pairs \texttt{emancipate} with \texttt{unmap} precisely because it fires after the drain, when there is no longer any in-flight traffic to preserve.
One subtlety attends the loser arm of a \(\Strictstar\) super-node, that is, a \Quiesce{} that fires in the middle of a \Replace{}.
There the lowering restricts the deassoc walk to loser-only flows, since \Designate{} routed the shared flows to the survivor's slot at commit time (\cref{sec:designate}), and de-associating them here would silence live traffic on the survivor.

The lowering for \Designate{} matches \cref{sec:grammar}'s tree-level reading exactly: a fresh Strict-2 PIFO \texttt{sp\_v} is spawned, \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}'s previous occupant becomes its favored child (rank 1.0), and the survivor becomes its second child (rank 2.0).
The lowering rewires \ensuremath{\mathit{path}}'s parent edge from \texttt{loser} to \texttt{sp\_v}, reusing the same parent-side index \texttt{k}; the parent's per-arm metadata for the slot is therefore preserved across \Designate{} and across the eventual \Undesignate{} that retires the loser.
\texttt{designate(loser, surv)} is the ISA-level marker that names the favored child and records the eventual successor; a later \texttt{undesignate} swings the parent's index over to \texttt{surv} and releases \texttt{sp\_v} along with the loser subtree.
A substrate that recognizes the same-PE invariant on \texttt{(sp\_v, loser, surv)} may coalesce the three into a single physical slot, the ``super-node'' optimization named above.
The ISA does not require it, leaving room for substrates that prefer the literal Strict-2 form.

\subsection{Substrate portability}\label{sec:substrate-portability}

These mechanisms are independent of the underlying PIFO implementation.
Our prototype uses a shift-register-based PIFO similar to the original design~\cite{Sivaraman16}, but Rio assumes only the push-in, first-out interface of \cref{sec:hierarchical}.
Other PIFO implementations can be substituted.

\subsection{Executing \(\delta\) as a transaction}\label{sec:hw-transaction}

Rio implements a transactional update controller that executes each lowered \(\delta\) as an atomic hardware transaction.
In hardware, the controller executes commands in order.
Ordinary update commands are routed to the target PEs and staged locally.
When the controller reaches the commit instruction, it generates the hardware commit signals that make the staged updates visible.
These signals are delay-aligned across PEs according to the PIFO tree topology, so that no enqueue or dequeue operation can traverse a partially updated tree.
Intuitively, the controller treats the commit boundary as a wavefront: the new configuration becomes visible at different PEs at carefully chosen times, ensuring that any individual packet observes a consistent configuration along its entire path.

The controller also implements guarded transitions.
As described in \cref{sec:sequences}, some transitions are guarded by predicates such as the emptiness of a subtree.
Rio lowers such guards to blocking commands in the controller queue.
A blocking command remains at the head of the queue until its predicate becomes true; only then can the subsequent update instructions execute.
In-order execution therefore ensures that instructions depending on the guard are not issued prematurely.

Our current implementation uses a single controller queue, which provides a simple global serialization point for updates.
This design is sufficient for correctness and avoids subtle ordering bugs between overlapping commits.
It can, however, introduce head-of-line blocking when a guarded transition waits for a long-running drain.
Supporting multiple independent queues for disjoint subtrees is a natural optimization and does not change Rio's ISA or correctness argument; we leave this extension to future work.

\section{Per-Discipline Slot Initialization}\label{app:init-slot}

\cref{sec:compilation} fixes the design choice that a freshly spliced arm should ``join the current round'', entering with neither the penalty nor the advantage of having been absent.
Here are the \(\mathit{init\_slot}_{D}\) recipes that realize it for our three representative disciplines.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{\texttt{RoundRobin}.} \texttt{init\_slot\_RoundRobin} returns the empty tuple, since \RR{} has no per-arm bookkeeping.
The round-robin cursor lives in \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}} instead, and at \Add{} we splice the new arm into the child list at the cursor's current position, so its first turn lands in the round already in progress.
The cursor itself is left pointing at the same physical arm it was pointing at before the splice.
\item \textbf{\texttt{Strict}.} An arm's priority is its metadatum and lives in \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{meta}\) (\cref{sec:controls}).
\Strict{} therefore has no per-arm bookkeeping of its own, and \texttt{init\_slot\_Strict} returns the empty tuple.
\(M_{\Strict}\) is a dense total order such as the rationals, so that a fresh priority can always be slotted strictly between two existing ones.
\item \textbf{\texttt{WFQ}.} The \ensuremath{\mathit{node\_state}} at a parent is the virtual time \texttt{vt}, and \texttt{init\_slot\_WFQ(vt, w) = vt}.
A new arm therefore inherits the parent's current \texttt{vt} as its last-finish tag, while its weight \texttt{w} sits in \(\mathit{z}.\mathit{meta}\).
Any standard WFQ tagging that derives the next packet's tag from the slot's last-finish tag and the parent's \texttt{vt}, such as the GPS-style \texttt{max(virtual\_clock, vt) + 1/w}, then places the first packet on this arm in the round that the established arms are currently in.
At compile time the parent's \texttt{vt} is freshly initialized (to zero), so every original arm starts tagged \texttt{0} and the round is ``the zeroth''; at \Add{} the parent's \texttt{vt} is whatever the clock has advanced to.
\end{itemize}
